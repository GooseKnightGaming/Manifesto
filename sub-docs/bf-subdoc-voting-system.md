# WHY THE CURRENT SYSTEM FAILS

## First Past the Post — The Problems

Britain currently uses First Past the Post (FPTP). The candidate with the most votes in a constituency wins, regardless of whether they have majority support.

**What this produces:**

**Wasted votes:** In safe seats — which make up the majority of constituencies — your vote has no practical effect on the outcome. A Conservative voter in a safe Labour seat, or a Labour voter in a safe Conservative seat, may as well not vote for the purposes of Parliamentary outcome. Britain has millions of these voters.

**Tactical voting:** Millions of British voters do not vote for who they actually want. They vote to stop someone else winning. This is not democracy. It is managed disappointment. "Hold your nose and vote Labour to keep the Tories out" is not a functioning democratic choice.

**Distorted representation:** A party can win a large number of seats with a minority of the national vote. In 2015, UKIP received 12.6% of the vote and won 1 seat. The SNP received 4.7% and won 56 seats. This is not proportional. It is geographic accident.

**Marginal seat dominance:** Government policy is shaped by what wins in approximately 80-100 marginal constituencies, regardless of what the rest of Britain thinks or needs. This is why certain regional infrastructure investments never happen and certain regional issues are never addressed — the votes don't matter enough.

**Safe seat complacency:** MPs in safe seats are effectively accountable only to their local party membership — not to their constituents. A safe-seat MP who ignores their constituency faces no meaningful electoral consequence.

**The result:** A system where millions of votes are meaningless, governments can be elected on minority support, and policy is made for 80 marginal constituencies rather than 650.

## Turnout — The Civic Crisis

In recent general elections, approximately 33-40% of eligible voters do not vote at all. Governments are formed on the support of a minority of the total electorate.

This is not apathy. It is rational response to a system where many people's votes do not change outcomes.

The solution is not to lecture non-voters about civic duty. It is to build a system where participation is meaningful and required.

# THE BRITAIN FORWARD SYSTEM

## Part One: Ranked Weighted Voting

### How It Works

Voters rank candidates in order of preference: 1st choice, 2nd choice, 3rd choice, and so on. You can rank as many or as few as you wish.

Points are awarded by ranking:
- 1st preference: [n] points
- 2nd preference: [n-1] points
- And so on down the ballot

The candidate with the highest total points across all voters' rankings wins.

### Why This Is Better Than FPTP

**No more wasted votes:** Your preferences are counted even if your first choice doesn't win. Voting for a smaller party first no longer "wastes" your vote — your lower preferences still count.

**No more tactical voting:** Vote for who you actually want first. Your lower preferences are your tactical vote — built into the system without requiring you to compromise your first choice.

**Broad appeal required:** To win under this system, a candidate needs to be positively regarded by a wide range of voters — not just intensely loved by a narrow minority. Extremist candidates who have passionate small followings but are disliked by most voters cannot win.

**Every constituency matters:** Because preferences from all voters count, there are no "safe seats" in the same way — a candidate who wins with broad second and third preference support from opposition voters is genuinely popular, not just geographically lucky.

### Why This Is Better Than Pure Proportional Representation

Pure PR (party lists, etc.) severs the constituency link — voters elect parties, not local representatives. Britain Forward believes in the constituency link. Your MP represents your area. That accountability is valuable and is maintained under Ranked Weighted Voting.

### Why This Is Better Than Simple Ranked Choice (AV)

The 2011 AV referendum failed. AV (Alternative Vote) — where the candidate with fewest votes is eliminated and votes redistributed — is simpler but does not adequately reward broad appeal.

Ranked Weighted Voting considers all preferences simultaneously rather than sequentially eliminating candidates. It is more mathematically robust at measuring genuine breadth of support.

*(Note: The precise mathematical weighting formula will be determined by an independent Electoral Commission review before legislation is finalised.)*

## Part Two: Compulsory Participation

### The Principle

Every eligible citizen participates in democratic elections. Participation does not mean choosing a candidate — it means showing up and registering your view, even if that view is "none of the above."

### The Australian Precedent

Australia has had compulsory voting since 1924. Turnout regularly exceeds 90%. Governments are genuinely elected by the population. No government has won on minority support.

Crucially: Australia has not become a totalitarian state. Democratic norms have been maintained. The sky has not fallen.

### What Happens If You Don't Vote

A fine — modest, proportionate, and consistent with Australia's model. Not a criminal penalty. A civil fine equivalent to approximately one hour's minimum wage.

Exemptions:
- Genuine illness or medical emergency
- Being overseas without reasonable ability to reach a voting facility (postal/overseas voting available)
- Religious objection — subject to formal application (as in Australia)
- Administrative exemptions for people who genuinely could not participate

The fine is not punitive. It is a signal that participation has value.

### The Human Rights Act Question

A legal challenge under Article 9 (freedom of thought) or Article 10 (freedom of expression) of the ECHR is possible.

**Our legal position:** Compulsory participation does not compel political expression. The NOTA option (see below) means no particular political view is required. Physical participation in a process that permits rejection of all candidates is arguably compatible with human rights law — the right not to express a political view is preserved; the obligation is only to show up.

**Our commitment:** An HRA compatibility assessment will be published before legislation is introduced. If compulsory participation is found incompatible with the HRA, we will introduce a maximum-incentive voluntary participation model — strong positive incentives for voting (civic reward schemes, small financial benefit for voting) rather than penalties for not voting. The goal is full participation. The mechanism adapts to the legal framework.

## Part Three: None of the Above (NOTA)

### What It Is

A formal ballot option: "None of the Above." A vote for NOTA is a formal, counted, published democratic signal — not a spoiled ballot, not an abstention, but an active registered rejection of all available candidates.

### What NOTA Votes Mean

- NOTA votes are counted and published alongside candidate votes
- High NOTA tallies in any constituency are formally reported to Parliament and to the Electoral Commission
- A sustained high NOTA percentage triggers mandatory constituency engagement — the sitting MP is required to hold public accountability sessions
- If NOTA wins outright (more votes than any candidate): a fresh election is triggered within 60 days

### What Happens When NOTA Wins

- Fresh election within 60 days
- Original candidates are ineligible to stand — they have been rejected
- New candidates must be selected by all parties
- The constituency is temporarily served by a caretaker from the National Oversight Council
- The fresh election is also subject to compulsory participation

**On gaming:** Can a well-organised minority force repeated NOTA victories? Under compulsory participation, NOTA winning requires genuine majority sentiment — not just a motivated minority. Repeated by-elections have logistical costs that deter organised spoiling. And original candidates are barred — persistent NOTA victories force genuinely new options to emerge.

# ELECTORAL INTEGRITY

## Campaign Finance Reform

- Spending limits: tightened and enforced — current limits are loosely monitored
- Foreign donations: prohibited absolutely — no foreign money in British elections
- Dark money: eliminated — all donations above £500 fully disclosed within 30 days
- Digital advertising: political ads online subject to the same spending limits as traditional media
- Electoral Commission: independent, adequately resourced, with real enforcement powers including criminal referral

## Candidate Eligibility

- Candidates must be British citizens
- No criminal conviction for serious offences (fraud, corruption, violence) within ten years
- Financial declarations before and after each Parliament — unexplained wealth triggers investigation

## Voter Registration

- Automatic voter registration at 18 — no opt-in required; opt-out available
- Same-day registration available at polling stations (with appropriate ID verification)
- Postal and proxy voting: maintained and improved
- Overseas British citizens: maintained voting rights for up to 15 years of non-residence (current)

## Electoral Boundaries

- Independent Electoral Commission manages boundary reviews without Parliamentary interference
- Reviews on a fixed schedule — not triggered by parties for political advantage
- Boundary changes cannot take effect within 12 months of a general election

# THE COALITION QUESTION

With Ranked Weighted Voting, coalition governments become more normal — single-party majorities under broad-appeal voting are achievable but less guaranteed.

Britain Forward's position on coalitions:

**Pre-election:** Red lines stated publicly. Voters know what we will and won't compromise.

**Non-negotiable:**
- Ranked weighted voting remains
- Ministerial competence standards remain
- Public ownership of core infrastructure is not reversed

**Negotiable:** Everything else. Policy positions, implementation timelines, priorities within the platform, Cabinet composition.

**Coalition formation:** Britain Forward will always seek to form the most stable coalition that can govern effectively and implement meaningful reform — not the coalition that gives us the most Cabinet posts.

**Confidence and supply:** If unable to form a coalition, confidence and supply agreements are acceptable where our non-negotiables are respected.

# THE LORDS REFORM QUESTION

The House of Lords currently provides the second chamber scrutiny function. Lords reform (see main platform) is separate from voting system reform — but they interact.

Until Lords reform is resolved by public referendum, the voting system overhaul applies to the House of Commons only.

The Lords reform referendum will present three options with detailed explanation of each. The voting system for that referendum will itself use ranked voting — voters will rank the three options. The option with the highest weighted score across all preferences wins.
