# PART ONE: UNIVERSAL BASIC INCOME

## What UBI Is — Simply Stated

Universal Basic Income is a regular, unconditional payment made to every qualifying person, regardless of whether they work, regardless of what they earn, regardless of their circumstances.

It is a floor. Not a ceiling. Not a replacement for ambition. A floor.

Everyone stands on it. Nobody falls through it.

## What UBI Is Not

- Not a replacement for all support — disability support, childcare, and housing support in high-cost areas remain separately
- Not a disincentive to work — the evidence consistently shows otherwise
- Not unaffordable — the gross figure is not the net cost
- Not new — Alaska has paid every resident a version of UBI since 1982 from oil revenues; Finland, Kenya, Canada, and others have run successful pilots

## Why Britain Needs It Now

The current benefits system:
- Costs billions to administer through means-testing, case management, sanctions, and appeals
- Creates poverty traps — people lose benefits faster than they gain wages when they start work
- Treats people with suspicion and bureaucratic hostility rather than as citizens
- Leaves millions in genuine hardship despite enormous administrative expenditure
- Is incomprehensible — dozens of different benefits with different rules, different qualifying criteria, different assessment processes

UBI replaces most of this with one simple, universal payment. The savings in administration are real and substantial.

## THE FULL UBI FRAMEWORK

### The Rate

**Target rate:** Approximately £100 per week (£5,200 per year)

This is the aspirational rate based on current analysis. The actual rollout rate will be:
1. Confirmed by evidence from properly evaluated pilots
2. Validated by independent economic modelling before national rollout
3. Set at a level the public finances can sustain from the start
4. Increased over time as the Sovereign Wealth Fund matures and national productivity grows

The £100/week figure is a planning target, not a day-one commitment. Honesty about this is more important than a headline number.

### Who Qualifies — The Contribution Threshold Model

UBI is not charity. It is a return on membership of and contribution to British society.

| Status | UBI Entitlement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| British citizens | Full, from adulthood | The baseline |
| Settled residents, 5+ years | Full | Contribution threshold met |
| High-skilled visa holders | Full after 2 years | Shorter threshold: economic contribution |
| Standard visa holders | Full after 5 years | Standard threshold |
| Refugees/asylum seekers | Needs-based support | Separate framework during processing |
| Pensioners | Full UBI + pension | Pension is separate entitlement |
| Working people | Full UBI + wages | UBI supplements, not replaces |
| Job-seeking unemployed | Full UBI + support | Active employment support alongside |
| Non-seeking, non-disabled unemployed | Full UBI only | Their choice. £100/week is their life. |
| Disabled people | Full UBI + disability support | Disability support is separate, needs-based |
| Students | Full UBI | Replaces most maintenance support |
| Under criminal sentence | Suspended | Resumes on day of release |
| Foreign nationals without qualifying residency | Not eligible | — |

### The Gross vs Net Cost Question — Answered Fully

**Gross cost calculation (what opponents will quote):**
~50 million qualifying adults × £100/week × 52 weeks = ~£260 billion per year

**Why the gross figure is misleading:**

**1. Tax recoupture:**
UBI paid to higher earners is partially or fully taxed back through income tax.
- A person earning £80,000 per year receives £5,200 UBI but pays it back through higher marginal income tax rates
- Effective net UBI receipt for higher earners: near zero
- Estimated recoupture from higher earners: significant — modelling required for precise figure

**2. Benefits system replacement:**
Current means-tested benefits expenditure (excluding state pension and NHS):
approximately £100+ billion per year.
UBI replaces the majority of this. The administrative cost of means-testing — assessments, case managers, tribunals, sanctions, appeals — is also eliminated.

**3. Economic stimulus effects:**
UBI recipients spend locally. Poverty-related NHS costs (A&E presentations, mental health crises, malnutrition) reduce. Justice costs (crime driven by destitution) reduce. These are not primary funding mechanisms but they are real fiscal offsets.

**4. Net cost:**
The net additional cost of UBI — after tax recoupture and benefits system replacement savings — requires independent economic modelling. That modelling will be commissioned and published before any national rollout decision.

Our honest position: we cannot give you a precise net figure without Treasury-level data and independent economic modelling. We commit to that work and to publishing the results, whatever they show.

### Addressing the Objections

**"It will make people lazy."**

Evidence says: No.

Finland's 2017-2018 pilot: UBI recipients were more likely to be employed after the pilot than the control group. They were significantly less stressed, healthier, and more confident.

Kenya's GiveDirectly programme: recipients invested in businesses, education, and housing — not in idleness.

Alaska Permanent Fund (since 1982): No measurable increase in unemployment. Poverty rates among the lowest in the US.

Canada's 1970s Mincome pilot: School attendance increased. Hospital visits decreased. People started businesses.

The theoretical argument for laziness misunderstands human motivation. Most people want to contribute, create, and connect with others through work. What they don't want is to be trapped in work that exploits or destroys them because they have no alternative. UBI gives people the ability to say no to bad work — which drives bad employers to improve conditions, not drives workers into idleness.

Some people will choose to live on £100 a week and not work. They will have a safe, austere life with few luxuries. That is their choice to make. The cost to the state of supporting that choice is less than the current cost of the sanctions, assessments, tribunals, and crisis services that same person currently generates while living in poverty and stress.

**"It's too expensive."**

Net cost after recoupture and system replacement is substantially lower than the gross figure. Independent modelling will confirm the real number before rollout.

**"It will cause inflation."**

Inflation from UBI occurs if the money supply increases without corresponding productive capacity. UBI funded from: (a) reduced benefits administration, (b) tax recoupture from higher earners, (c) asset revenues, and (d) broader tax revenues, does not automatically increase the money supply. The concern is legitimate for poorly designed UBI. It is manageable for well-designed UBI. The pilots will measure this.

**"Non-workers don't deserve it."**

UBI is not a reward for work. It is a foundation for a functioning society. The person who raises children, cares for elderly parents, volunteers in their community, or is between jobs is not a drain on society. The current system treats them as one. UBI treats them as a person.

**"£100 a week isn't enough to live on."**

Correct. It isn't intended to be. It is a floor, not a living wage. Working people receive UBI plus wages. Retired people receive UBI plus pension. Disabled people receive UBI plus targeted disability support. Only non-working, non-seeking, non-disabled adults live on UBI alone — and they live simply and austerely by choice.

### The Criminal Sentence Question — Fully Addressed

UBI is **suspended** during any period under criminal sentence. It is not permanently withdrawn.

On the day of release, UBI resumes. Immediately. No application, no assessment, no waiting period.

The sentence period creates a gap in UBI that cannot be recovered — those weeks and months are gone. That is the financial penalty of the sentence.

But: a released prisoner who is immediately financially destitute is more likely to reoffend. This is well-documented. A released prisoner who immediately has £100/week coming in — not much, but something — has more options than one who has nothing. Reoffending costs the state far more than £100/week.

Released prisoners additionally receive an active resettlement package: housing support, employment guidance, mental health referral where needed. UBI is the floor. Resettlement support helps them build above it.

### Implementation

**This Parliament:**
- Pilot design finalised — locations, size, duration, evaluation framework
- Pilots launched in diverse locations: urban, rural, economically deprived, economically mixed
- Pilots run for minimum three years
- Independent evaluation published — full data, not selected highlights

**Next Parliament (decision based on evidence):**
- If pilots support national rollout: legislation introduced, phased rollout begins
- If pilots identify problems: modified design, extended pilots, or alternative approach
- The decision follows the evidence. Not the timetable.

# PART TWO: THE 4-DAY WORKING WEEK

## The Problem with Five Days

The five-day, 40-hour working week was designed for an industrial economy in the early 20th century. It was not designed for:
- Knowledge work (where output quality is not proportional to hours)
- Service industries (where scheduling flexibility benefits both workers and customers)
- Parenting (which currently conflicts maximally with a five-day working week for both parents)
- Health (chronic overwork has well-documented negative health outcomes)
- Productivity (tired, burned-out workers produce less than rested, motivated ones)

Britain is one of the least productive economies in the developed world per hour worked, despite working long hours. The problem is not effort. It is system design.

## The Evidence

**Iceland (2015-2019):** Four-day week pilots covering over 1% of the working population. Productivity maintained or improved in the vast majority of workplaces. Worker wellbeing improved significantly. The model has since been adopted by the majority of Iceland's workforce.

**Microsoft Japan (2019):** Four-day week pilot. Productivity increased 40%. Energy consumption fell. Paper use fell.

**UK 4-Day Week Pilot (2022):** 61 companies, 2,900 workers, six months. 92% of companies continued the four-day week after the pilot. Revenue was broadly unchanged. Sick days fell 65%. Staff turnover fell significantly.

**The evidence is consistent:** In knowledge and service work, working fewer hours does not reduce output. In many cases, it increases it.

## What Britain Forward Proposes

### Not a Mandate. A Transition.

Britain Forward does not mandate a four-day week immediately for all employers. This would be:
- Practically impossible for many sectors (healthcare, emergency services, hospitality, logistics)
- Economically disruptive if implemented without preparation
- Counterproductive — mandating what needs to be adopted thoughtfully

Instead: a managed transition, sector by sector, evidence-led.

### Phase One — Public Sector Pilots

The government implements four-day working weeks in:
- Civil service departments where it is operationally feasible
- NHS administrative functions (not clinical — see below)
- Local authority administrative functions
- Government agencies

Evaluation is rigorous and published. This Parliament, year 1-2.

### Phase Two — Evidence Publication and Employer Support

- Full evaluation published: productivity, costs, benefits, challenges
- Employer guidance published: how to implement, how to manage transitions
- Tax incentives for employers who adopt — productivity gains partly shared as tax relief
- This Parliament, year 2-4.

### Phase Three — Sector-by-Sector Dialogue

Formal engagement with:
- Trade unions (by sector)
- Employer bodies (by sector)
- Agreeing implementation timelines that work for each sector
- Beyond this Parliament.

### Sectors Requiring Special Consideration

**Healthcare and emergency services:** Cannot simply reduce hours without reducing cover. Four-day week here means different shift patterns — the same number of hours of cover delivered by workers doing fewer hours each, which requires more staffing. This is a longer-term transition dependent on NHS workforce expansion.

**Hospitality and retail:** Often already operate on flexible shift patterns. The four-day week here is more about consecutive days off than total hours. Manageable with reasonable transition time.

**Logistics and transport:** Dependent on legal driving and rest time regulations — which already constrain hours. The four-day week in logistics may manifest as better rest patterns rather than simply fewer days.

**Self-employment and freelancing:** Not mandatable. The evidence from pilot programmes suggests self-employed people often naturally move toward this model when their clients adopt it.

### What Doesn't Change

- Total earnings: the four-day week is not a pay cut. 80% of hours with 100% of pay is the standard model in pilots.
- Annual leave entitlement: separate from the four-day week structure
- Zero-hours contracts: remain subject to separate reform — guaranteed minimum hours regardless of working week structure
- Overtime: still compensated appropriately

### The Equal Responsibility Note

Not every job can do this. Some jobs require continuous cover — doctors, police, firefighters, infrastructure workers. Britain Forward's four-day week policy is for office-based, knowledge-based, and many service-based roles. Essential services are explicitly handled differently, with equivalent rest and wellbeing benefits delivered through different mechanisms.

# HOW UBI AND THE 4-DAY WEEK WORK TOGETHER

These are not separate policies. They are a coherent restructuring of Britain's relationship with work.

**UBI provides the floor** — nobody is forced to take any available job just to survive. This drives:
- Better job matching (people take jobs they're suited for)
- Better working conditions (employers must offer something worth choosing)
- More people starting businesses (the risk of failure is survivable)
- More people retraining (the cost of time out of work is lower)

**The 4-day week raises the ceiling** — work becomes a more sustainable, healthier part of life rather than the whole of it. This drives:
- Lower burnout and better long-term productivity
- Better parenting and family stability
- Lower NHS costs from overwork-related illness
- More time for civic engagement, community, and creativity

Together: Britain works less desperately and more effectively. People spend their working hours on work they've chosen, in conditions that don't destroy them, without fear of destitution if circumstances change.

That is not a utopia. It is a realistic description of what evidence-based work policy can achieve.
