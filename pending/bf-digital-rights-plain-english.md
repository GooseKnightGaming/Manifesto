# BRITAIN FORWARD
## DIGITAL RIGHTS — PLAIN ENGLISH GUIDE
### What It Means For You

---

> *Your time, your data, and your choices belong to you.*
> *Not to apps. Not to advertisers. Not to algorithms.*

---

# COOKIES — THE POPUPS THAT WON'T GO AWAY

You know those popups that appear when you visit a website, asking about cookies?

Most of them are designed to trick you into clicking "Accept All." This is deliberate. Under Britain Forward, it becomes illegal.

**Here's what must change:**

✅ **If a website wants to track you — it has to ask first.**
Tracking cookies must be switched *off* by default. You switch them *on* if you want them. Not the other way around.

✅ **The "No" button must be as easy to find as the "Yes" button.**
Right now, "Accept All" is usually a big colourful button. "No thanks" is often tiny, grey, or hidden behind several more clicks. That's illegal under our rules. Both buttons must be the same size and equally easy to find.

✅ **Tickboxes must start empty.**
If a website shows you a list of tracking options with tickboxes, every box must start unticked. You tick the ones you want. Nothing is pre-ticked for you.

✅ **Sliders must start switched off.**
If a website uses on/off sliders for tracking options, every slider must start on "off." You switch on the ones you choose.

✅ **Saying "no" must be as quick as saying "yes."**
If accepting all cookies takes one click, refusing them must take one click too. Making "no" take five steps when "yes" takes one is banned.

✅ **Websites must tell you what cookies actually do — in plain English.**
"Third party partners" tells you nothing. Websites must say, in plain English, exactly what each type of cookie does and who gets your data.

✅ **You must be able to change your mind.**
There must always be a "manage cookies" option available — not just when you first visit. You can change what you've agreed to, at any time.

# ADS IN FREE APPS AND GAMES

You're playing a free game. An advert comes on. The countdown runs down. And then... the X button is tiny, fake, or doesn't appear at all.

**Under Britain Forward:**

✅ **When the countdown ends, the X must appear — immediately.**
No delays. No disappearing. The X button appears when the countdown ends and stays there.

✅ **The X must actually close the advert.**
An X button that takes you to a shop or downloads an app instead of closing the advert is not an X button — it's a trick. Banned.

✅ **The X must be big enough to actually tap.**
The minimum size is 44x44 pixels — that's the size recommended by Apple and Google in their own design guidelines. An X button smaller than this in an advert is illegal.

✅ **The X must not be right next to the "buy" button.**
If tapping the X and tapping the advert link are basically the same tap, that's engineered to make you accidentally click the advert. The X must have clear space around it.

✅ **If you tap X, you go back to your game — within one second.**
No "are you sure?" No second countdown. You said you wanted to close it. You close it.

# SNEAKY TRICKS THAT WILL BE ILLEGAL

These are called "dark patterns" — designs deliberately made to trick you. They will all become illegal.

**Subscription traps:**
If signing up for something takes one click, cancelling must also take one click (or the same number of steps). You won't be forced to phone a number to cancel something you signed up for online.

**Hidden costs:**
The price you see first must be the price you pay. No adding delivery, service charges, or booking fees at the last step of checkout.

**Fake "limited time" pressure:**
"Only 2 left!" when there are actually thousands. "Offer ends in 10 minutes!" when the timer resets if you refresh the page. Both banned.

**Guilt-trip buttons:**
Labelling the "no thanks" option with something like "No, I hate saving money" to make you feel bad for declining. Banned. The "no" option must have a normal, neutral label.

**Disguised adverts:**
If something is an advert, it must clearly say so — in the same size text as the content, not in tiny grey text underneath it.

# YOUR DATA

**You have rights over your data. Britain Forward will actually enforce them.**

✅ **You can ask for your data.** Any company holding data about you must send you a copy within 30 days.

✅ **You can ask for your data to be deleted.** And they must actually delete it — not just hide it.

✅ **If a computer made a decision about you, you can ask why.** Credit refused? Account suspended? Insurance more expensive? You have the right to a plain English explanation of why — "the algorithm decided" is not an answer.

✅ **Companies can only collect data they actually need.** "We might find it useful one day" is not a reason to collect your data.

# CHILDREN

Children get stronger protections because they are more easily manipulated.

✅ **No targeted adverts for children.** If an app or website is mainly used by children, it cannot target adverts based on their behaviour.

✅ **No tricks to keep children hooked.** Streaks that make children feel bad for missing a day. Notifications designed to make them compulsively check. Autoplay that never stops. Infinite scroll. All banned in apps for children.

✅ **Loot boxes are gambling.** Paying real money for a random chance at in-game items is gambling. Children cannot buy them. Advertising them to children is banned.

---

# AT WORK

✅ **Outside your working hours, you are off.**
Your employer cannot expect you to read emails, reply to messages, or do work outside your contracted hours. You will not be penalised for not responding when you are not at work.

---

# LATE AT NIGHT

✅ **Apps cannot send you notifications between 10pm and 7am** unless you have specifically said you want them at those times.

---

# WHAT HAPPENS IF COMPANIES BREAK THE RULES?

The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) — the body that enforces data and privacy law — gets proper powers and proper funding to actually do its job.

Companies that break the rules face:
- Fines up to 4% of their global income
- Named senior person held personally responsible
- Their specific practices banned within 24 hours if the violation is serious
- Their name published on a public list of violations

If you've been harmed — lost money, had your privacy violated — you can bring a claim without needing a lawyer, through a new Digital Consumer Tribunal.

---

# WHAT WE DON'T KNOW YET

Digital technology moves fast. There are almost certainly harmful practices that don't exist yet — or that exist but haven't been identified yet.

Britain Forward commits to reviewing these rules every year. A panel of consumer advocates, disability experts, child safety specialists, and technology experts will watch for new harmful practices and recommend new rules.

We will keep pace with the technology. We will not wait for harm to become enormous before acting.
