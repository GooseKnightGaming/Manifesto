# PILLAR ONE: FIX THE SYSTEM
## Democratic & Political Renewal

## 1.1 MINISTERIAL COMPETENCE

Ministers will have demonstrable expertise or professional experience relevant to their department. Health Secretary: worked in healthcare. Chancellor: worked in economics. Education Secretary: worked in education. Defence Secretary: served or worked in defence. No learning on the job at the nation's expense.

---

## 1.2 ELECTORAL REFORM — RANKED WEIGHTED VOTING

*(Full detail: sub-document — Voting System Overhaul)*

Ranked Weighted Voting replaces First Past the Post. Voters rank in preference order. Points by ranking. Winners must command broad support. No wasted votes. No tactical voting. Every constituency matters.

Compulsory Participation — Australian Model. Every eligible citizen participates. Spoiled ballots and NOTA valid.

*HRA fallback:* If legal opinion concludes compulsory voting is unlawful under the HRA, we implement an incentive-based alternative instead. The principle — full democratic participation — is preserved; the mechanism adapts to constitutional law. This is constitutional respect, not abandonment.

NOTA on every ballot. Formally counted, published, acted on. NOTA winning outright triggers fresh election within 60 days — original candidates ineligible.

**Coalition Red Lines:** No coalition with any party that opposes ranked weighted voting, reverses public ownership of core infrastructure, or abandons ministerial competence requirements.

---

## 1.3 POLICY-BASED VOTING — THE LONG-TERM DIRECTION

Most voters want policies from multiple parties — not one party's entire package. The current system forces all-or-nothing choices.

**The Swiss Federal Council Model:** A National Implementation Council — five to seven senior figures drawn proportionally from parties per electoral share, confirmed by Parliament through cross-party vote, serving fixed terms. The Council implements. Parliament debates and legislates. Citizens vote directly on major questions through binding referenda.

**Britain Forward's pathway:** This Parliament: Citizens' Assembly established, policy-based voting researched and published. Next Parliament: binding referendum on Assembly proposals. Long-term: constitutional framework adapted based on what Britain votes for.

---

## 1.4 CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

**The Monarchy:** Constitutional custodian of last resort. Sovereignty resides in the British people. Monarch acts only in genuine constitutional crisis to restore conditions for popular sovereignty.

**The House of Lords:** Current form indefensible; function valuable. Three options; public consultation; binding referendum. The public decides.

**National Oversight Council:** Elected members plus independently appointed experts. No government appointments. Power to delay and refer — not permanently block.

**Parliament:** London now. Government departments phased out regionally. Independent commission reports within five years. Public referendum decides Parliament's future location.

---

## 1.5 POLITICAL ACCOUNTABILITY

**Conflict of Interest:** The test: Did this company materially benefit from a specific decision this MP made or influenced? Five-year pre-office exclusion. No involvement while serving. Ten-year post-office exclusion. Family benefit: same standard applies.

**MP Pay — No Exceptions:**

MPs paid the median wage of the constituency they represent. Full stop. No floor above the constituency reality. The principle is skin-in-the-game. Fix it for your constituency, or live with what you've failed to fix.

Reasonable accommodation allowance for genuine travel and staff costs. Zero outside income while serving. All expenses: publicly searchable, audited quarterly. Personal and family costs never eligible. Pay rises: same process as public sector.

Annual plain-English breakdown — national and local. Every library, GP surgery, job centre, post office, and online. Independently verified. Actual proportions. Because the reason people resist taxation is not selfishness — it's that they don't see the return.

## 1.8 PLAIN LANGUAGE — A LEGAL STANDARD
*Model: Sweden (plain language legal requirement since 1993)*

**The Problem:** Government communications are frequently written in jargon designed to protect institutions rather than inform citizens. This disproportionately affects people with lower literacy, cognitive differences, and less exposure to bureaucratic language. It costs people money, rights, and outcomes — invisibly, daily.

**The Plain Language Standard — Legal Requirement:**

All communications from any public body must meet the Plain Language Standard before issue:
- Reading age: accessible to approximately age 12 for standard communications; age 9 for emergency or safety communications
- Average sentence length under 20 words; no sentence over 35 words
- One idea per paragraph
- Jargon defined on first use; avoided where plain alternatives exist
- Acronyms defined on first use
- Active, direct address: "you need to do X by Y date" not "it is required that the recipient comply"
- Every communication makes clear: what the person needs to do, by when, and what happens if they don't

**Applies to:** All government letters, online forms, legal notices, benefit letters, tax communications, healthcare communications, court documents for members of the public, policy documents.

**Exempt:** Internal technical documents between qualified professionals; academic publications (though plain English summaries still required).

**The Plain Language Commissioner:** Independent office established day one. Powers to audit, require rewriting, and fine bodies that systematically fail the standard. Reports to Parliament, not ministers. Annual public compliance report. Decisions are judicially reviewable but not ministerially overridable.

**Plain Language in Courts:** Every participant in legal proceedings involving the public has the right to a plain-language explanation of what is happening, what is being decided, and what their options are. Judgements affecting members of the public include a plain-language summary.

**Britain Forward applies this to itself first.** All policy documents: technical AND plain English. All political materials: plain English standard. If something cannot be explained plainly, it has not been thought through clearly enough.

> *"If you can't explain it simply, you don't understand it well enough."*

## 1.10 FEDERAL STRUCTURE — THE CANTON PRINCIPLE FOR BRITAIN
*Model: Swiss cantons (adapted); German Länder*

**The Principle — Subsidiarity:**

Decisions should be made at the lowest level of government that can make them effectively. This is called subsidiarity — the organising principle of Swiss cantonal democracy and German federalism. National government should not decide what a village does with its high street.

**The England Problem — Why Switzerland Doesn't Directly Translate:**

Switzerland's largest canton has ~16% of national population. England has 85% of the UK population. A cantonal model treating England as a single unit reproduces the existing problem with a different name. The honest answer requires English regional assemblies with real powers — which currently don't exist and must be created.

**The Four-Level Federal Model:**

*Level One — Federal Government:* Defence, foreign policy, macro-economic policy, currency, immigration, constitutional matters, national infrastructure standards.

*Level Two — National Parliaments:*
- Scotland: existing Scottish Parliament — powers maintained
- Wales: Senedd — powers expanded to match Scottish model
- Northern Ireland: Assembly — maintained under GFA framework
- England: *NEW* — England needs its own Parliament or Assembly, separate from the UK Parliament. The West Lothian Question (Scottish MPs voting on English matters) ends.

*Level Three — Regional Assemblies (England):*
Approximately 9 regions based on existing combined authority and regional boundaries:
Greater London (GLA expanded) | South East | South West | East of England | East Midlands | West Midlands | Yorkshire and the Humber | North West | North East

Regional powers: transport, skills and further education, economic development, spatial planning, healthcare delivery elements, policing priorities, culture and tourism.

*Level Four — Local Government:*
Day-to-day local services, local planning, local transport, parks, environmental enforcement, local housing decisions.

**The Fiscal Model — Genuine Financial Autonomy:**

Each level has defined taxing powers within federal limits. Regional and national governments set their own rates within bands. Federal government sets minimum service standards (a floor, not a ceiling). Fiscal equalisation mechanism: richer regions contribute to a fund supporting less well-resourced regions — formula-based and transparent, not politically negotiated each spending round.

**The Northern Ireland Question:**

Northern Ireland's constitutional position under the Good Friday Agreement is sui generis. The Assembly and cross-border bodies (North-South Ministerial Council) are maintained within the federal structure. GFA compatibility is non-negotiable and requires specialist constitutional legal work before legislation.

**The Scottish Independence Question:**

A genuine federal model with real Scottish Parliament powers changes the independence calculus. If Scotland has genuine autonomy over most domestic matters, real financial independence, and a voice in federal government equivalent to its population share — the argument that independence is necessary to achieve self-determination is substantially altered. This does not make independence impossible or unnecessary from a Scottish perspective — that remains for Scottish people to determine. But it changes what the comparison is.

**Implementation — Generational, Not One Parliament:**

- *This Parliament:* Constitutional Convention established — citizens and experts from all nations and regions; Citizens' Assembly input; federal model designed; public consultation; draft constitutional framework published
- *Referendum:* UK-wide, with separate regional breakdowns of results published
- *Next Parliament (if yes vote):* Enabling legislation; English regional assemblies established; powers transferred; fiscal framework designed
- *Subsequent Parliaments:* Full implementation over 10-15 years; adjustments based on evidence

This is a direction and a process — not an imposition. Britain decides.

## 3.2 PUBLIC DIGNITY INFRASTRUCTURE

*Not charity. Infrastructure.*

**Public Toilets:** Statutory minimum, ring-fenced funding. A-road laybys: free, maintained, lit, safe. HGV rest areas: proper facilities, showers, security, CCTV. Petrol stations on public contracts: free access. Changing Places in all new major public buildings. Free at point of use.

**Hygiene Standards — Legal Requirement:**

Where hand washing facilities are available in a public toilet, they must be: functional, supplied with soap, supplied with running warm water, and supplied with a drying method. A publicly maintained toilet without functioning hand washing facilities is not a compliant facility — maintenance obligation enforced.

*On hand washing behaviour:* Making it a criminal offence for individuals to not wash their hands is not viable — enforcement requires observation inside toilet facilities, which creates worse problems than it solves. The policy solution is: ensure the facilities are so good that not using them is the exception, not the norm. Clean sinks. Reliable soap. Working taps. Good lighting. Mirrors. The evidence on nudge design for hand washing compliance is strong — environment drives behaviour more reliably than law.

For food handlers, healthcare workers, and others where hand hygiene is a genuine public health matter: enforcement of existing law strengthened. The gap is resourcing, not legislation.

**Public Showers:** At community centres, leisure facilities, and urban hubs. Infrastructure, not charity.

**Public Kitchens:** Accessible cooking spaces, any time, FSA-standard, staffed during peak hours. Evening cooking classes. Available to anyone.

**Public Orchards & Community Growing:** Public orchards — free harvest. Commons Reserve — protected seed stock, clearly signed. Community growing alongside. Connected to schools.

**HGV Rest Areas — Secure and Equipped:**
- Proper facilities: toilets, showers, hot food and drink
- Security: CCTV coverage of all bays and access points; adequate lighting; on-site or contracted security at major rest areas, particularly on high-theft routes; partnership with police and hauliers on load theft prevention
- Load theft and driver assault treated as serious crimes; reporting mechanisms that actually work

## 3.3 PUBLIC TRANSPORT — FREE OR HEAVILY SUBSIDISED

*Model: Luxembourg (free buses since 2020)*

**Buses — Free at Point of Use:** Urban, suburban, and rural buses free. Minimum service standards — 30-minute peak frequency baseline. Night services restored. Real-time information at all stops.

**Trams — Inner-City and Beyond:**
*Model: Strasbourg, Vienna, Amsterdam, Manchester Metrolink*

Trams serve the zone between pedestrianised centres and the motorised periphery — where cars shouldn't go but buses can't reach as effectively. Inner-city tram networks in all major cities above a defined population threshold. Extensions in existing systems. New systems assessed for cities where density supports viability. Zero local emissions. Integrated ticketing. Free or heavily subsidised fares.

**Urban Commuter Rail:** Fares capped and subsidised within major city regions. Integrated ticketing. Oyster-style systems extended to all major city regions.

Funding: road pricing revenue; reduced car infrastructure costs; reduced congestion costs; reduced NHS air pollution costs; general taxation.

## 3.4 WALKABLE CITIES
*Design principle: Copenhagen, Amsterdam, Vienna — the 15-minute city*

**The 15-Minute City:** Most daily needs accessible within 15 minutes on foot. New developments: maximum distances — primary school (10-min walk), GP (10-min walk), green space (5-min walk), shops (10-min walk). Street design prioritises pedestrian movement. Ground floors: shops, cafes, services — not car parks or blank walls.

**Existing City Centres:** Pedestrianisation expanded. Pavement quality: national minimum standard. Crossings timed for pedestrians. Pavement parking: enforcement substantially strengthened.

**Accessibility:** Wheelchair and mobility access throughout. Tactile paving and audible signals maintained. Dropped kerbs at all crossings. Shared space (kerb-free): evidence on visual impairment clearly supports kerb separation — used only where evidence genuinely supports it.

---

## 3.5 BIKEABLE CITIES
*Model: Netherlands — physical separation, not painted lines*

**Protected Cycle Lanes:** Physically separated on all urban arterial roads — raised kerb, planters, or parking buffer. Painted lines are not cycle infrastructure. Minimum 1.5m each direction. Continuous — a lane that disappears at every junction is not a lane.

**Safe Junctions:** Separate cyclist signal phases. Advanced stop lines elsewhere.

**Cycle Parking:** Secure, covered at all public transport stops, stations, and major public buildings. Cargo bike parking and charging.

**Bike-Share Schemes:** Publicly funded or subsidised in all cities above a defined population threshold. E-bikes included. Integrated with public transport accounts.

**E-Bike Subsidy:** Subsidised purchase for households below a defined income threshold — French and German evidence base.

**Rural Cycling:** Speed limit review on narrow rural roads. Improved signage. Off-road paths alongside major rural A-roads where on-road cycling is genuinely dangerous.

## 3.6 ENERGY — BRITAIN POWERS BRITAIN

**Honest Transition Sequence:** Renewable capacity cannot replace all demand immediately. During transition, Britain maintains domestic fossil fuel production. Not contradiction — honest sequencing. Germany's cautionary tale: premature nuclear phase-out, Russian gas dependence, acute crisis. Britain will not repeat it.

**Renewables:** Solar (rooftops not fields, mandatory on new builds, retrofit programme); Wind (onshore and offshore); Tidal and wave (world-class resources currently neglected); Hydro (Scotland's river network — community micro-hydro); Geothermal (Cornwall as template).

**Nuclear:** Stable baseload. Next-generation reactor investment.

**Transition Fuels — Milestone Phase-Out (achievement thresholds, not calendar dates):**
- Coal power: phases out when green baseload covers peak demand for 30 consecutive days
- Gas for domestic heating: phases out sector by sector as heat pump penetration and grid capacity demonstrate readiness
- Coking coal: phases out when green steel is commercially viable at scale

Worker transition: explicit commitment. No community left behind. Retraining funded. Green energy jobs prioritised for fossil fuel workers.

## 3.7 RURAL CONNECTIVITY — NO COMMUNITY LEFT BEHIND

*Capital punishment:* Britain Forward's position on current evidence: The wrongful conviction problem — Birmingham Six, Guildford Four, Stefan Kiszko — each would have been executed; each was innocent; each was exonerated by later evidence. ## 5.5 EMPLOYMENT REFORM

**Advertised Salaries: Mandatory.** All job ads state actual salary. "Competitive" banned. Salary question in interviews abolished.

**Banned Interview Questions:** Hypothetical personality questions; personal life, family, relationship status; salary history; questions irrelevant to job performance.

**Alternative Assessment Formats:** Work trials, portfolio reviews, task assessments. Public sector leads.

**Neurodivergent Adaptations and Strengths:**

*Adaptations:* Questions in advance as standard option. Reasonable adjustments enforced throughout hiring.

*Strengths recognition:* Neurodivergent people are not a category to be managed — they are a talent pool with specific capabilities. Pattern recognition, attention to detail, hyperfocus, systemised thinking, lateral connection — capabilities, not symptoms. Public sector leads in matching neurodivergent candidates to roles where their strengths genuinely excel. Private sector: tax recognition for genuine strength-matching programmes.

**Gait and Physical Bearing Bias:**

Research on interview bias documents that assessors make rapid, largely unconscious judgements based on how candidates physically present — including walk, posture, and bearing — within seconds of a candidate entering a room, before any substantive interaction.

Where physical presentation is influenced by factors entirely unrelated to job performance — including structural foot differences, footwear design, disability, chronic pain, or neurological variation — assessments based on it are both unfair and poor predictors of actual capability.

Britain Forward commits to:
- Including gait and physical bearing bias in unconscious bias training frameworks for all public sector hiring
- Commissioning the Office for Evidence-Based Policy to research the scope of physical presentation bias in British hiring, including the specific mechanisms by which foot structure and footwear design may influence gait and assessor perception
- Extending "alternative assessment formats" to explicitly include alternative entry formats — candidates should not be disadvantaged by a walk across a room before an interview has begun
- Requiring public sector hiring panels to be trained in recognising and discounting physical presentation cues unrelated to role performance

**Entry-Level Standards:** Experience requirements for starter positions are banned. Employers cannot reclassify genuinely entry-level roles as mid-level to impose experience barriers. Statutory framework defines entry-level based on actual role requirements, not employer labelling. Enforcement: review mechanism with real consequences for mislabelling.

**4-Day Working Week — Flexible Rolling Structure:**

Piloted in public sector with rigorous evaluation. National rollout conditional on demonstrated productivity maintenance and worker wellbeing improvement.

*Pattern flexibility is the principle — not "everyone gets Friday off."* Workers choose their 4-day pattern where jobs permit. Rolling schedules accommodated. Services requiring 7-day coverage keep 7-day coverage through rotating staff patterns — not through individual workers doing 5 days.

**Workplace Rights:** Equal pay regardless of age. Zero-hours reform. Right to disconnect. Corporate pay ratio limits.

## 5.8 NATIONAL CIVIC RESILIENCE
*Model: Finland's Comprehensive Security doctrine; Nordic civic education tradition*

**The Finnish Insight:**

Finland treats an informed citizenry as a component of national defence — not metaphorically, but structurally. Their "Comprehensive Security" doctrine explicitly includes civic resilience: the ability of the population to recognise and resist manipulation, disinformation, and the erosion of democratic norms.

The logic: a democracy whose citizens cannot recognise propaganda is vulnerable to propaganda. A democracy whose citizens understand how it works is harder to manipulate, destabilise, or dismantle. Therefore: educating citizens in critical thinking, media literacy, and democratic participation is as much a defence investment as building tanks.

When a wannabe-authoritarian arrives in a country with genuine civic education, they find an audience that laughs at them rather than rallying to them. Their techniques are immediately recognisable. Their emotional manipulation doesn't land because people have been taught to notice the manipulation rather than feel the emotion.

**The Programme:**

*Propaganda Recognition — Explicit Curriculum:*
- How propaganda techniques work: emotional manipulation, false urgency, us-vs-them framing, repetition, manufactured consensus, authority figures, scapegoating
- Historical case studies: how specific disinformation campaigns worked
- Modern disinformation: social media exploitation; coordinated inauthentic behaviour; bot-amplified content
- Practical application: students assess real historical examples; identify techniques in use

*How Democracies Erode — Historical Curriculum:*
- Case studies: Weimar Germany, Hungary post-2010, interwar Italy
- Common patterns: emergency powers creep, judicial capture, press freedom erosion, minority scapegoating, election manipulation
- Early warning signs: what they looked like before the damage was obvious
- What individual citizens and institutions did and didn't do that made a difference

*Prebunking — The Evidence-Based Approach:*

Research consistently shows prebunking (explaining manipulation techniques before exposure) is more effective than debunking (correcting after exposure). Once a false belief is formed, correction is unreliable. Prevention is more effective.

The curriculum teaches the *technique* before exposing students to it — "this is how people try to make you angry at a group; now watch how this real example uses exactly those techniques." Recognition before encounter.

**The Civic Resilience Standard:**

Every student completing compulsory education can:
- Identify at least six common propaganda and manipulation techniques by name and example
- Explain how at least two historical democratic systems eroded and what the warning signs were
- Assess a piece of political or media communication for persuasion vs manipulation indicators
- Explain how social media algorithms create filter bubbles and emotional amplification
- Distinguish between a legitimate disagreement and a manufactured controversy

These are analytical skills — not a political position. They apply equally to manipulation from the left, the right, foreign governments, commercial advertisers, and domestic political parties including Britain Forward.

**Implementation:** Integrated across subjects — not a standalone "propaganda class." History, English, PSHE, computing, and citizenship all contribute. Age-appropriate from primary through secondary. Teacher training included in initial teacher training and CPD. Assessed through portfolio evidence and project work — not high-stakes exam format.

**Beyond Schools:** Public library civic resilience resources — free, accessible, regularly updated. Online resources in plain English. Community education programmes for adults who didn't receive this in school. Partnership with trusted broadcasters on public information campaigns.

## 6.3 THE LANGUAGE COMPETENCY STANDARD

**The Principle:**

Functional communication in a native British language — English, Welsh, Scottish Gaelic, Irish, Scots, Cornish, or British Sign Language — is a reasonable expectation of participation in British civic and economic life.

This is not an Oxonian standard. It is not about accent, dialect, or regional variation. It is about basic functional literacy and communication at a level sufficient to navigate daily life, employment, civic participation, and legal processes.

**This standard applies to everyone.** Because the alternative — a standard that applies only to immigrants — would be both discriminatory and immediately circumventable. The people who write "we want are cuntry back" are subject to the same standard as every person they wish to exclude. A universal standard, honestly applied, exposes this.

**What the Test Covers:**

*Not tested:* Spelling perfection; academic grammar; vocabulary breadth; formal writing style; accent or pronunciation; regional dialect or vernacular expression.

*Tested — basic functional communication only:*
- Reading and understanding a short plain-English text (a GP letter, a council notice, a job advertisement)
- Writing a short response that communicates a clear meaning (a simple letter, a form, a basic message)
- Understanding and following simple spoken instructions
- Making yourself understood in spoken communication on everyday topics

**Language Options:** English; Welsh; Scottish Gaelic; Irish (Northern Ireland contexts); Scots; Cornish (where demonstrated as primary language); British Sign Language — full equivalence.

**Accommodations — Non-Negotiable:**

*Dyslexia:* Extended time; oral alternatives; screen reader and text-to-speech support; alternative question formats; spelling errors that don't affect comprehension are not penalised — the test measures communication, not spelling.

*Other specific learning differences:* Format adaptations; reader/scribe support; extended time.

*Cognitive Impairment — Genuine:* Medical assessment pathway — certified by independent medical panel, not self-declared. Exemption or adapted assessment. Support and advocacy provision rather than punitive consequences.

*Physical disability affecting communication:* AAC devices accepted; BSL as equivalent pathway; extended time and format adaptations.

*Acquired conditions (stroke, brain injury, progressive neurological conditions):* Medical assessment pathway; rehabilitation support prioritised; reassessment available after rehabilitation.

*Recent arrival:* Newly arrived residents have a defined period (minimum 24 months) to reach the standard before assessment. Free language education provided — the state's obligation is to enable, not just to demand.

**Consequences — Honest and Constitutional:**

*British citizens — native-born or naturalised:* Cannot be deported. This is constitutionally absolute — international law, domestic citizenship law, and the ECHR all prohibit deportation or statelessness of nationals. Consequence of failing: mandatory enrolment in a funded language support programme; retest available after programme completion. No punitive financial consequence. The political consequence — having failed a standard they claim others should meet — is its own result.

*Non-citizens with settled status:* Mandatory funded language programme; retest required. Continued failure: citizenship pathway affected until standard is met.

*Non-citizens without settled status (visa holders, recent arrivals, immigration applicants):* Language competency standard integrated into immigration assessment; failure results in visa application refused or renewal refused; grace period for genuine recent arrivals.

*Asylum seekers:* Asylum assessment not affected by language competency — asylum is humanitarian, not a language test. Language support provided; competency expected within a defined integration period if asylum is granted.

**Administration:** Delivered through libraries, job centres, adult education centres, and online. Free at point of use — always. Retest available with no limit. Independent assessment body — not administered by the Home Office.

## 6.5 FOREIGN AID — REFORM, NOT SENTIMENT

Independent audit commissioned day one. Three outcome categories:

1. **Retain and expand:** Clear measurable outcomes, genuine recipient benefit
2. **Reform:** Genuine potential, poor implementation — results-based model required
3. **Cut:** Cannot demonstrate recipient benefit; money captured by corruption or administration

**The Philosophy:**

The next person who could cure a disease, solve a food crisis, or transform clean energy might currently be a child without clean water, without education, without the chance to develop what they have. Effective aid is not charity in that frame — it is unlocking capability the world needs. It addresses circumstance so that capability can develop. This guides which aid Britain retains: not the aid that makes donors feel better, but the aid that genuinely changes what a person can become.

**Results-Based International Investment:** Trade, science, infrastructure partnerships. Measurable outcomes. Britain as a hub of excellence. Partnerships of equals.

**The EU Question:** Explicit neutrality. Constructive partnership on science, research, education, and trade. Any formal relationship change: public referendum.

# SOCIAL POLICY

## Equality & Identity
Full legal recognition of non-binary identities. Gender transition recognition — streamlined, respectful. Protection from discrimination under law.

## Relationships & Unions — Recognition For All

Britain recognises committed adult relationships in all forms. Civil marriage, civil partnership, religious marriage in any faith, pagan hand-fasting, humanist ceremony, secular declaration — all equally valid in law where freely entered between consenting adults.

Legal status depends on commitment and process, not which tradition performed the ceremony. Religious institutions perform ceremonies according to their own principles; the state recognises the commitment.

Universal minimum requirements: consent, adulthood, competence, non-coercion — applied universally regardless of number of participants or tradition.

Where adult relationships extend beyond the traditional two-person form and all parties freely consent, legal recognition is not categorically refused. The core requirements apply universally. Practical legal framework design (inheritance, next-of-kin, parental rights, tax, property) follows evidence-led review with non-negotiable safeguards against coercion.

## Sign Language
BSL promoted nationally. Required in healthcare, education, emergency services. Curriculum option in all schools.

## Separation of Church and State
Legal recognition of relationships determined solely by civil law. Religious institutions perform ceremonies freely — no independent legal authority over civil status.

# OFFICE FOR EVIDENCE-BASED POLICY — RESEARCH COMMISSIONS

The following specific research commissions are outstanding, to be initiated within the first Parliament:

**Foot Configuration, Footwear Design, and Structural Disadvantage:**

The Office is commissioned to investigate:
1. Actual prevalence of different foot configurations (including Egyptian foot, Morton's toe, and other common variations) in the British population
2. Relationship between foot configuration and standard footwear design — whether common British foot configurations are systematically disadvantaged by current design norms
3. Documented relationship between gait, physical presentation, and professional and social outcomes — including interview assessment, arts audition, and sports selection
4. Extent to which footwear-induced gait differences may mediate professional outcomes unrelated to actual capability
5. Representation patterns in visible barefoot contexts (swimming, dance, arts) — whether any foot configurations are statistically underrepresented and through what mechanisms

Research published in full — technical and plain English — within the first Parliament. Policy follows the evidence. If the research confirms the mechanisms described, Britain Forward will legislate accordingly. If it does not confirm them, Britain Forward will say so openly and explain what it found instead.
