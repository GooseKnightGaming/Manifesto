# SUB-DOCUMENT ONE: RE-NATIONALISATION IN FULL

## The Core Argument

Britain sold its national infrastructure to private investors over several decades. The results are measurable:

- **Water:** Thames Water near insolvent; 3 billion litres lost daily through leaking pipes; rivers contaminated by sewage; shareholders paid dividends while infrastructure decayed
- **Energy:** Prices dependent on global markets Britain does not control; profits extracted by foreign-owned companies from British households
- **Rail:** The most expensive fares in Europe; fragmented services; private operators subsidised by the state while keeping profits in good years
- **Oil:** North Sea reserves extracted by private companies; revenues leaving Britain rather than funding British public services

The privatisation experiment has run for forty years. The evidence is in.

---

## The Three-Tier Model — In Full Detail

### Tier One: Infrastructure Layer — 100% Publicly Owned

The *physical networks* — pipes, wires, cables, track — owned outright by the British public, managed by public bodies.

**British Water** — owns and maintains the water network
**British Grid** — owns and maintains energy transmission and distribution
**British Rail Infrastructure** — owns track, stations, and signalling

**Direct accountability:** When water is contaminated, British Water is accountable. Not a private company, then an internal review, then an independent review, then a regulator, then a tribunal. One body. Direct. Real consequences.

**Acquisition mechanism:**

*Regulatory Asset Base model:* We acquire at the independently assessed value of the physical infrastructure assets — not at inflated stock market valuations that include future profit expectations and speculative premiums.

*Distressed asset opportunity:* Several water companies are currently in financial difficulty. Thames Water is effectively insolvent. Distressed acquisition through administration costs a fraction of market rate. The private sector's failure is not a tragedy for nationalisation — it is an opportunity.

*Long-term bond financing:* Capital acquisition financed through long-term public infrastructure bonds spread over years. Interest rates at government borrowing rates — lower than any private company can achieve. Bond repayments funded by the revenues of the acquired assets themselves. The asset pays for its own acquisition.

*Operational transition:* Not overnight. Existing regulators (Ofwat, Ofgem) immediately repurposed as transitional public bodies. Existing management retained during transition — we are changing ownership, not sacking everyone who knows how a water treatment plant works. Service continuity a legal requirement throughout. Full operational transition: 5-7 years per sector.

**International law:** Public ownership of infrastructure is explicitly permitted under WTO rules and all trade agreements Britain has signed. Every WTO member has publicly owned infrastructure. Legal opinions from leading international trade law counsel will be published before any legislation.

### Tier Two: Operating Layer — Majority Public, Citizen Shares

Companies operating *through* the public infrastructure:
- Government holds majority ownership (51-75%)
- Remaining shares available to British citizens directly — not institutional investors, not foreign funds
- Citizens receive annual dividends — a genuine stake in their own country
- Some market discipline maintained — citizen shareholders lose value if the company is badly managed

**Citizen share registry:** Managed similarly to Premium Bonds or NS&I accounts. Citizens register their holding. Dividends paid annually. Shareholder votes on operational matters conducted digitally.

### Tier Three: Competitive Layer — Fully Private

Services and innovations built *on top of* the public infrastructure compete freely. The infrastructure is public. What runs on it competes. Innovation encouraged.

**The internet analogy:** The internet runs on publicly-funded backbone infrastructure. Private companies build on top of it and compete freely. Britain's physical infrastructure follows the same logic.

---

## British Oil — A Different Model

Oil is different from water and energy networks — it is not a natural monopoly; multiple companies can extract from different fields.

**The Norwegian Template:** Equinor (formerly Statoil) is 67% government-owned. It operates commercially, competes internationally, and funds Norway's sovereign wealth fund with its profits.

**The British Model:**

*British Petroleum plc (new):* New state-owned oil company with majority government stake. Operates alongside existing private extractors. Bids for new North Sea licences and licences coming up for renewal. Over time, government stake in British oil production grows as licences roll over.

*Windfall tax on excess profits:* Existing private extractors pay windfall tax on excess profits above a defined threshold. Revenue to the Sovereign Wealth Fund.

*Why not full nationalisation of existing operations:* International investment law protects existing licence holders against expropriation without full market compensation. The licence rollover model achieves the same long-term goal without legal exposure.

---

## What Nationalisation Fixes — Specifically

**Water contamination:** Under British Water, the CEO answers to Parliament. Contamination triggers immediate public inquiry with legal force. No private shareholders to protect. No incentive to minimise disclosure. The Thames Water sewage scandal — 300,000+ sewage dumps over three years — becomes structurally impossible under this model.

**Leaking pipes:** Britain loses ~3 billion litres of water daily through leaking infrastructure. Under private ownership, fixing leaks costs money that reduces dividends. Under public ownership, fixing leaks reduces costs and improves service. The incentive aligns with the public interest for the first time.

**Rail fares:** Britain has the most expensive rail fares in Europe. Under British Rail Operations (Tier Two), profit goes to British citizens as shareholders or to reinvestment in the service — not to shareholders extracting value from a captive public.

---

## What Nationalisation Does NOT Fix

We are honest about limits:

- Nationalisation does not eliminate management failure — public bodies can be badly managed; the difference is that badly managed public bodies are directly accountable to Parliament
- Nationalisation does not eliminate maintenance costs — the pipes still need fixing; the incentive to fix them is now aligned with the public interest
- Nationalisation does not solve the energy generation capacity gap immediately — building generation takes years under any ownership model

# SUB-DOCUMENT TWO: UBI AND THE 4-DAY WORKING WEEK IN FULL

## Part One: UBI

### What UBI Is — Simply Stated

Universal Basic Income is a regular, unconditional payment made to every qualifying person, regardless of whether they work, regardless of what they earn, regardless of their circumstances.

It is a floor. Not a ceiling. Not a replacement for ambition.

### The Rate and Net Cost

**Target rate:** Approximately £100 per week (£5,200 per year)

This is the aspirational rate. The actual rollout rate will be confirmed by evidence from properly evaluated pilots and validated by independent economic modelling before national rollout.

**Gross cost:** ~50 million qualifying adults × £100/week × 52 weeks = ~£260 billion per year gross.

**Why the gross figure is misleading:**

*Tax recoupture:* UBI paid to higher earners is taxed back through marginal income tax rates. A household earning £80,000 effectively returns their UBI through the tax system. Net receipt for higher earners: near zero.

*Benefits system replacement:* Current means-tested benefits expenditure (excluding state pension and NHS) is approximately £100+ billion per year. UBI replaces the majority of this. The administrative cost of means-testing — assessments, case managers, tribunals, sanctions, appeals — is also eliminated.

*Economic stimulus:* UBI recipients spend locally. Poverty-related NHS costs and justice costs reduce. These are real fiscal offsets.

**Net cost:** The net additional cost of UBI — after tax recoupture and benefits system replacement savings — requires independent economic modelling. That modelling will be commissioned and published before any national rollout decision.

### Addressing the Objections

**"It will make people lazy."**

Evidence says: No.

Finland's 2017-2018 pilot: UBI recipients were more likely to be employed after the pilot than the control group. Significantly less stressed, healthier, and more confident.

Kenya's GiveDirectly programme: recipients invested in businesses, education, and housing.

Alaska Permanent Fund (since 1982): No measurable increase in unemployment.

Canada's 1970s Mincome pilot: School attendance increased. Hospital visits decreased. People started businesses.

People in jobs they chose, that they can afford to take, stay longer and perform better. People who don't need to take any available job just to survive find jobs that suit them — and those matches last.

Some people will live on £100 a week and not work. They will have a safe, austere, very quiet life without luxuries. This is their choice. The cost to the state of supporting that choice is less than the current cost of sanctions, assessments, tribunals, and crisis services around the same people living in poverty and stress.

**"£100 a week isn't enough to live on."**

Correct. It isn't intended to be. It is a floor. Working people receive UBI plus wages. Retired people receive UBI plus pension. Disabled people receive UBI plus targeted disability support. Only non-working, non-seeking, non-disabled adults live on UBI alone — and they live simply and austerely by choice.

### The Criminal Sentence Question

UBI is suspended *during sentence* — not permanently withdrawn.

On the day of release, UBI resumes immediately. No application, no assessment, no waiting period.

The sentence period creates a gap in UBI that cannot be recovered. That is the financial penalty. But a released prisoner who is immediately financially destitute is more likely to reoffend. This is well-documented. A released prisoner who immediately has £100/week coming in has more options than one who has nothing.

Released prisoners additionally receive an active resettlement package.

## Part Two: The 4-Day Working Week

### The Problem with Five Days

The five-day, 40-hour working week was designed for an industrial economy in the early 20th century. It was not designed for knowledge work, service industries, or modern parenting. Britain is one of the least productive economies in the developed world per hour worked, despite working long hours. The problem is not effort. It is system design.

### The Evidence

**Iceland (2015-2019):** Four-day week pilots covering over 1% of the working population. Productivity maintained or improved in the vast majority of workplaces. Worker wellbeing improved significantly. The model has since been adopted by the majority of Iceland's workforce.

**Microsoft Japan (2019):** Productivity increased 40%. Energy consumption fell.

**UK 4-Day Week Pilot (2022):** 61 companies, 2,900 workers, six months. 92% of companies continued. Revenue broadly unchanged. Sick days fell 65%. Staff turnover fell significantly.

### The Rolling Pattern Principle

*Not "everyone gets Friday off."*

Workers choose their 4-day pattern where jobs permit. Rolling schedules accommodated. Services requiring 7-day coverage keep 7-day coverage through rotating staff patterns — not through individual workers doing 5 days.

A healthcare worker, a police officer, a firefighter — their sector cannot simply take Fridays off. The four-day week for them means better scheduling, proper rest patterns, and equivalent hours through different configurations. The principle is the same; the mechanism adapts.

### Implementation

**Phase One (this Parliament):** Public sector pilots with rigorous evaluation.
**Phase Two:** Full evaluation published. Employer guidance. Tax incentives for adopters. Evidence base built.
**Phase Three:** Sector-by-sector dialogue with unions and employers. Implementation timelines that work for each sector.

**National rollout:** Conditional on demonstrated productivity maintenance and worker wellbeing improvement. The pilots produce the evidence. The evidence drives the decision.

# SUB-DOCUMENT THREE: VOTING SYSTEM OVERHAUL IN FULL

## Why FPTP Fails

**Wasted votes:** In safe seats — the majority of constituencies — your vote has no practical effect on the outcome.

**Tactical voting:** Millions of British voters do not vote for who they actually want. They vote to stop someone else winning.

**Distorted representation:** In 2015, UKIP received 12.6% of the national vote and won 1 seat. The SNP received 4.7% and won 56 seats.

**Marginal seat dominance:** Government policy is shaped by approximately 80-100 marginal constituencies. The rest of Britain's views are structurally irrelevant.

## Ranked Weighted Voting — How It Works

Voters rank candidates in order of preference: 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and so on. Points are awarded by ranking — higher for first preference, decreasing for each subsequent preference.

The candidate with the highest total points wins.

**Why better than FPTP:** Votes in every constituency count. No tactical voting required. Winning candidates must have broad appeal, not just an intense minority.

**Why better than pure PR:** The constituency link is maintained. Your MP represents your area. That accountability is valuable and is preserved.

**Why better than simple AV:** Ranked Weighted Voting considers all preferences simultaneously rather than sequentially eliminating candidates. More mathematically robust at measuring genuine breadth of support.

## Compulsory Participation

Every eligible citizen participates. Spoiled ballots and NOTA are valid. The goal is full democratic engagement — not compelled political opinion.

The HRA fallback: if compulsory participation is found incompatible with the Human Rights Act, the alternative is maximum-incentive voluntary participation — strong positive incentives for voting — achieving the same goal through a different mechanism.

## NOTA — What Happens If It Wins

Fresh election within 60 days. Original candidates ineligible to stand. New candidates selected. Constituency served by caretaker from National Oversight Council.

Cannot be gamed: under compulsory participation, NOTA winning requires genuine majority sentiment. The logistics and costs of repeated by-elections deter organised spoiling.

## Britain Forward's Additional Revenue Sources

**1. Nationalised asset revenues**
Water, energy, and oil currently generate profits flowing to foreign sovereign wealth funds, private equity firms, and offshore-registered investors. Under Britain Forward, these flow to British citizens and the Sovereign Wealth Fund.

*Estimated initial additional revenue:* £5-8 billion per year, growing as stakes increase.

**2. Pharmaceutical price negotiation savings**
NHS England's own analysis estimates £1-2 billion per year initially, rising as the body's bargaining position strengthens.

**3. Stopping mismanagement**
- Procurement reform: £500 million - £1 billion per year in reduced waste
- Benefits administration savings: £5-10 billion per year as UBI replaces means-testing
- Anti-avoidance enforcement: several billion per year in recovered large business tax

**4. Road pricing**
Replaces fuel duty (approximately £25 billion per year) as electric vehicles grow. Revenue hypothecated to roads and transport.

**5. UBI higher-earner tax recoupture**
Significant — precise figure requires full economic modelling. The principle: UBI is largely self-funding at higher income levels through the existing income tax system.

**6. Long-term economic growth**
Better-educated population → higher productivity → higher tax revenues. Healthier population → lower NHS crisis costs. Better infrastructure → higher corporate profitability → higher corporation tax. UBI → more entrepreneurship → more businesses → more income tax.

## The Revenue vs Capital Distinction

**Revenue spending** (NHS wages, UBI payments, welfare, education running costs): must be funded from tax revenues. Cannot be borrowed sustainably.

**Capital investment** (roads, rail, cities, water infrastructure): can be financed through long-term bonds because the asset generates returns or savings over its lifetime.

*This is not ideology. This is how every business and every homeowner finances large purchases. Treating infrastructure investment as current spending is how governments make bad decisions and why Britain keeps patching the same roads.*

## Drug Law Reform

**The problem with the current system:** The classification system is widely criticised as inconsistent, ineffective, and not fit for purpose.

**The evidence:** Portugal (decriminalisation since 2001) — drug-related deaths fell dramatically, HIV infections fell, drug use did not increase significantly, enforcement resources redirected to trafficking. Canada (cannabis regulation since 2018) — tax revenue generated, youth access did not increase. Switzerland (heroin prescription since 1994) — crime by addicts fell substantially, health outcomes improved.

**The framework (when criteria are met):**

*Tier 1 (Low Harm — cannabis-class substances):* Legal, regulated, taxed. Licensed vendors only. Age verification mandatory. No advertising. Quality standards enforced. Revenue to NHS, addiction services, and education.

*Tier 2 (Moderate Harm):* Controlled access through licensed venues or pharmacies. Quantity limits. Harm reduction information at point of sale.

*Tier 3 (High Harm):* Available only through medical prescription or addiction treatment programmes.

*Enforcement focus:* Personal possession for personal use decriminalised across all tiers. Trafficking: serious criminal offence with increased sentences. Supply outside the licensed system: serious offence.

**Criteria for action:** Evidence review concludes a harm-based regulatory model produces lower overall harm than the current criminalised model in a British cultural and legal context; and a full legal and operational framework has been developed.

---

## Sex Work Law Reform

**The problem:** Sex work in Britain operates in a grey area that leaves workers without protection, makes it impossible to report crimes, drives trafficking victims underground, and consumes policing resources that could target actual traffickers.

**The evidence:** New Zealand (2003 decriminalisation) — sex workers gained employment rights, health and safety protections, and ability to report crimes. Trafficking did not increase. Worker health outcomes improved significantly. The model is the strongest evidence base available.

**The framework (when criteria are met):**

- Sex work between consenting adults: fully legal
- Workers: full employment rights, health and safety protections, right to refuse clients, access to healthcare
- Commercial venues: licensed and regulated, mandatory anti-trafficking training, zero tolerance for underage involvement
- Enforcement: trafficking (severe criminal offence with increased resources); coercion (criminal offence); exploitation (criminal offence). Enforcement focus is on exploiters, not workers.
- Exit support: funded pathways, no conditions attached, permanently available

**Criteria for action:** British evidence review of New Zealand's 20+ years of operation completed; regulatory framework fully designed; CPS and NCA confirm operational readiness for redirected enforcement; worker organisations formally consulted.

---

## Relationship Law Reform

**The situation:** Multi-partner relationships exist. They are not recognised by law. The people in them have no property rights, no next-of-kin status, no protection if things go wrong. The law does not prevent these relationships — it simply refuses to protect the people in them.

**The framework (when criteria are met):**

*Registered Multi-Partner Unions:*
- Explicit documented consent from all parties
- Independent legal advice for all parties before registration
- Full financial disclosure between partners
- Cooling-off period (minimum 90 days)
- Legal frameworks for: property, parental responsibility, inheritance, dissolution, partial exit

*Enhanced safeguards:*
- Where significant age disparity exists: additional independent assessment
- Where financial dependency exists: additional independent assessment
- Regular reaffirmation of consent (every five years)
- Strong penalties for coercion — treated as domestic abuse

**Criteria for action:** Full legal framework designed and reviewed by Law Commission; family law practitioners and safeguarding organisations formally consulted; tax and benefits implications fully resolved.

## Summary

Britain Forward introduces a comprehensive Digital Rights Framework:

**Cookie consent:** Non-essential cookies are opt-in by default. Checkboxes start empty. Sliders start off. "Necessary Cookies Only" button must be more prominent than "Accept All Cookies." Rejection must take no more steps than acceptance. Categories must be named in plain English.

**Digital advertising:** Exit buttons must appear immediately when countdown ends. Exit buttons must actually exit. Minimum 44x44 pixel size. Minimum 20px clearance from advert content. Countdown timers must be accurate.

**Dark patterns prohibited:** Subscription traps; hidden costs; false urgency; confirmshaming; disguised adverts; engagement manipulation targeting children; loot boxes classified as gambling for under-18s.

**Algorithmic transparency:** Platforms must explain how recommendation algorithms work. Chronological feed option must be available. Amplification of known false content by algorithm: prohibited.

**Data rights:** Right to your data (30 days). Right to deletion (genuine). Right to explanation of automated decisions (plain English). Right to correction. Data minimisation enforced.

**Children's protections:** No behavioural advertising targeting. No engagement manipulation mechanics. Social media minimum age: genuine verification required.

**Right to disconnect:** No routine employer contact outside contracted hours. No push notifications between 10pm and 7am without explicit consent.

**Enforcement:** ICO gains new powers and substantially increased resourcing. Fines up to 4% of global annual turnover. Named senior accountability at platforms. Private right of action through new Digital Consumer Tribunal.
