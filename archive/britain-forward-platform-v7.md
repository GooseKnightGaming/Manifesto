# THE EVIDENCE COMMITMENT — NON-NEGOTIABLE

This is not a standard political claim. It is a structural commitment backed by a new institution.

**Britain Forward will:**

- Commission independent research before major policy decisions — not after, not to justify decisions already made
- Publish the full research papers underlying every major policy — not summaries, not press releases, the actual research
- Publish plain-English versions of every research paper simultaneously — because evidence that only academics can read is not democratic evidence
- Publish the findings even when they contradict our assumptions — and amend policy accordingly
- Review every major policy annually against its stated objectives — publishing whether it is working, partially working, or failing
- Reverse or reform policies that evidence shows are not achieving their purpose — and explain why, publicly

**The Office for Evidence-Based Policy:**

A new independent body established in the first 100 days:
- Commissions and publishes all government research in the above format
- Reviews policy outcomes annually against stated objectives
- Reports to Parliament, not to ministers — genuinely independent
- Findings cannot be suppressed or delayed by government — published on the Office's schedule, not the government's
- Staffed by independent researchers, not civil servants seconded from the departments being evaluated

**The Plain English Requirement:**

Every research paper published in two formats simultaneously:
- Full academic/technical paper — complete methodology, data, references, limitations
- Plain English summary — written to be understood by anyone, tested on non-specialist readers before publication

This is democracy made real. If the evidence supports a policy, you should be able to explain why to any voter. If you can't explain it plainly, you don't understand it well enough to legislate it.

**The Reversal Commitment:**

Britain Forward will reverse policies that evidence shows are failing. We will not defend failures because they are "our" policies. We will explain what we got wrong, why, and what we are doing instead.

This is not weakness. This is what evidence-based governance actually means.

## 1.3 POLICY-BASED VOTING — THE LONG-TERM DIRECTION

*Aspirational. Genuinely complex. Included honestly. This is where we are heading.*

Most voters don't want Column A whole-hog or Column B whole-hog. They want the Green Party's environmental policy, a different party's immigration approach, a third party's economic model. The current system forces all-or-nothing choices between packages most voters wouldn't design themselves if given the option.

**The Idea — Liquid Democracy:**

Citizens vote on individual policies, not just parties. Switzerland demonstrates this is workable through frequent binding referenda on specific policy questions. Finland, Ireland, and Iceland have used Citizens' Assemblies to develop and ratify constitutional and policy changes.

**The Constitutional Problem It Creates — Honest:**

If citizens vote on policies from multiple parties, a partisan Prime Minister implementing "their" policies regardless of the voted mandate defeats the purpose. You need an implementation layer that isn't party-captured.

**The Solution — A Collective Executive Model:**

Rather than a single Prime Minister from the winning party, a **National Implementation Council** — five to seven senior figures drawn proportionally from parties per their electoral share, confirmed by Parliament through a cross-party vote, serving fixed terms not tied to electoral cycles.

The Council implements policy. Parliament debates and legislates. Citizens vote directly on defined major questions through binding referenda. The Monarch remains as constitutional custodian of last resort.

This separates three functions that are currently collapsed into one:
- Democratic mandate (citizens voting on what they want)
- Legislative process (Parliament debating and refining how)
- Executive implementation (the Council delivering it)

**The precedent:** Switzerland's Federal Council — seven members, multiple parties, collective decisions, no dominant Prime Minister. It has operated for over 170 years. It works.

**Britain Forward's pathway:**

- This Parliament: Citizens' Assembly on constitutional reform established; policy-based voting explored in depth; evidence gathered
- Next Parliament: Binding referendum on constitutional reforms proposed by the Assembly
- Long-term: Constitutional framework adapted based on what Britain votes for

This is a direction. The mechanism is too significant to impose — it must be voted on by the British public. We are honest about that.

## 1.7 PUBLIC BUDGET REFORM — END "USE IT OR LOSE IT"

**The Problem:**

One of the most wasteful structural features of British public finance is the "use it or lose it" budget system. Councils, schools, NHS trusts, and government departments that underspend their annual allocation risk receiving less funding the following year. The rational response: spend everything, whether or not it needs spending.

The result: a surge of unnecessary commissioning every February and March. Paths resurfaced that don't need resurfacing. Equipment ordered that nobody requested. Consultants hired for reports nobody will read. Purely to avoid losing next year's budget.

This costs hundreds of millions annually in unnecessary expenditure — and simultaneously creates a perverse disincentive against efficiency. An organisation that finds a clever way to do more with less is punished by having its budget cut.

**The Fixes — Applied to All Public Bodies:**

**1. Multi-Year Budgets**
All public bodies receive three-year rolling budget settlements — not annual. They can plan, contract, and commit over a genuine planning horizon rather than managing to a 12-month cycle. This alone eliminates much of the end-of-year panic.

**2. Carry-Forward Allowance**
Unspent funds up to 20% of the annual allocation can be carried forward to the following year. Organisations that genuinely don't need to spend everything this year can save it for a genuine need next year.

**3. Efficiency Dividend**
Where an organisation demonstrates genuine efficiency savings — doing the same or more with less — it retains 50% of the saving for its own future use. The other 50% returns to the central budget. This creates an active incentive for efficiency rather than a punishment for it.

**4. Outcome-Based Funding Review**
Budgets are set and reviewed based on demonstrated need and achieved outcomes — not solely on what was spent last year. An organisation that spent £10 million last year and achieved its objectives efficiently receives a budget based on what it needs, not on what it spent. An organisation that spent £10 million and achieved little faces a genuine review.

**5. Ring-Fenced Honest Underspend**
Organisations that report honest underspend — rather than rushing to spend — are explicitly protected from automatic budget cuts as a result. The protection is written into the funding framework. Honest financial management is rewarded, not penalised.

**Implementation:**
- Legislation in year one
- All public bodies — councils, schools, NHS trusts, government departments — on the new framework
- HM Treasury oversight reformed to support rather than punish multi-year planning
- Annual audit of end-of-year spending patterns — "use it or lose it" spending identified, reported, and addressed

**The Anti-Fragmentation Rule — Where This Policy Lives or Dies:**

**Legal definition of "common control" — treated as a single entity if ANY of the following apply:**

1. **Ownership threshold:** One person or entity holds more than 25% beneficial ownership in two or more companies
2. **Director control:** A director or officer holds executive authority in two or more companies

## 2.5 FUEL DUTY RELIEF — AUTOMATIC AND DEFINED

**Automatic Trigger — ALL conditions must be met simultaneously:**

**Condition 1 — Qualifying Crisis Event.** One of the following confirmed by independent assessment:
- Armed conflict directly affecting a major oil-producing region or supply route
- OPEC+ production cut of more than 10% of quota within a 90-day period
- Sanctions on a major producer affecting more than 5% of global supply
- Critical infrastructure failure affecting more than 5% of European refining or distribution capacity
- A declared energy emergency by the International Energy Agency

*This condition specifically excludes routine market fluctuation. Petrol being 6p more expensive than last week does not trigger this. War disrupting global supply does.*

**Condition 2 — Price Threshold.** Retail unleaded petrol exceeds the 36-month rolling average by more than 20%, sustained for at least 14 consecutive days.

**The Relief:** Fuel duty reduced by up to 50% of standard rate while both conditions are met. Scales with severity. Ends automatically when either condition is no longer met — assessed every 14 days.

## 3.3 ENERGY — BRITAIN POWERS BRITAIN

**The Honest Transition Sequence:**

Britain is committed to green energy. Britain is honest about timescales. Renewable capacity cannot replace all current demand immediately. During the transition, Britain maintains domestic fossil fuel production. This is not contradiction — it is honest sequencing. Germany's cautionary tale: premature nuclear phase-out, dependence on Russian gas, acute crisis. Britain will not repeat it.

**Renewables — expanded aggressively:**
- Solar: rooftops, not fields; mandatory on all new builds; retrofit programme
- Wind: onshore and offshore expansion
- Tidal and wave: world-class British resources — currently strategic negligence; exploitation begins
- Hydro: Scotland's river network; community micro-hydro — aggregate effect substantial
- Geothermal: Cornwall as expansion template

**Nuclear:** Stable baseload. Next-generation reactor investment.

**Transition Fuels — Milestone Phase-Out System:**

Each fossil fuel use has a defined phase-out trigger — achievement thresholds, not calendar deadlines:
- Coal power generation: phases out when green baseload can demonstrably cover peak demand for 30 consecutive days
- Gas for domestic heating: phases out sector by sector as heat pump penetration and grid capacity demonstrate readiness
- Coking coal for steel: phases out when green steel is commercially viable at scale in Britain

Milestones: public, independently monitored, reported to Parliament annually.

**Worker Transition:** Workers in oil, gas, coal not abandoned. Retraining funded and practical. Green energy jobs prioritised for former fossil fuel workers. Regional investment in fossil-fuel-dependent communities. No community left behind. Policy.

**Smart grid.** Excess household power bought back fairly. **District heating** in new cities and major urban areas.

## 3.5 THE NATIONAL HIGH-SPEED RAIL SPINE

**The Vision:**

A true national rail spine — not a London-to-somewhere line, but a Britain-to-Britain line. Running from Bournemouth in the south to Thurso in the far north, with branches serving every major region along the way.

**Why this framing matters:**

Every previous high-speed rail proposal has been conceived as a London-centric project: London to Birmingham, London to Manchester, London to Leeds. The result is always a line that primarily benefits London and its commuter zones. The Bournemouth-to-Thurso framing deliberately inverts this — the spine connects Britain end to end, with London as a stop along the way, not the destination everything radiates from.

**The Spine — Approximate Route:**

Bournemouth → Southampton → [London junction — existing Waterloo/St Pancras integration] → Milton Keynes → Birmingham → Manchester [northern junction] → Leeds [eastern junction] → Newcastle → Edinburgh → Inverness → Thurso

**The Branches:**

- Southwest: Plymouth and Cornwall branch from Southampton or Bristol junction
- Wales: Cardiff and Swansea branch from Birmingham or Bristol junction
- East: Norwich and East Anglia branch from London or Cambridge junction
- Yorkshire and Humber: Hull and York from Leeds junction
- Northeast: Sunderland and Middlesbrough from Newcastle junction
- Northeast Scotland: Aberdeen and Dundee from Edinburgh junction
- Northwest Scotland: Fort William from Inverness junction

**Why Thurso:**

Thurso already has a railway — the Far North Line from Inverness. North of Inverness, the requirement is speed upgrade and reliability improvement rather than entirely new track. The symbolism of connecting the far north to the rest of Britain matters: the communities that have been most disconnected from national infrastructure investment are explicitly included in the national vision.

**The Engineering Principles — HS2's Lessons Applied:**

HS2 failed because:
- Scope expanded repeatedly under political pressure
- Budget was set politically rather than engineered
- Routes were decided by lobbying, not by economic analysis
- Overruns were absorbed rather than addressed

Britain Forward's programme:
- Independent delivery body — insulated from ministerial interference
- Engineering decisions made by engineers
- Routes determined by independent economic benefit analysis — not political geography or lobbying
- Transparent budget published before a single shovel breaks ground, with genuine contingency built in
- Overruns trigger independent review and public reporting — not quiet budget increases
- This is a multi-Parliament programme. It will not be cancelled when inconvenient.

**The sequencing:**

- First Parliament: commission established; evidence published; routes confirmed by engineering analysis; detailed design begun; Parliamentary approval sought for construction funding
- Second Parliament: construction begins on highest-benefit sections
- Long-term: full spine operational

## 3.9 INTERNATIONAL GREEN PRESSURE

**Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM):**

Imports from countries not meeting agreed minimum carbon standards face a border levy proportionate to the carbon cost of production not priced into the import. WTO compliant. EU has already implemented this — Britain follows the same framework and adds diplomatic pressure through trade agreements.

**Effect:** British green producers not disadvantaged. Foreign producers face financial incentive to decarbonise.

**Trade Agreements:** All new bilateral agreements include binding green standards — not aspirational language.

**International Carbon Accountability Report:** Published annually. Named publicly. Countries backing out of commitments face: public naming, CBAM at full rate, reduced partnership investment priority.

Agreements mean something. Britain acts like they do.

## 6.1 DEFENCE

**Britain WILL:** Honour Article 5 — full stop; maintain strong capable military; invest in cyber defence; support domestic defence industry; support veterans.

**Britain WILL NOT:** Participate in offensive operations outside Article 5 without Parliamentary debate, legal basis, and genuine British security interest; be pressured into commitments serving other nations' agendas.

## 6.2 IMMIGRATION — POINTS-BASED, SKILLS-FIRST

**The Principle:** Britain is open to people willing and able to contribute. Britain manages immigration in the interest of existing residents and newcomers alike.

**Points-Based Assessment — All Economic Immigration:**

| Factor | Points Available |
|---|---|
| Skills and qualifications (shortage occupations) | Up to 30 |
| English language proficiency (tested) | Up to 20 |
| Age (working-age weighted) | Up to 15 |
| British employer sponsorship (confirmed job offer) | Up to 25 |
| Regional sponsorship (willing to work outside major cities) | Up to 10 |

Minimum threshold: 65 points.

**Visa Categories:**

*Skilled Worker:* Points threshold; employer sponsorship; shortage occupation or salary threshold.
*High-Value Investor/Entrepreneur:* Genuine high threshold.
*Family Reunion:* Spouse/partner and dependent children of settled residents only.
*Humanitarian Protection:* Separate from economic migration. Processed fairly, promptly, humanely.

*Temporary Sector Visas — The Honest Complexity:*
- Seasonal agriculture: capped, time-limited, no settlement route
- Social care: transitional visa while Britain invests in domestic training and pay; phases down as domestic workforce grows

Both explicitly time-limited and reviewed annually.

**Integration Requirements:** English language test; civic knowledge assessment before settlement; employment or genuine family dependency; no recourse to public funds during qualifying period.

**Enforcement:** Visa overstaying: serious enforcement priority; employer sanctions; civil penalty then removal. Serious crime by foreign nationals: deportation on completion of sentence.

**UBI and Voting:** After 5 years settled residency (2 years for high-skilled visa tier).

## 6.3 FOREIGN AID — REFORM, NOT SENTIMENT

Independent audit commissioned day one. Every programme reviewed.

*Three outcome categories:*
1. **Retain and expand:** Clear measurable outcomes, genuine recipient benefit — vaccines, emergency relief, demonstrated infrastructure impact
2. **Reform:** Genuine potential, poor implementation — results-based model with measurable outcomes required before continued funding
3. **Cut:** Cannot demonstrate recipient benefit; money demonstrably captured by corruption or administration; serves donor interests. Cut.

Britain is not obligated to fund corruption. We are obligated to help where help is effective. The audit determines which is which.

**Results-Based International Investment:** Trade, science, infrastructure partnerships. Measurable outcomes. Britain as a hub of excellence. Partnerships of equals.

**The EU Question:** Explicit neutrality. Constructive partnership on science, research, and trade. Any formal relationship change: public referendum.

# FISCAL FRAMEWORK — THE HONEST VERSION

*(Full detail: sub-document — Money)*

**Revenue spending** funded from tax revenues and nationalised asset revenues.
**Capital investment** financed through long-term bonds, repaid by the assets' own revenues.

*These are not the same thing. Treating infrastructure investment as current spending is how governments make bad decisions.*

**How we pay for it:**
1. Nationalised asset revenues — profits flowing abroad stay in Britain
2. Pharmaceutical savings
3. Stopped mismanagement — procurement reform, benefits administration replaced by UBI efficiency, anti-avoidance enforcement
4. Road pricing — replaces fuel duty
5. UBI tax recoupture — higher earners return UBI through income tax
6. Foreign aid reform — ineffective programmes redirected
7. Budget reform savings — "use it or lose it" waste eliminated
8. Long-term economic growth from investment in people and infrastructure
9. Long-term bond financing for capital investment

Full independent fiscal assessment before any general election. Both technical and plain English formats. We show our working. Always.
