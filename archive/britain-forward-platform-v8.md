## 1.2 ELECTORAL REFORM — RANKED WEIGHTED VOTING

*(Full detail: sub-document — Voting System Overhaul)*

**Ranked Weighted Voting replaces First Past the Post.** Voters rank candidates in preference order. Points awarded by ranking. Winners must command broad support. No wasted votes. No tactical voting. Every constituency matters.

**Compulsory Participation — Australian Model.** Every eligible citizen participates. Spoiled ballots and NOTA are valid. HRA compatibility assessment published before legislation. Incentive-based alternative prepared if compulsion is found unlawful.

**None of the Above on every ballot.** Formally counted, published, acted on. NOTA winning outright triggers fresh election within 60 days — original candidates ineligible. Caretaker from National Oversight Council serves until completion.

**Coalition Red Lines — stated before any election:**
Britain Forward will not coalition with any party that opposes ranked weighted voting, reverses public ownership of core infrastructure, or abandons ministerial competence requirements. Everything else is negotiable. These three are not.

## 1.3 POLICY-BASED VOTING — THE LONG-TERM DIRECTION

*Aspirational. Genuinely complex. Included honestly.*

Most voters want policies from multiple parties — not one party's entire package. The current system forces all-or-nothing choices between packages most voters wouldn't design themselves.

**The Idea:** Citizens vote on individual policies, not just parties. Switzerland demonstrates this is workable through frequent binding referenda on specific policy questions.

**The Constitutional Problem:** A partisan Prime Minister implementing their own policies regardless of cross-party mandates defeats the purpose.

**The Solution — Switzerland's Federal Council Model:**

A **National Implementation Council** — five to seven senior figures drawn proportionally from parties per their electoral share, confirmed by Parliament through a cross-party vote, serving fixed terms not tied to electoral cycles.

The Council implements policy. Parliament debates and legislates. Citizens vote directly on major questions through binding referenda. The Monarch remains as constitutional custodian of last resort.

This separates three functions currently collapsed into one: democratic mandate (what people want), legislative process (how it's refined), and executive implementation (how it's delivered).

**Britain Forward's pathway:**
- This Parliament: Citizens' Assembly on constitutional reform established; policy-based voting explored, researched, and published
- Next Parliament: Binding referendum on Assembly proposals
- Long-term: Constitutional framework adapted based on what Britain votes for

This is a direction — significant enough to require a public vote, not a government imposition.

## 1.4 CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

**The Monarchy — Constitutional Custodian of Last Resort.**
Sovereignty resides in the British people. The Monarch is a non-political safeguard — acts only in genuine constitutional crisis to restore conditions for popular sovereignty. Ceremonial in normal times. Real in extremis.

**The House of Lords — Public Decision.**
Current form: indefensible. Function: valuable. Three reform options published; public consultation; binding referendum. The public decides. Options: elected Senate; hybrid chamber (partially elected, partially independently expert-appointed); full abolition with functions to National Oversight Council.

**National Oversight Council.** Mixed composition — elected members plus independently appointed experts. No government appointments, ever. Power to delay and refer legislation. Integrated with judicial review.

**Parliament — London Now, Direction Set.** Government departments: phased regional relocation begins immediately. Independent commission assesses Parliament's future location — reports within five years. Public referendum decides.

## 1.5 POLITICAL ACCOUNTABILITY

**Conflict of Interest — The Precise Standard.**

*The test:* Did this company materially benefit from a specific decision this MP made or influenced?

- No involvement with such companies in the previous **5 years** before taking office
- No involvement while **serving**
- No involvement for **10 years after** leaving office
- Where demonstrable benefit flows to an MP through a family connection: same standard applies

Sector expertise: encouraged. Personal financial benefit from specific decisions: prohibited.

**MP Pay — Public Service Rate.**
- National median wage with London cost-of-living adjustment only
- Zero outside income while serving
- Genuine work accommodation expenses only — not second property purchase
- All expenses: publicly searchable, audited quarterly, personal costs never eligible
- Pay rises: same process as public sector — not self-determined

## 1.7 PUBLIC BUDGET REFORM — END "USE IT OR LOSE IT"

**The Problem:**

Public bodies that underspend their annual allocation risk receiving less next year. The rational response: spend everything, whether or not it needs spending. The result: paths resurfaced that don't need it; equipment ordered nobody requested; consultants hired for reports nobody will read. Purely to protect next year's budget.

This is a structural incentive toward waste — and a structural disincentive against efficiency. An organisation that finds a clever way to do more with less is punished by having its budget cut.

**The Five Fixes — Applied to All Public Bodies:**

1. **Multi-Year Budgets:** Three-year rolling settlements — not annual. Plan, contract, and commit over a genuine horizon.

2. **Carry-Forward Allowance:** Unspent funds up to 20% of annual allocation can roll forward. Save this year for a genuine need next year.

3. **Efficiency Dividend:** Where an organisation demonstrates genuine efficiency savings, it retains 50% of the saving for its own future use. The other 50% returns to central budget. Active incentive for efficiency, not punishment for it.

4. **Outcome-Based Funding Review:** Budgets set and reviewed based on demonstrated need and achieved outcomes — not solely on what was spent last year.

5. **Ring-Fenced Honest Underspend:** Organisations that report honest underspend are explicitly protected from automatic budget cuts as a result. Written into the funding framework.

## 2.3 SMALL BUSINESS — BRITAIN'S BEST PLACE TO START

**The Vision:**

Britain becomes the best place in the world to start and grow a small business. Near-zero tax rates for genuine small enterprises. Maximum simplicity of administration. Active support for growth.

This is not charity toward small businesses. It is strategic recognition that small businesses:
- Employ the majority of British workers
- Drive regional economic diversity and anchor local high streets
- Provide the seedbed from which larger enterprises grow
- Are currently taxed and regulated in ways designed for large organisations

**Small Business Tax Rates — The Lowest in the World for Genuine Small Enterprises:**

| Annual Qualifying Revenue | Corporation Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Under £100,000 | 0% — zero |
| £100,000 — £500,000 | Minimal rate (target: 5%) |
| £500,000 — £5 million | Intermediate rate |
| £5 million — £50 million | Standard rate |
| Over £50 million | Full rate — no reduction |

*Precise rates confirmed by independent economic modelling — the structure is fixed, the rates follow evidence on optimal growth incentive vs revenue impact.*

**Britain as a Small Business Haven — The International Dimension:**

With near-zero tax rates for genuine small businesses, Britain actively attracts entrepreneurs from anywhere in the world to register, operate, and grow in Britain. This is the inverse of Ireland's large-business low-tax model — Britain becomes the destination for people starting something, not for multinationals restructuring to avoid tax.

*The effect:* entrepreneurship, innovation, and small business formation flow into Britain. Employment grows. Economic activity diversifies regionally. As those businesses grow through the tiers, they eventually pay full rates — by which point they are contributing jobs, supply chains, and economic activity that justify the earlier tax relief.

**The Anti-Fragmentation Rule — Where This Policy Lives or Dies:**

Without rigorous anti-fragmentation, large businesses simply restructure into twelve artificial small companies. The safeguards are built into the primary legislation.

**Legal definition of "common control" — treated as a single entity if ANY of the following apply:**

1. **Ownership threshold:** One person or entity holds more than 25% beneficial ownership in two or more companies — assessed together for tax purposes
2. **Director control:** A director or officer holds executive authority in two or more companies — flagged for assessment
3. **Operational integration:** Two or more companies share premises, branding, supply chains, customer base, or key personnel above a defined threshold — HMRC assesses whether genuine operational independence exists
4. **Non-arm's-length financial flows:** Transactions between companies at demonstrably artificial rates — assessed as a single entity
5. **Post-legislation division:** A company that was a single entity before this legislation and divided after — presumed to remain a single entity for 10 years unless genuine operational independence is demonstrated to HMRC's satisfaction

The burden of proof is on the business to demonstrate genuine operational independence.

*Penalty for deliberate fragmentation:* Full back-tax at higher rate plus 100% penalty plus interest. Criminal referral for systematic schemes.

**Additional Small Business Measures:**
- Simplified single reporting framework for businesses under £500,000 revenue — one return, one contact, one set of rules
- Business rates: substantial relief for small high-street businesses
- Late payment: large companies paying small suppliers late face escalating penalty interest; expedited dispute resolution for small businesses
- Public procurement: contracts below a defined threshold reserved for British small businesses — cannot be bundled into large contracts only large businesses can bid for
- Start-up support: funded business advice, mentoring, and incubator access for all new businesses in their first two years

## 2.5 FUEL DUTY RELIEF — AUTOMATIC AND DEFINED

**Automatic Trigger — ALL conditions must be met simultaneously:**

**Condition 1 — Qualifying Crisis Event.** One of the following confirmed by independent assessment:
- Armed conflict directly affecting a major oil-producing region or supply route
- OPEC+ production cut of more than 10% of quota within a 90-day period
- Sanctions on a major producer affecting more than 5% of global supply
- Critical infrastructure failure affecting more than 5% of European refining or distribution capacity
- A declared energy emergency by the International Energy Agency

*Petrol being 6p more expensive than last week does not trigger this. War disrupting global supply does.*

**Condition 2 — Price Threshold.** Retail unleaded petrol exceeds the 36-month rolling average by more than 20%, sustained for at least 14 consecutive days.

**The Relief:** Fuel duty reduced by up to 50% of standard rate while both conditions are met. Scales with severity. Ends automatically when either condition is no longer met — assessed every 14 days. No ministerial announcement required. No political decision. The mechanism does it.

## 3.3 PUBLIC TRANSPORT — FREE OR HEAVILY SUBSIDISED

*Model: Luxembourg (free buses since 2020); integrated with Dutch cycling infrastructure*

**The Principle:**

A bus carrying 50 people does less road damage, produces less pollution, and uses less road space than 50 cars. The bus is better for everyone — including the drivers stuck in less traffic behind it. Subsidising the bus benefits the whole country, not just the passengers.

Public transport is not a service for people who can't afford cars. It is infrastructure for a functioning society.

**Buses — Restored, Free at Point of Use:**

Bus networks renationalised or brought under municipal control — operated as public services, not commercial enterprises:
- Urban and suburban buses: free at point of use
- Rural buses: free at point of use — these serve people with no viable alternative
- Frequency: minimum service standards for all routes serving inhabited areas — buses every 30 minutes during peak hours is a baseline
- Night services: restored in towns and cities — night economy workers need to get home
- Real-time information at all stops — not just major interchange points

**Trams and Light Rail:**

- Tram networks extended in cities with existing systems: Manchester, Edinburgh, Nottingham, Sheffield
- New light rail assessed for cities with sufficient density: Leeds, Bristol, and others
- Capital costs: long-term bonds, self-funding from reduced road and congestion costs
- Free or heavily subsidised fares

**Urban Commuter Rail:**

- Fares capped and subsidised within major city regions
- Integration with bus and tram networks — one ticket, one network
- Oyster-style integrated ticketing extended to all major city regions, not just London

**How the Subsidy Is Funded:**

Not a cost — an investment with measurable returns:
- Reduced road maintenance (buses damage roads less than equivalent car journeys)
- Reduced congestion costs (economic cost of British urban congestion estimated in billions annually)
- Reduced air pollution NHS costs
- Increased urban productivity (people on public transport can work; people in traffic cannot)
- Reduced carbon emissions

Revenue from: road pricing (hypothecated to transport), reduced car infrastructure requirement, general taxation justified by economic returns.

## 3.4 WALKABLE CITIES
*Design principle adapted from Copenhagen, Amsterdam, and Vienna*

**The 15-Minute City Principle:**

The most liveable cities share a characteristic: most daily needs are accessible within 15 minutes on foot. Schools, shops, healthcare, parks, work, leisure — arranged so that a car is optional, not essential.

Britain Forward adopts this as the design standard for all new development and urban renewal.

**New Cities and New Developments — Designed for Walking:**

- Maximum distances: primary school (10-minute walk), GP surgery (10-minute walk), green space (5-minute walk), shops (10-minute walk)
- Street design prioritises pedestrian movement — not car throughput
- Ground floor uses: shops, cafes, services — not car parks or blank walls
- Continuous, well-lit, accessible pavements — no gaps, no barriers
- Street furniture that serves pedestrians: benches, shelters, public toilets

**Existing City Centres — Progressive Pedestrianisation:**

- Town and city centre pedestrianisation expanded — more streets permanently car-free
- Pavement quality: national minimum standard; uneven, blocked pavements are not acceptable
- Crossings: sufficient, frequent, timed for pedestrians not cars
- Pavement parking: enforcement substantially strengthened — pavements are for pedestrians, not car storage

**Accessibility — Walking for Everyone:**

Walkability only works if everyone can walk safely:
- Wheelchair and mobility scooter accessibility throughout pedestrian networks
- Tactile paving and audible signals for people with visual impairments — maintained to standard
- Dropped kerbs at all crossings
- Shared space (where pedestrians and cars mix without kerbs): not the default; evidence on visual impairment clearly supports maintaining kerb separation

**Night-Time Walkability:**

- Adequate functional street lighting
- Active frontages on key pedestrian routes at night
- Safe crossing points available at night, not just daytime
- Connects to night bus policy — you can only walk home if it's safe to do so

**The Health Case:**

Walkable cities produce measurably healthier populations. Incidental exercise through walking for transport reduces obesity, cardiovascular disease, and mental health conditions. The NHS savings are documented and substantial. Walkability is health policy.

## 3.5 BIKEABLE CITIES
*Model: Netherlands — physical separation, not painted lines*

**Why People Don't Cycle in Britain:**

Not laziness. Fear. Cycling on British roads — mixed with fast-moving traffic in narrow lanes with poor driver behaviour and no physical protection — is genuinely dangerous. People who would happily cycle on a protected lane will not cycle on a busy A-road with lorries passing at 40mph. Surveys consistently show large numbers of people would cycle more if they felt safe.

The solution is physical separation, not appeals to drivers to be nicer.

**Protected Cycle Lanes — Not Painted Lines:**

- Dedicated, physically separated cycle lanes on all urban arterial roads — separated by raised kerb, planters, or parking buffer, not paint
- Painted lines are not cycle infrastructure; they are a gesture toward it
- Minimum lane width: 1.5 metres each direction — wide enough for cargo bikes and panniers
- Continuous routes — a lane that disappears at every junction is not a lane

**Safe Junctions — The Dutch Solution:**

Junctions are where cyclists are most at risk. Separate cycling signal phases — cyclists have their own green phase before or after vehicle traffic, eliminating the most common collision type (vehicle turning across cyclist):
- Protected junction design at all major urban junctions with cycle infrastructure
- Cycle-specific signals with separate phase
- Advanced stop lines at all other junctions — cyclists wait in front of traffic, not alongside it

**Cycle Parking and Infrastructure:**

- Secure, covered cycle parking at all public transport stops, stations, and major public buildings
- Cycle parking at workplaces: building regulations updated to require provision proportionate to occupancy
- Cargo bike parking and charging at key urban locations

**Bike-Share Schemes:**

- Publicly funded or subsidised bike-share in all cities above a defined population threshold
- E-bikes included — extending viable distance and making cycling accessible to more people
- Integration with public transport accounts — one payment for bike and bus

**E-Bike Subsidy:**

- Subsidised e-bike purchase for households below a defined income threshold — modelled on successful French and German schemes
- E-bikes make cycling viable for: commutes above 5 miles, hilly terrain, people returning to cycling after injury or age-related fitness change, people carrying children or cargo

**Rural Cycling:**

- Rural road speed limit review: 60mph inappropriate for many narrow rural roads — evidence-based review of appropriate limits where cyclists and cars share space
- Signage: rural cycle route signage improved and standardised
- Off-road paths alongside major rural A-roads where traffic volume and speed make on-road cycling dangerous

**The Combined Case:**

- Health: cycling commuters have substantially better health outcomes than car commuters
- Environment: cycling is zero-emission for the journey; significant if replacing car trips
- Economic: cycling infrastructure is dramatically cheaper per person moved than road or rail; cyclists demonstrably spend more in local shops than car drivers
- Road capacity: a cycle lane replacing a car lane carries more people per hour than the car lane did

## 3.6 ENERGY — BRITAIN POWERS BRITAIN

**The Honest Transition Sequence:**

Britain is committed to green energy. Britain is also honest about timescales. Renewable capacity cannot replace all current demand immediately. During the transition, Britain maintains domestic fossil fuel production. Germany's cautionary tale: premature nuclear phase-out, dependence on Russian gas, acute crisis. Britain will not repeat it.

**Renewables — Expanded Aggressively:**
- Solar: rooftops, not fields; mandatory on all new builds; retrofit programme
- Wind: onshore and offshore expansion
- Tidal and wave: world-class British resources — currently strategic negligence; exploitation begins
- Hydro: Scotland's river network; community micro-hydro — aggregate effect substantial
- Geothermal: Cornwall's deep geothermal project as expansion template

**Nuclear:** Stable baseload. Next-generation reactor investment.

**Transition Fuels — Milestone Phase-Out System:**

Each fossil fuel use has a defined phase-out trigger — achievement thresholds, not calendar deadlines:
- Coal power generation: phases out when green baseload can demonstrably cover peak demand for 30 consecutive days
- Gas for domestic heating: phases out sector by sector as heat pump penetration and grid capacity demonstrate readiness
- Coking coal for steel: phases out when green steel is commercially viable at scale in Britain

Milestones: public, independently monitored, reported to Parliament annually.

**Worker Transition — Explicit Commitment:** Workers in oil, gas, coal not abandoned. Retraining funded and practical. Green energy jobs prioritised for former fossil fuel workers. No community left behind. Policy.

**Smart grid.** Excess household power bought back fairly. **District heating** in new cities and major urban areas.

*Energy:* Grid extension funded nationally. Community energy schemes funded. Insulation programme to heat-pump-readiness before transition required. Heating oil phase-out with notice, funded alternatives, and extended timelines where no viable alternative yet exists.

*Transport:* Rural bus network restored — minimum service standards. Demand-responsive transport for sparse areas. Branch line restoration assessed — communities isolated since Beeching cuts reviewed for reconnection. Rural EV charging: installed as public infrastructure where commercial installation is not viable.

*Digital:* Gigabit broadband: universal, including all rural areas — publicly funded where commercially unviable. Satellite broadband as interim for the most remote.

*Healthcare and services:* Mobile healthcare services for communities below viable fixed-service threshold. Telemedicine investment. Rural pharmacies and post offices: supported and protected.

## 3.8 THE NATIONAL HIGH-SPEED RAIL SPINE

**The Vision: Bournemouth to Thurso**

Not a London-to-somewhere line. A Britain-to-Britain line.

Running from Bournemouth in the south to Thurso in the far north — with London as a stop along the way, not the destination everything radiates from.

**The Spine:**
Bournemouth → Southampton → London junction → Milton Keynes → Birmingham → Manchester → Leeds → Newcastle → Edinburgh → Inverness → Thurso

**The Branches:**
- Southwest: Plymouth and Cornwall from Southampton/Bristol junction
- Wales: Cardiff and Swansea from Birmingham/Bristol junction
- East: Norwich and East Anglia from London/Cambridge junction
- Yorkshire: Hull and York from Leeds junction
- Northeast: Sunderland and Middlesbrough from Newcastle junction
- Northeast Scotland: Aberdeen and Dundee from Edinburgh junction
- Northwest Scotland: Fort William from Inverness junction

**Why Thurso:** Already has a railway — the Far North Line from Inverness. North of Inverness is speed upgrade and reliability improvement, not entirely new track. The communities most disconnected from national infrastructure are explicitly included.

**The Engineering Principles — HS2's Lessons Applied:**

HS2 failed: scope expanded under political pressure; budget set politically not engineered; routes decided by lobbying; overruns absorbed quietly.

Britain Forward: independent delivery body insulated from ministerial interference; engineering decisions by engineers; routes by independent economic benefit analysis; transparent budget with genuine contingency published before construction approved; overruns trigger public reporting, not quiet increases; multi-Parliament direction — will not be cancelled when inconvenient.

## 4.3 HOMELESSNESS — HOUSING FIRST

*Model: Finland (near-elimination of rough sleeping since 2008); Canada (Housing First evidence base)*

**The Problem:**

Britain has thousands of people sleeping rough on any given night, tens of thousands in temporary accommodation, and hundreds of thousands in housing insecurity. The current approach — requiring people to achieve sobriety, mental health stability, and employment before accessing stable housing — gets the sequence wrong.

**The Evidence:**

Finland adopted the Housing First model in 2008. Rough sleeping in Finland is now near-zero. The model: give people a stable home first, unconditionally, then provide the support they need. Sobriety is not a prerequisite. Mental health treatment is not a prerequisite. You cannot address addiction and mental health from a park bench. You can address them from a stable home.

Canada's evidence base confirms: Housing First is more effective and cheaper than the alternative. Stable housing reduces NHS emergency presentations, reduces criminal justice costs, and enables people to begin the other work of rebuilding their lives.

**The Policy:**

*Emergency — this Parliament:*
- Housing First programme established nationally — stable housing offered unconditionally to all rough sleepers
- Emergency hostel capacity expanded immediately — interim safe accommodation while Housing First placements are made
- Rough sleeping outreach teams funded and operational in all local authorities with significant rough sleeping
- No one turned away from emergency accommodation because of addiction or mental health status — these are reasons to provide support, not grounds for exclusion

*Support Alongside Housing:*
- Mental health support: every Housing First placement has access to ongoing mental health support — not a referral to a waiting list
- Addiction support: available, voluntary, and non-coercive — people engage better with support when they're not fighting for survival
- Employment support: practical, individually tailored, and patient — the goal is stability first, employment when ready
- Benefits navigation: Housing First key workers help residents access all entitlements they are owed — including UBI when it launches

*Medium Term:*
- Social housing supply (100,000 new homes) provides the permanent placements as the programme scales
- Preventative focus: people at risk of homelessness identified and supported before they lose their home — not after
- Prison release: no one released from prison into homelessness — housing plan in place before release date

*The Target:*
Rough sleeping eliminated within one Parliament. No one in Britain sleeping outside for want of a home.

## 6.2 IMMIGRATION — POINTS-BASED, SKILLS-FIRST

*Model: Australia — adapted for British conditions and honest about where sector-specific exceptions are needed*

**The Principle:** Britain is open to people willing and able to contribute. Britain manages immigration in the interest of existing residents and newcomers alike.

**Points-Based Assessment — All Economic Immigration:**

| Factor | Points Available |
|---|---|
| Skills and qualifications (shortage occupations) | Up to 30 |
| English language proficiency (tested) | Up to 20 |
| Age (working-age weighted) | Up to 15 |
| British employer sponsorship (confirmed job offer) | Up to 25 |
| Regional sponsorship (willing to work outside major cities) | Up to 10 |

Minimum threshold to qualify: 65 points.

**Visa Categories:**

*Skilled Worker:* Points threshold; employer sponsorship; shortage occupation or salary threshold.
*High-Value Investor/Entrepreneur:* Genuine high threshold — not residency-for-investment.
*Family Reunion:* Spouse/partner and dependent children of settled residents.
*Humanitarian Protection:* Separate from economic migration. Processed fairly, promptly, humanely.

*Temporary Sector Visas — The Honest Complexity:*
Some sectors genuinely require workers Britain cannot currently supply domestically:
- Seasonal agriculture: capped, time-limited, no settlement route; workers must leave at visa expiry
- Social care: transitional visa while Britain invests in domestic training and pay; phases down as domestic workforce grows

Both reviewed annually and explicitly time-limited.

**Integration Requirements:** English language test; civic knowledge assessment before settlement; employment or genuine family dependency; no recourse to public funds during qualifying period.

**Enforcement:** Overstaying: serious enforcement priority; employer sanctions; civil penalty then removal. Serious crime: deportation on sentence completion.

**UBI and Voting:** After 5 years settled residency (2 years for high-skilled visa tier).

## 6.3 FOREIGN AID — REFORM, NOT SENTIMENT

Independent audit commissioned day one. Every programme reviewed against three outcome categories:

1. **Retain and expand:** Clear measurable outcomes, genuine recipient benefit — vaccines, emergency relief, demonstrated infrastructure impact
2. **Reform:** Genuine potential, poor implementation — results-based model with measurable outcomes required before continued funding
3. **Cut:** Cannot demonstrate recipient benefit; money captured by corruption or administration; serves donor rather than recipient interests

Britain is not obligated to fund corruption. We are obligated to help where help is effective. The audit determines which is which. Total budget may decrease substantially based on evidence.

**Results-Based International Investment:** Trade, science, infrastructure partnerships with measurable outcomes. Britain as a hub of excellence. Partnerships of equals — not post-imperial paternalism.

**The EU Question:** Explicit neutrality. Constructive partnership on science, research, education, and trade where agreements serve Britain. Any formal relationship change: public referendum.

# FISCAL FRAMEWORK — THE HONEST VERSION

*(Full detail: sub-document — Money)*

**Revenue spending** (NHS, UBI, welfare, education, public services): funded from tax revenues and nationalised asset revenues.

**Capital investment** (roads, rail, cities, water, energy): financed through long-term bonds, repaid by the assets' own revenues. Investments, not spending.

*These are not the same thing. Treating infrastructure investment as current spending is how governments make bad decisions.*

**How we pay for it:**
1. Nationalised asset revenues — profits flowing abroad stay in Britain
2. Pharmaceutical savings
3. Stopped mismanagement — procurement reform, benefits administration replaced by UBI efficiency
4. Road pricing — replaces fuel duty fairly
5. UBI tax recoupture — higher earners return UBI through income tax
6. Foreign aid reform — ineffective programmes redirected
7. Budget reform savings — "use it or lose it" waste eliminated
8. Long-term economic growth from investment in people and infrastructure
9. Long-term bond financing for capital investment, self-funded by asset revenues
10. Small business growth — more businesses, more employment, more income tax as they grow

Full independent fiscal assessment — technical AND plain English — before any general election. We show our working. Always. To everyone.
