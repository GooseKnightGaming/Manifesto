# A NOTE ON WHERE WE STAND

Britain Forward acknowledges something most parties won't:

Our policies draw from multiple traditions. Some look left. Some look right. Most look like neither, because they're drawn from evidence rather than ideology.

Public ownership of national resources — water, energy, oil — is associated with the left. It is also simply *reasonable*: these are the nation's assets and they should serve the nation.

Strong national defence, controlled borders, and support for British business and innovation are associated with the right. They are also simply *reasonable*: a country must be secure and capable.

Universal basic services — free school meals, public facilities, healthcare — are socialist in character. They are also simply *good value*: a population that is fed, healthy, and housed costs far less in crisis management than one that isn't.

We take what works. We leave what doesn't. We are honest about where ideas come from and why we've chosen them.

**Evidence, not tribe. Always.**

This platform leans, at present, toward investing in people and systems. We acknowledge that. We believe the evidence supports it. We will always show our working.

# THE LEADER QUESTION — ADDRESSED DIRECTLY

Britain Forward requires ministers to have relevant expertise in their departments. Critics will ask: does the party leader have relevant expertise?

Yes — because the relevant expertise for leading a political party and a country is *different* from the expertise required to run a government department.

Leading Britain requires: the ability to build and hold a coalition of people, to communicate complex ideas plainly, to make decisions under pressure, to hold a long-term vision against short-term noise, and to be publicly accountable for outcomes.

These are skills found in people who have led large, complex organisations through genuine change — not necessarily in Parliament.

Britain Forward will be led by someone who has *done things* — built things, reformed things, delivered things at scale. Not a career politician who has navigated Westminster. Not a political veteran from another party carrying that party's baggage and ideological label.

The leader of Britain Forward has not come from left or right. They have come from *the country*.

**The five qualities required of our leader:**
1. Demonstrable record of delivering complex change at scale
2. No prior affiliation with a major party — genuinely new
3. Origin outside London and the political establishment
4. Ability to speak plainly to ordinary people without condescension
5. Willingness to be publicly held to account — including by their own party

*The specific individual will be determined by member selection. This document defines what we are looking for, not who it is.*

## 1.1 MINISTERIAL COMPETENCE

Ministers will have demonstrable expertise or professional experience relevant to their department.

A Health Secretary will have worked in healthcare. A Chancellor will have worked in economics. An Education Secretary will have worked in education.

This is not elitism. A surgeon performs your operation. A structural engineer designs your bridge. The people running Britain's critical systems must understand those systems.

No learning on the job at the nation's expense.

## 1.2 ELECTORAL REFORM

**Replace First Past the Post with Ranked Weighted Voting.**

Voters rank candidates in preference order. Points awarded by ranking. The winner must have broad support — not just an intense minority.

This means:
- Votes in every constituency count, not just marginals
- Extreme polarisation is reduced — candidates need appeal beyond their core
- Tactical voting is no longer necessary
- Cooperation is incentivised

**Compulsory Voting — The Australian Model.**

Every eligible citizen must vote. Spoiled ballots and None of the Above are valid choices — but opting out entirely is not.

Why: A government elected by 35% of those who bother to vote does not represent Britain. If your vote is compulsory, every candidate must try to earn it.

NOTA votes are counted, published, and taken seriously. A high NOTA vote is a formal signal of public dissatisfaction — not an invisible protest.

**Coalitions where no majority exists** — built into the system as normal, not exceptional. Britain Forward will state its coalition red lines explicitly before any election:

*We will not enter coalition with any party that opposes electoral reform, seeks to reverse public ownership of core infrastructure, or abandons the ministerial competence requirement. Everything else is negotiable.*

## 1.3 CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

**The Monarchy — Ultimate Seat of Sovereign Authority**

The Monarch remains as the ultimate constitutional backstop — the final sovereign authority in genuine constitutional crisis. Mostly ceremonial. Largely silent. But present, and real.

This is not sentiment. It is stability. A constitutional anchor that sits above political contestation, available in extremis, unused in normal times.

The Monarchy's role: to ensure no single political actor can capture the entire constitutional apparatus. They reign. They do not govern.

**The House of Lords — Reform, Not Necessarily Abolition**

The Lords' current form is indefensible: unelected, unaccountable, stuffed with political appointees and hereditary privilege.

But its *function* — scrutiny, revision, the long view — is valuable. The question is not whether we need scrutiny. We do. The question is who should provide it and how they should be chosen.

Options under active consideration:
- Full replacement with an elected Senate with fixed terms and regional representation
- Hybrid chamber: partially elected, partially expert-appointed through independent process
- Full abolition with functions transferred to a new National Oversight Council

*This will be subject to public consultation and a binding referendum. Britain Forward will not impose a constitutional settlement. We will propose one and let Britain decide.*

**National Oversight Council**

Established in parallel with Lords reform — a scrutiny body with:
- Mixed composition: elected members, independently appointed experts (judiciary, science, ethics, civic society)
- Power to delay and refer legislation — not to block permanently
- Explicit, publicly defined selection process — no government appointments
- Integrated with judicial review

**Parliament — London, For Now**

Parliament stays in London for the current term. However:
- Government departments begin phased relocation to regional cities
- A formal commission established to assess and plan full or partial Parliament relocation within the next decade
- Regional parliamentary offices opened in major cities — genuine presence, not symbolic

The direction is set. The timeline is honest.

## 2.1 THE SOVEREIGN WEALTH FUND — HOW IT'S ACTUALLY FUNDED

Norway has the world's largest sovereign wealth fund. France controls its energy nationally. Britain had equivalent resources and gave the revenue away.

We will not repeat that mistake.

**The fund is built from:**

**Nationalised asset revenues** — water, energy, and oil brought under public ownership generate returns currently flowing to private shareholders. Those returns go to the fund instead.

**Stopping mismanagement** — a significant portion of the fund comes from *not wasting money*:
- Ending MPs' unjustifiable expense claims
- Ending political donor contracts and procurement corruption
- Reforming welfare systems that cost more to administer than they save
- Pharmaceutical price negotiation — we currently overpay for drugs developed partly with public research money
- Whole-life infrastructure costing — building roads once, properly, rather than resurfacing them repeatedly

**British Oil and Gas** — domestic production for domestic use first. No more paying foreign prices for British resources. Surplus sold internationally at market rate. Revenue to the fund.

**The honest position on scale:** We will not promise Norway's fund in five years. Britain's North Sea is past peak production. We are promising Norway's *model* — starting now, building over decades. Every year we fail to start is a year of compounded loss.

**The fund invests in:** British infrastructure first. Surplus invested globally. Returns reinvested in public services and future generations.

---

## 2.2 NATIONALISATION — THE HONEST CASE

Water, energy networks, and a meaningful public stake in British oil.

**Why:**
- These are natural monopolies. Competition doesn't work in pipes and wires — you can't choose whose pipe your water comes through.
- They are essential to life. Profit extraction from essential services is a transfer from citizens to shareholders.
- Every comparable successful economy maintains public ownership or strong public control of core infrastructure. Britain is the outlier.

**How — not the 1970s:**
- Public ownership of the *network* — the pipes, wires, and infrastructure
- Competitive private and cooperative layers operating *within* that network
- Not Soviet-style state operation. A modern hybrid serving the public.

**Self-sufficiency as the goal:**
- Britain produces its own energy — no more dependency on foreign supply or price shocks
- Britain controls its own water — no more leaking 3 billion litres a day while shareholders profit
- Britain uses its own oil first — surplus sold internationally
- Other countries are needed for luxuries and specialisms, not essentials

# PILLAR THREE: NATIONAL INFRASTRUCTURE
## Roads, Cities, Energy & Connectivity

*Build it properly. Once. Right.*

## 3.1 ROADS — REBUILT TO LAST

British roads are resurfaced constantly because cheap materials degrade quickly under British weather. We pay again and again for the same roads.

**The policy:**
- Long-life composite surfaces — built to last, not to be resurfaced
- Whole-life costing in all road contracts — the cheapest road upfront is not the cheapest road overall
- Road-embedded EV charging — inductive coils embedded during rebuild; vehicles charge while moving; solves the commercial EV payload problem (a laden electric van currently manages ~50 miles; road charging removes that constraint entirely)
- Road hierarchies redesigned for modern freight, cycling, and future autonomous vehicles
- Green corridors alongside all new roads — wildlife routes built into engineering design

**Usage-based road pricing:**
- Replaces fuel duty as EVs eliminate pump revenue
- Fair — you pay for what you use
- Revenue hypothecated to road maintenance and public transport

---

## 3.2 PUBLIC DIGNITY INFRASTRUCTURE

*The basics. Working. For everyone.*

A country that functions properly provides certain things as standard. Not as charity. As infrastructure.

**Public Toilets**
- Statutory minimum provision — councils cannot cut below the floor
- Ring-fenced funding — not subject to general budget cuts
- All A-road laybys and rest stops: free facilities, maintained, lit
- HGV rest areas: proper facilities, showers, safe — truckers are legally required to rest; the infrastructure must exist
- Petrol stations on public roads: free access required for any public contract
- Changing Places facilities required in all new major public buildings
- Free at point of use — the 30p turnstile is abolished
- National standards: cleanliness, lighting, safety — inspected and enforced

**Public Showers**
- Available in community centres, leisure facilities, and dedicated hubs in city centres
- Serves the homeless, outdoor workers, cyclists, and anyone who needs them
- Linked to public swimming pools and leisure centres where possible
- Not a charity. Infrastructure.

**Public Kitchens**
- Accessible cooking spaces available at community centres, libraries, and dedicated facilities
- Open for use any time — equipped, clean, safe
- Evening cooking classes and skills workshops run from the same spaces
- Serves people without home cooking facilities, those facing energy cost pressure, people in temporary accommodation, and anyone who wants to learn
- Hotel guests who would rather cook than eat out every night
- Not a soup kitchen. A kitchen. Available to everyone.

**Public Orchards & Community Growing**
- Public orchards planted in parks and green spaces — fruit freely available to anyone
- Three categories:
  - *Public Orchard*: fully open, anyone harvests freely
  - *Commons Reserve*: protected growing stock — seed trees and mother trees that regenerate public orchards; not for public harvest
  - *Private*: clearly marked, not public
- Community growing spaces alongside — allotments, raised beds, shared gardens
- Connected to school growing programmes

## 3.3 ENERGY — INDEPENDENCE AND SECURITY

**The goal: Britain powers Britain.**

**Renewables:**
- Solar: rooftops, not fields. Fields grow food. Roofs generate power. Mandatory on all new builds.
- Wind: onshore and offshore expansion
- Tidal and wave: Britain has world-class tidal resources and is barely using them. This is strategic negligence.
- Hydro: Scotland's river network makes community micro-hydro viable across dozens of sites — each scheme powering a local town frees national grid capacity. Aggregate effect is significant.
- Geothermal: Cornwall's deep geothermal project provides the expansion template

**Nuclear:** Modern nuclear provides stable baseload. Investment in next-generation reactors alongside renewables.

**Oil and gas:** Domestic production maintained during transition — British oil for British people first. Surplus sold internationally. Revenue to Sovereign Wealth Fund.

**Smart grid:** Upgraded to handle distributed rooftop generation. Excess household power bought back at fair rates.

**District heating:** Shared heat networks in new cities and urban areas — efficient, lower carbon than individual boilers.

## 3.4 THE BULLET TRAIN — ENGINEERING, NOT POLITICS

Japan builds high-speed rail through earthquake zones on mountainous islands, on time and on budget. Britain is flat, geologically stable, and comparatively small.

The argument against a British bullet train is not engineering. It is political will and fiscal priority.

HS2 failed because it was mismanaged, over-scoped, and subjected to political interference at every stage. That is a lesson in how *not* to do it — not an argument against doing it at all.

Britain Forward commits to:
- A properly scoped, properly managed high-speed rail programme
- Engineering decisions made by engineers — not politicians
- Transparent budget with genuine contingency built in
- A national high-speed network as a long-term goal — starting with the routes that deliver most economic benefit

*This is not a promise for this Parliament. It is a direction. And the direction will not change.*

## 3.5 CITIES — BUILT FOR PEOPLE

**New Planned Cities**

The housing crisis is a supply and distribution problem. New cities, built right:
- Located deliberately outside the South East — redistributing opportunity, not just population
- Multi-layer design from day one (see below)
- Mixed housing: owned, social, cooperative
- Employment planned alongside residential
- Green corridors and open space integrated — not afterthought
- Public transport from day one

**Multi-Layer Urban Design**

- Underground: heavy transit and private vehicles — fast, separated, weather-proof; underground freight delivery for city centres
- Surface: walking and cycling only; green space, clean air, community
- Rooftops: solar panels and green roofs standard
- Sky bridges connecting civic and commercial districts

**Listed and Derelict Buildings**

- Listed building reform: tiered review; repair pathways that work within listing; demolition permitted for genuinely unsalvageable structures with replacement requirements
- Derelict buildings: compulsory purchase at fair value; rebuild to modern standard or convert to greenspace

## 4.8 POLICIES UNDER RESEARCH — NOT YET ENACTED

Britain Forward is committed to evidence-led policy. The following areas are under active research and will be brought forward when the evidence base is sufficiently robust:

**Drug Policy Reform**
The current classification system is widely criticised as inconsistent and not fit for purpose. The evidence from countries that have pursued decriminalisation and regulation — Portugal, Netherlands, Canada — shows that:
- Treating drug use as a health issue reduces harm
- Regulation allows identification and protection of vulnerable people
- Criminalising users prevents them seeking help
- Illegal markets fund serious organised crime

Britain Forward commits to evidence review and, when the evidence supports it, a harm-based regulatory framework. Curtailing drug use is easier when it is legal — you can regulate, tax, and identify victims rather than criminalising them.

**Sex Work Policy**
Legal, regulated sex work enables:
- Worker protections and health standards
- Identification and protection of trafficking victims — far easier in a regulated environment
- Enforcement focused on exploitation rather than workers

Britain Forward commits to evidence review of international models (New Zealand's model in particular) before legislation.

**Relationship Law Reform**
Legal recognition of diverse adult relationship structures is under review. Safeguards, legal frameworks, and international precedents are being assessed.

**Personal Liberty and Decriminalisation**
Britain Forward believes in the principle that what consenting adults do in ways that harm nobody else is not the state's business. The practical and legal frameworks for various liberalisations are under review.

*These policies will be brought forward when the evidence and legal frameworks are ready — not before, and not never.*

# THE VISION — BRITAIN IN THE 22ND CENTURY

This is not a party for the next election. It is a movement for the next century.

By 2100, Britain should be:

**Energy sovereign** — producing more than it needs, selling the surplus, funding the future.

**A place that works** — roads that last, cities designed for people, trains that connect, water that doesn't leak, broadband that reaches everywhere.

**A capable people** — educated in how to think, not just what to think; equipped for work, for life, and for democracy.

**A fair country** — where effort leads to reward, where the basics are provided, where the system serves the people rather than extracting from them.

**A world leader** — not through military dominance but through excellence in medicine, technology, clean energy, food innovation, and engineering.

**A sovereign democracy** — making its own decisions, for its own people, accountable to nobody but Britain.
