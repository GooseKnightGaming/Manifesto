# WHAT WE ARE — NOT LEFT, NOT RIGHT, FORWARD

**Not Left** — we don't accept that helping people must come at the structural expense of the businesses they depend on. Where trade-offs exist, we acknowledge them honestly and minimise them visibly.

**Not Right** — we don't accept that helping business must come at the structural expense of the people that businesses serve. Where trade-offs exist, we acknowledge them honestly and minimise them visibly.

**Not Centre** — we reject the triangulated preservation of a status quo that is demonstrably not working. "Moderate" is not the same as "right." Avoiding hard choices is not wisdom. It is failure deferred.

**Forward** — for every policy, we ask: does this make Britain genuinely better? Trade-offs are acknowledged openly. Mitigations are planned visibly. Evidence, not tribe, guides us. Always.

Some trade-offs are unavoidable. A corporation that pays fair wages contributes less to shareholder returns. A tax system that funds public services takes from disposable income. These are real. We will not pretend they aren't. What we will do is make them as small as possible, distribute them as fairly as possible, and explain every one of them.

**Evidence, not tribe. Honesty, not pretence. Always.**

# THE EVIDENCE COMMITMENT — NON-NEGOTIABLE

**Britain Forward will:**

- Commission independent research before major policy decisions — not after, not to justify decisions already made
- Publish the full research papers — not summaries, not press releases, the actual research
- Publish plain-English versions simultaneously — evidence that only academics can read is not democratic evidence
- Publish findings even when they contradict assumptions — and amend policy accordingly
- Review every major policy annually against its stated objectives
- Reverse or reform policies that evidence shows are failing — and explain why, publicly

**The Office for Evidence-Based Policy:**

New independent body established day one. Reports to Parliament, not ministers. Publishes on its own schedule — findings cannot be suppressed by government. Staffed by independent researchers.

**The Reversal Commitment:**

Britain Forward will reverse policies that evidence shows are failing. We will not defend failures because they are "our" policies. This is what evidence-based governance actually means.

# WHAT WE ARE — SOVEREIGN DEMOCRACY

**What Sovereign Democracy IS:**

**Democratic control of national essentials.** Water, energy, food capacity, oil reserves, core infrastructure — owned by the British public, answerable to the British public, operated for the British public.

**Decision-making lodged with the British electorate.** Not with foreign governments pressuring policy. Not with donors buying influence. Not with media barons dictating terms. Not with unaccountable technocrats overriding votes. Where decisions affect Britain, British citizens decide — through votes, representatives, and referenda.

**Transparent accountability between elections, not just at them.** Every major policy published with evidence. Every spending decision visible in plain language. Every decision-maker known and answerable. Democracy does not pause between election days.

**Active international cooperation where it serves Britain.** Sovereignty is the capacity to choose — including the capacity to choose partnership. Britain enters agreements as a sovereign actor making sovereign decisions. Cooperation by choice, not by capture.

**Resilience against capture.** Mechanisms exist — Evidence Commitment, transparent funding, conflict of interest rules, Vote of Incompetence — to prevent British democracy being quietly bought, bullied, or bypassed.

**The test:** When a decision is made that affects Britain, the answer to "who decided this and why?" must be clear, publicly available, and accountable to the British electorate. If the answer is foreign, captured, or hidden — Sovereign Democracy has failed.

# PILLAR ONE: FIX THE SYSTEM
## Democratic & Political Renewal

---

## 1.1 MINISTERIAL COMPETENCE

Ministers will have demonstrable expertise or professional experience relevant to their department. A Health Secretary will have worked in healthcare. A Chancellor in economics. An Education Secretary in education. A Defence Secretary will have served or worked in defence.

This is not elitism. This is responsibility. No learning on the job at the nation's expense.

---

## 1.2 ELECTORAL REFORM — RANKED WEIGHTED VOTING

*(Full detail: sub-document — Voting System Overhaul)*

**Ranked Weighted Voting replaces First Past the Post.** Voters rank candidates in preference order. Points awarded by ranking. Winners must command broad support. No wasted votes. No tactical voting. Every constituency matters.

**Compulsory Participation — Australian Model.** Every eligible citizen participates. Spoiled ballots and NOTA are valid.

*HRA fallback:* If legal opinion concludes compulsory voting is unlawful under the Human Rights Act (as may be argued under freedom of conscience protections), we implement an incentive-based alternative instead. The principle — full democratic participation — is preserved; the mechanism adapts to constitutional law. This is not abandonment; it is constitutional respect.

**None of the Above on every ballot.** Formally counted, published, acted on. NOTA winning outright triggers fresh election within 60 days — original candidates ineligible.

**Coalition Red Lines — stated before any election:**
Britain Forward will not coalition with any party that opposes ranked weighted voting, reverses public ownership of core infrastructure, or abandons ministerial competence requirements.

---

## 1.3 POLICY-BASED VOTING — THE LONG-TERM DIRECTION

*Aspirational. Genuinely complex. Included honestly.*

Most voters don't want Column A whole-hog or Column B whole-hog. The current system forces all-or-nothing choices between packages most voters wouldn't design themselves.

**The Swiss Federal Council Model:**

A **National Implementation Council** — five to seven senior figures drawn proportionally from parties per their electoral share, confirmed by Parliament through a cross-party vote, serving fixed terms not tied to electoral cycles. The Council implements policy. Parliament debates and legislates. Citizens vote directly on major questions through binding referenda.

This separates three functions currently collapsed into one: democratic mandate, legislative process, and executive implementation.

**Britain Forward's pathway:**
- This Parliament: Citizens' Assembly on constitutional reform established; policy-based voting researched and published
- Next Parliament: Binding referendum on Assembly proposals
- Long-term: Constitutional framework adapted based on what Britain votes for

---

## 1.4 CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

**The Monarchy — Constitutional Custodian of Last Resort.** Sovereignty resides in the British people. The Monarch is a non-political safeguard acting only in genuine constitutional crisis to restore conditions for popular sovereignty.

**The House of Lords — Public Decision.** Three reform options; public consultation; binding referendum. The public decides. Options: elected Senate; hybrid chamber; full abolition with functions to National Oversight Council.

**National Oversight Council.** Elected members plus independently appointed experts. No government appointments, ever. Power to delay and refer legislation. Integrated with judicial review.

**Parliament — London Now, Direction Set.** Government departments: phased regional relocation begins immediately. Independent commission reports within five years. Public referendum decides Parliament's future location.

---

## 1.5 POLITICAL ACCOUNTABILITY

**Conflict of Interest — The Precise Standard.**

*The test:* Did this company materially benefit from a specific decision this MP made or influenced?

- No involvement with such companies in the previous **5 years** before taking office
- No involvement while **serving**
- No involvement for **10 years after** leaving office
- Where demonstrable benefit flows to an MP through a family connection: same standard applies

**MP Pay — Public Service Rate. No Exceptions.**

MPs will be paid the median wage of the constituency they represent. Full stop.

If that median is below basic decency, the MP lives at that level until they fix it — for themselves and for everyone they represent. There is no floor above the constituency reality. The principle is not comfort. The principle is skin-in-the-game. Fix it for your constituency, or live with what you've failed to fix.

*Reasonable accommodation allowance remains:* London accommodation costs for non-London MPs; genuine travel costs; staff costs — all transparent and audited.

*Zero outside income while serving.* Full attention to constituents.

*All expenses:* Publicly searchable, audited quarterly. Personal and family expenses are never eligible. The standard: "Would a reasonable constituent accept this as a legitimate work cost?"

*Pay rises:* Same process as public sector. Not self-determined.

---

## 1.6 TRANSPARENT TAX SPENDING

Annual plain-English breakdown — national and local. Every library, GP surgery, job centre, post office, and online. Independently verified. Actual proportions.

---

## 1.7 PUBLIC BUDGET REFORM — END "USE IT OR LOSE IT"

Public bodies that underspend risk having their budget cut next year. The rational response: spend everything, whether or not it needs spending. This incentivises waste and punishes efficiency.

**The Five Fixes:**
1. **Multi-Year Budgets** — three-year rolling settlements
2. **Carry-Forward Allowance** — up to 20% rolls forward
3. **Efficiency Dividend** — genuine savings: 50% retained, 50% returns to central budget
4. **Outcome-Based Funding Review** — based on demonstrated need and achieved outcomes, not just last year's spend
5. **Ring-Fenced Honest Underspend** — organisations that report honestly are protected from automatic cuts

---

## 1.8 CIVIL SERVICE REFORM

Outcome-based accountability. Digital-first government. Cross-department integration. Major projects through independent delivery bodies. Political neutrality protected.

## 2.3 SMALL BUSINESS — BRITAIN'S BEST PLACE TO START

**The Vision:** Near-zero tax for genuine small enterprises. Britain becomes the best place in the world to start something.

| Annual Qualifying Revenue | Corporation Tax Rate |
|---|---|
| Under £100,000 | 0% — zero |
| £100,000 — £500,000 | Minimal (target: 5%) |
| £500,000 — £5 million | Intermediate |
| £5 million — £50 million | Standard |
| Over £50 million | Full — no reduction |

**The Anti-Fragmentation Rule — Five Legal Tests (any one triggers):**
1. One entity holds more than 25% beneficial ownership in two or more companies
2. A director holds executive authority in two or more companies
3. Two or more companies share premises, branding, supply chains, customer base, or key personnel above a defined threshold
4. Transactions between companies at non-arm's-length rates
5. A company divided after this legislation takes effect — presumed single entity for 10 years unless genuine independence demonstrated to HMRC

Burden of proof on the business. Penalty for deliberate fragmentation: full back-tax plus 100% penalty plus interest. Criminal referral for systematic schemes.

**Additional measures:** Simplified reporting; business rates relief for small high-street businesses; late payment penalties for large companies; public procurement reserved for small businesses below a threshold.

## 2.5 FUEL DUTY RELIEF — AUTOMATIC AND DEFINED

Trigger: **both** conditions simultaneously:

**Condition 1 — Qualifying Crisis Event:** armed conflict affecting major supply route; OPEC+ cut >10%; sanctions affecting >5% global supply; European refining failure >5%; IEA declared energy emergency. *Petrol being 6p more expensive than last week does not trigger this. War disrupting global supply does.*

**Condition 2 — Price Threshold:** retail unleaded exceeds 36-month rolling average by more than 20%, sustained for 14+ consecutive days.

Relief: up to 50% of standard rate, scaling with severity. Ends automatically when either condition no longer met — assessed every 14 days. No ministerial announcement required.

## 2.6 REINDUSTRIALISATION — BRITAIN MAKES THINGS AGAIN

Britain built the modern industrial world. Then Britain lost it — to offshoring, short-term thinking, and the idea that services alone could sustain a modern economy. The results are visible: supply chain vulnerability, regional economic collapse, over-reliance on hostile or unstable foreign suppliers.

Britain WILL rebuild industrial capability.

**Honest Framing:**
- *Return:* Where industries can genuinely compete — steel, shipbuilding, specialist manufacturing
- *Build new:* Where the future hasn't been claimed yet — green manufacturing, battery chemistry, critical minerals
- *Stop losing:* Where capability is bleeding away — pharmaceuticals, aerospace
- *Not nostalgia:* Industries that genuinely can't compete in a modern economy don't come back through wishful thinking

**Priority Sectors:**

*Steel — Sheffield, Scunthorpe, Port Talbot.*
Green steel via electric arc furnaces and hydrogen reduction. Domestic supply for construction, rail, offshore wind, shipbuilding. Britain should lead green-transition steel, not import it.

*Shipbuilding — Clyde, Tyne, Belfast, Barrow.*
Commercial and defence. Offshore wind support vessels particularly — the market is enormous and Britain should be building them.

*Textiles — Lancashire, Yorkshire.*
Wool, technical textiles, sustainable fashion. Market shift toward lower-turnover, higher-quality products favours British production.

*Ceramics — Stoke.*
Specialist industrial ceramics; construction materials; heritage tableware retained.

*Green Manufacturing — New Capability.*
Solar panels, wind turbine components, heat pumps, battery chemistry, EV components, grid infrastructure. Britain deploys green technology at scale — Britain should also BUILD it.

*Critical Minerals Processing.*
Currently dependent on foreign — often hostile — processing. Lithium, rare earths, recycling capability — strategic national infrastructure.

*Specialist Manufacturing.*
Pharmaceuticals, medical devices, aerospace components, scientific instruments. Sectors where Britain retains capability that must not be allowed to erode.

**Non-Negotiable Constraints:**
- Green standards: no dirty reindustrialisation; British producers meet the same emission standards as CBAM imports
- Workers' rights: union recognition, safety standards, real living wage minimum
- Evidence-led: targeted investment where sectors can genuinely compete, not subsidies propping up the uncompetitive
- Sovereign Wealth Fund: strategic equity stakes — British public benefits from British industrial revival

**Mechanisms:**
- Published sector-by-sector industrial strategy — not announcement-by-announcement
- Procurement preferences for British production where quality and price competitive
- Training pipelines tied to sector needs — Education-Employment Liaisons extended to manufacturing
- Regional industrial zones with supporting infrastructure, housing, and transport
- Patient capital: long-term investment horizon, not quarterly returns

**Evidence:** Germany's Mittelstand. South Korea's industrial policy. US CHIPS Act. Industrial policy works when done properly — patient capital, strategic focus, honest evaluation.

# PILLAR THREE: NATIONAL INFRASTRUCTURE
## Roads, Cities, Transport, Energy & Connectivity

---

## 3.1 ROADS — BUILT TO LAST

- Long-life composite surfaces — decades, not years
- Whole-life costing in all contracts
- Road-embedded EV charging — inductive coils during rebuild; laden commercial EVs viable
- Green corridors alongside all new roads
- Usage-based road pricing replaces fuel duty — fair, transparent, hypothecated to roads and transport

---

## 3.2 PUBLIC DIGNITY INFRASTRUCTURE

*Not charity. Infrastructure.*

**Public Toilets:** Statutory minimum, ring-fenced funding. A-road laybys: free, maintained, lit, safe. Petrol stations on public contracts: free access required. Changing Places in all new major public buildings. Free at point of use.

**HGV Rest Areas — Secure and Equipped:**

Truckers are legally required to take rest breaks. The infrastructure must exist — and it must be safe.

- Proper facilities: toilets, showers, hot food and drink
- **Security:** CCTV coverage of all bays, load bays, and vehicle access. Adequate lighting throughout. On-site or contracted security presence at major rest areas — particularly on high-theft routes. Partnership with police, hauliers, and insurers on load theft prevention. Load theft and driver assault treated as the serious crimes they are. Reporting mechanisms that actually work.
- Regular inspection and maintenance — not built once and left
- EV charging integrated — heavy goods EV transition requires charging at rest stops

**Public Showers:** At community centres, leisure facilities, and urban hubs. Infrastructure, not charity.

**Public Kitchens:** Accessible cooking spaces, any time, FSA-standard. Evening cooking classes. Available to anyone.

**Public Orchards & Community Growing:** Public orchards — free harvest. Commons Reserve — protected seed stock, not for harvest. Community growing alongside. Connected to schools.

---

## 3.3 PUBLIC TRANSPORT — FREE OR HEAVILY SUBSIDISED

*Model: Luxembourg (free buses since 2020)*

**The Principle:** A bus carrying 50 people does less road damage, produces less pollution, and uses less road space than 50 cars. Subsidising the bus benefits everyone — including drivers in less traffic.

**Buses — Free at Point of Use:**
- Urban and suburban buses: free
- Rural buses: free — these serve people with no viable alternative
- Frequency: minimum service standards — 30-minute peak frequency is a baseline
- Night services: restored where cut
- Real-time information at all stops

**Trams — Inner-City and Beyond:**
*Model: Strasbourg, Vienna, Amsterdam, Manchester Metrolink*

Trams serve the zone between pedestrianised centres and the motorised periphery — where cars shouldn't go, but buses can't reach as effectively.

- Inner-city tram networks in all major cities above a defined population threshold
- Tram extensions in cities with existing systems: Manchester, Edinburgh, Nottingham, Sheffield
- New systems assessed for: Leeds, Bristol, Cardiff, Belfast, and others where population density supports viability
- Trams on clean electricity — zero local emissions
- Integrated ticketing with buses and rail
- Free or heavily subsidised fares

**Urban Commuter Rail:** Fares capped and subsidised within major city regions. Integrated ticketing. Oyster-style systems extended beyond London to all major city regions.

**How the Subsidy Is Funded:** Road pricing revenue; reduced car infrastructure costs; reduced congestion costs; reduced NHS air pollution costs; general taxation justified by the documented economic returns.

---

## 3.4 WALKABLE CITIES
*Design principle: Copenhagen, Amsterdam, Vienna*

**The 15-Minute City:** Most daily needs accessible within 15 minutes on foot — schools, shops, healthcare, parks, work. Car optional, not essential.

**New Cities and New Developments:**
- Maximum distances: primary school (10-min walk), GP (10-min walk), green space (5-min walk), shops (10-min walk)
- Street design prioritises pedestrian movement
- Ground floor uses: shops, cafes, services — not car parks or blank walls
- Continuous, well-lit, accessible pavements throughout

**Existing City Centres:** Town centre pedestrianisation expanded. Pavement quality: national minimum standard. Crossings timed for pedestrians, not cars. Pavement parking: enforcement substantially strengthened.

**Accessibility:** Wheelchair and mobility scooter access throughout. Tactile paving and audible signals maintained to standard. Dropped kerbs at all crossings. Shared space (kerb-free) used only where evidence genuinely supports it — evidence on visual impairment clearly supports kerb separation.

---

## 3.5 BIKEABLE CITIES
*Model: Netherlands — physical separation, not painted lines*

**Why People Don't Cycle in Britain:** Fear. Cycling mixed with fast traffic and no physical protection is genuinely dangerous. People who would happily cycle on a protected lane will not cycle on a busy A-road beside lorries. Physical separation is the answer.

**Protected Cycle Lanes:**
- Physically separated on all urban arterial roads — raised kerb, planters, or parking buffer
- Minimum 1.5m each direction
- Continuous routes — a lane that disappears at every junction is not a lane

**Safe Junctions:** Separate cyclist signal phases. Cycle-specific signals. Advanced stop lines at all other junctions.

**Cycle Parking:** Secure, covered at all public transport stops, stations, major public buildings. Cargo bike parking and charging at key locations.

**Bike-Share Schemes:** Publicly funded or subsidised in all cities above a defined population threshold. E-bikes included. Integrated with public transport accounts.

**E-Bike Subsidy:** Subsidised purchase for households below a defined income threshold — French and German models as evidence base.

**Rural Cycling:** Speed limit review on narrow rural roads. Improved route signage. Off-road paths alongside major rural A-roads where on-road cycling is genuinely dangerous.

---

## 3.6 ENERGY — BRITAIN POWERS BRITAIN

**Honest Transition Sequence:** Renewable capacity cannot replace all demand immediately. During transition, Britain maintains domestic fossil fuel production. Not a contradiction — honest sequencing.

**Renewables:** Solar (rooftops not fields, mandatory on new builds); wind (onshore and offshore); tidal and wave (world-class resources currently neglected); hydro (Scotland's river network); geothermal (Cornwall as template).

**Nuclear:** Stable baseload. Next-generation reactor investment.

**Transition Fuels — Milestone Phase-Out (not calendar dates):**
- Coal power: phases out when green baseload covers peak demand for 30 consecutive days
- Gas for domestic heating: phases out sector by sector as heat pump penetration and grid capacity demonstrate readiness
- Coking coal: phases out when green steel is commercially viable at scale in Britain

Worker transition: explicit commitment. No community left behind. Retraining funded. Green energy jobs prioritised for fossil fuel workers.

---

## 3.7 RURAL CONNECTIVITY — NO COMMUNITY LEFT BEHIND

Communities that cannot decarbonise at urban pace need connection, not condemnation.

**The Rural Connectivity Fund:** Grid extension funded nationally (not charged to communities). Insulation to heat-pump-readiness before transition required. Heating oil phase-out with notice, funded alternatives, extended timelines where no viable alternative yet exists. Rural bus minimum service standards. Demand-responsive transport for sparse areas. Branch line restoration assessed. Rural EV charging as public infrastructure. Gigabit broadband universal. Mobile healthcare. Telemedicine. Rural pharmacies and post offices: protected.

## 3.9 CITIES AND HOUSING

**Immediate:** 100,000 new social homes. Cooperative Housing programme. Planning reform. Derelict building compulsory purchase. Agricultural land protected.

## 4.10 JUSTICE & CRIMINAL REFORM

**Principle: Rehabilitation, Not Only Punishment**

Prison is the state's strongest intervention against its citizens. When the state uses that power:
- Public protection: yes, non-negotiable
- Punishment: proportionate to harm
- Rehabilitation: genuine intention, measurable outcomes
- Deterrence: where evidence supports it

**Evidence:** Norway's recidivism rate (~20%) is less than half the UK's (~50%+). Norwegian prisons are more expensive per prisoner — but rehabilitation reduces the lifetime public cost of crime. Evidence consistently shows: harsh conditions increase reoffending; rehabilitation reduces it.

**Understanding Drivers:**
Most crime has drivers: poverty, addiction, mental illness, abuse histories, lack of opportunity. These do not excuse crime. They do explain it. Addressing drivers reduces crime.
- Mental health assessment for every prisoner on entry; treatment provided
- Addiction services in prison and on release — continuity, not cliff-edge
- Literacy and education: prison as genuine second chance
- Housing on release: no one released into homelessness
- Employment pathways: see in-prison work below

**In-Prison Work — Expanded:**
Work in prison (meaningful, skilled, paid) reduces reoffending substantially when structured properly.
- All able prisoners have work opportunity — not optional warehousing
- Fair wages: prisoner saves for release, contributes to victim compensation where relevant, supports dependants
- Skills development tied to post-release employment
- For serious and repeat offenders, work participation is an expectation — connecting prison time to preparation for productive citizenship

**Extended Sentences for Abuse of Position/Trust:**
Where an offender abused a position of trust to commit a crime, the breach of trust is a distinct harm.
- Statutory sentencing enhancement for crimes involving abuse of position (medical, educational, care, legal, financial, political)
- Sentence multiplier where role specifically enabled the crime
- Longer supervision post-release

**Prison Conditions — Honest Framing:**
UK prisons are violent, overcrowded, understaffed, and drug-saturated. "Soft" they are not.
- Basic dignity: clean, safe, medical care — non-negotiable under international law
- Earned privileges: access to entertainment and improved conditions tied to behaviour and engagement with rehabilitation
- Earned-privilege model: access tied to behaviour and engagement is the evidence-led middle path between "luxury" and "cruelty that increases reoffending"

**Courts — Faster Justice:**
- Court capacity expanded; delay reduction targets published and enforced
- Victim-focused reform throughout
- Legal aid restored: access to justice is not optional

**Policies Under Evidence Review — Justice Section:**

*Death penalty:* Britain Forward's honest position on current evidence:
- Wrongful convictions — Birmingham Six, Guildford Four, Stefan Kiszko — each would have been executed; each was innocent; each was exonerated by evidence that emerged years later
- No procedural safeguard has historically prevented wrongful conviction
- Reinstating requires leaving the ECHR (Protocol 13 prohibits it absolutely)
- Evidence on deterrent effect does not support it
- The wrongful-conviction problem alone is likely to rule this out under the Evidence Commitment — the Commitment's core mechanism is reversibility; execution is irreversible. **Not reinstated on current evidence. Position reopens only if the wrongful-conviction problem can be credibly resolved.**

*Chemical and surgical castration for sex offenders:* **Not currently supported as British policy.** Evidence is methodologically weak; carceral-consent problems strain ordinary medical ethics; irreversible interventions fail the Evidence Commitment's reversibility requirement; wrongful-conviction risk applies equally. Genuine openness if proper evidence develops that addresses these concerns. What Britain Forward supports instead: forensic mental health treatment; CBT programmes with demonstrated recidivism reduction; long sentences where warranted; victim support. Britain Forward commits to effective intervention and honest evidence — not performative severity.

## 5.2 SCHOOL UNIFORM POLICY

**The Problem:**

Mandatory specific uniforms create financial hardship for families — especially where "approved supplier" monopolies inflate prices — and punish children for circumstances they did not choose. A child who arrives in a non-approved-supplier blazer that looks identical to the approved one should not be excluded, sent home, or shamed.

**The Policy:**

**What schools CAN have:**
- Dress codes — defining colours, styles, standards of appearance
- House colours — team spirit, belonging, identity within the school community
- General appearance standards — neat, appropriate for an educational environment
- Specialised kit requirements for genuinely specialist activities (PE, science labs, catering, workshops) — safety requirements are legitimate

**What schools CANNOT do:**
- Mandate a specific uniform available only from a specific supplier
- Require branded items that carry a significant premium over functionally identical alternatives
- Penalise students for wearing functionally equivalent alternatives in the correct colours and style

**The Affordability Principle:**
If something is mandatory for education, it cannot be prohibitively expensive for any family. Where specialised items are genuinely required (safety equipment, specific protective gear), the school provides them or funds access to them. Cost is never a barrier to education.

**Second-Hand and Exchange:**
Schools are actively encouraged to establish and maintain uniform exchange programmes — second-hand uniform availability as standard, not as an afterthought.

**Enforcement:**
Trading Standards empowered to investigate school suppliers charging monopoly premiums. Local authority oversight of school uniform policies against these standards.

## 5.3 BAREFOOT AND LOW-SHOE LEARNING ENVIRONMENTS

**The Evidence:**

Research emerging from New Zealand, Scandinavia, and UK pilots suggests benefits from reduced or removed footwear in educational settings:
- Improved balance and proprioception
- Improved posture
- Reduced noise in classrooms and corridors
- Some evidence of improved focus and sensory regulation
- Children naturally more comfortable, less physically restricted

This evidence is not conclusive at scale — which is exactly why it belongs in the Evidence Commitment framework rather than as a national mandate.

**The Policy:**

School and Trust decisions — evidence-based, locally governed, not nationally mandated.

Schools and Multi-Academy Trusts may implement, at their own discretion:

*Level 1:* No shoes in specific classrooms (often infant classrooms, drama rooms, libraries) — classroom-by-classroom policy

*Level 2:* No shoes in classrooms generally across the school — shoes removed on entry to any classroom, worn in corridors and outdoors

*Level 3:* No shoes indoors — shoes removed on school entry, worn only in outdoor areas

*Level 4:* Full barefoot school — bare feet in all areas including playgrounds and outdoor learning (weather and safety permitting)

**Safeguards for all levels:**
- Floor surfaces assessed for safety before implementation — no barefoot policy on surfaces that are not safe
- Adequate hygiene standards — floors maintained appropriately
- Medical and sensory accommodations: children who genuinely cannot participate (foot conditions, sensory sensitivities, prosthetics) are never excluded or pressured; alternative accommodation always available
- Cold weather: appropriate accommodation always available
- Parental consultation before implementation — not imposed without community conversation
- Annual evidence review — schools implementing these policies contribute data to national evidence base through the Office for Evidence-Based Policy

**The National Framework:**
- Circular guidance published to all schools on the evidence base and implementation considerations
- No mandate — local decision, parental consultation, safety assessment
- Evidence collected centrally from implementing schools — published, reviewed, updated
- Schools that find the evidence doesn't apply locally can reverse — this is the Evidence Commitment in action at school level

## 5.5 EMPLOYMENT REFORM

**Advertised Salaries: Mandatory.** All job ads state actual salary. "Competitive" banned. Salary question in interviews abolished.

**Banned Interview Questions:** Hypothetical personality questions; personal life, family, relationship status; salary history; questions irrelevant to job performance.

**Alternative Assessment Formats:** Work trials, portfolio reviews, task assessments. Public sector leads.

**Neurodivergent Adaptations and Strengths:**

*Adaptations:* Questions in advance as standard option. Reasonable adjustments enforced throughout the hiring process.

*Strengths recognition:* Neurodivergent people are not a category to be managed — they are a talent pool with specific strengths. Pattern recognition, attention to detail, hyperfocus, systemised thinking, lateral connection — these are capabilities, not symptoms. Public sector leads in matching neurodivergent candidates to roles where their strengths genuinely excel. Private sector: tax recognition for genuine strength-matching programmes (not hiring quotas — genuine capability matching). Principle: the right person in the right role, not tokenistic placement.

**Entry-Level Standards:**
Experience requirements for starter positions are banned. Additionally: employers cannot reclassify genuinely entry-level roles as mid-level to impose experience barriers. Statutory framework defines entry-level based on actual role requirements, not employer labelling. Enforcement: review mechanism with real consequences for mislabelling.

Piloted in public sector with rigorous evaluation. National rollout conditional on demonstrated productivity maintenance and worker wellbeing improvement.

*Pattern flexibility is the principle — not "everyone gets Friday off."* Workers choose their 4-day pattern where jobs permit. Contracts specify patterns where specific coverage is required. Rolling schedules (Week 1 Mon-Thu, Week 2 Tue-Fri) accommodated. Services requiring 7-day coverage keep 7-day coverage through rotating staff patterns — not through individual workers doing 5 days.

## 5.6 EXAM BOARDS & ASSESSMENT REFORM

**The Problem:** High-pressure single-event written exams test one narrow ability — performing under timed memory pressure — and fail to measure most of what education is supposed to develop.

**Exam Board Reform:**
Move toward consolidated national framework with strong independent regulation. Scotland's single-board model offers evidence. Transition managed: teachers and schools not destabilised; qualification portability preserved. Cost to schools reduced.

**Assessment Diversification:**

Exams retained WHERE they genuinely test the relevant skill. Supplemented or replaced WHERE they don't.

*Formats available:*
- Traditional written exams — retained where timed recall genuinely matters
- Coursework — sustained project work
- Research projects — independent investigation
- Group projects — collaboration skills explicitly assessed
- Application exams (not memory tests) — open-book, problem-solving
- Oral examinations/vivas — spoken demonstration of understanding
- Portfolio assessment — body of work over time
- Practical demonstrations — sciences, trades, performance
- Work placement assessment — vocational parity
- Take-home exams — longer timescale, deeper thinking

Student choice within teacher guidance: where multiple formats are valid, students select the format that tests what they can actually do — not "choose the easy option."

**Accommodations — Genuine, Not Grudging:**
Viva/oral anxiety: alternative assessment provides equivalent qualification. Processing time: extra time, quiet rooms, screen readers as standard where needed. Neurodivergent accommodations: questions in advance where applicable; assessment formats adapted.

**Pre-GCSE Qualification — The "Something at 12" Principle:**

Three years of secondary learning currently produces nothing concrete until GCSEs at 16. Students who struggle or leave education early have nothing recognised from those years.

Britain WILL introduce recognised qualifications at approximately age 12-13:
- National standard at end of Key Stage 2 / beginning of Key Stage 3
- Proper qualification, not just internal assessment
- Means adults returning to education don't need to re-sit what they already achieved
- Foundation for GCSE-level study; enables genuine assessment of progress

**Grading:** Broaden how grades are earned across assessment formats. Deeper grading restructure — not cosmetic change (9-1 is just re-masked A*-U) — assessed after assessment diversification beds in.

**Evidence:** International Baccalaureate; Finnish education; New Zealand's NCEA. Multiple assessment formats consistently produce better outcomes than narrow single-format examination.

# SOCIAL POLICY

## Equality & Identity
Full legal recognition of non-binary identities. Gender transition recognition — streamlined, respectful processes. Protection from discrimination under law.

## Relationships & Unions — Recognition For All

Britain recognises committed adult relationships in all forms. Civil marriage, civil partnership, religious marriage in any faith, pagan hand-fasting, humanist ceremony, secular declaration — all equally valid in law where freely entered between consenting adults.

Legal status depends on commitment and process, not on which tradition performed the ceremony. Religious institutions perform ceremonies according to their own principles; the state recognises the commitment, not the religion of the officiant.

**Universal Recognition Framework:**
- Civil registration remains the legal foundation — a record exists
- Ceremonies in any tradition (or none) can serve as the registration event, subject to legal witnesses
- Minimum requirements: consent, adulthood, competence, non-coercion — applied universally
- Religious institutions retain their own internal rules for who they will marry; the state does not compel
- Legal effects (inheritance, next-of-kin, tax, parental) flow from registered status, not from the tradition

**Non-Traditional Consensual Units:**
Where adult relationships extend beyond the traditional two-person form, and all parties freely consent, legal recognition is not categorically refused. The core requirements — consent, adulthood, competence, non-coercion, absence of duress — apply universally regardless of number of participants or tradition.

Practical implementation requires careful legal framework design — inheritance, next-of-kin, parental rights, tax, property. Britain Forward commits to evidence-led review of how other jurisdictions have handled (and failed to handle) this. Safeguards against coercion are non-negotiable. Freely consenting means freely — not pressured by family, religion, or economic dependency.

*Direction stated. Detail follows evidence and consultation. Not leading the section — present because it is honest.*

## Sign Language
BSL promoted nationally. Required in healthcare, education, emergency services. Curriculum option in all schools.

## Separation of Church and State
Legal recognition of relationships determined solely by civil law. Religious institutions perform ceremonies freely — no independent legal authority over civil status.

# THE LONG GAME

This platform is not for the next election. It is a direction for the long term.

Every decision evaluated against: *Does this make Britain better in twenty, forty, a hundred years?*

Roads built to last. Cities designed for people — walkable, cycleable, served by transport people actually want to use. Energy from Britain, for Britain. A rail spine from Bournemouth to Thurso. Schools that don't punish children for what their parents can't afford. Animals treated with the respect their sentience demands.

Starting now.
