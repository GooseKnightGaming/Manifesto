## 1.2 ELECTORAL REFORM — RANKED WEIGHTED VOTING

*(Full detail: sub-document — Voting System Overhaul)*

**Ranked Weighted Voting replaces First Past the Post.** Voters rank candidates in preference order. Points awarded by ranking. Winners must command broad support — not just an intense minority. No wasted votes. No tactical voting. Every constituency matters.

**Compulsory Participation — Australian Model.** Every eligible citizen participates. Spoiled ballots and NOTA are valid. Invisible abstention replaced by formal registered dissatisfaction. HRA compatibility assessment published before legislation. Incentive-based alternative prepared if compulsion is found unlawful.

**None of the Above on every ballot.** Formally counted, published, acted on. NOTA winning triggers fresh election within 60 days — original candidates ineligible. Caretaker from National Oversight Council serves until fresh election completes.

**Coalition Red Lines — stated before any election:**
Britain Forward will not coalition with any party that opposes ranked weighted voting, reverses public ownership of core infrastructure, or abandons ministerial competence requirements. Everything else is negotiable. These three are not.

## 1.3 POLICY-BASED VOTING — THE LONG-TERM DIRECTION

*Aspirational. Genuinely complex. Included honestly.*

Most voters don't want Column A whole-hog or Column B whole-hog. They want the Green Party's drug policy AND a different party's immigration approach AND a third party's economic model. The current system forces an all-or-nothing choice between packages most voters would not design themselves.

**The idea — Liquid Democracy:** Citizens vote on individual policies, not just parties. Switzerland demonstrates this is workable at national scale through frequent binding referenda on specific policy questions.

**The implementation challenge — honest:** In a parliamentary system, someone must implement cross-party policy mandates. If voters choose policies from three different parties, which ministers implement which policy? This requires either a technocratic implementation layer beneath a policy-voting democratic layer, or a constitutional framework for cross-party policy mandates.

**Britain Forward's position:**
- More frequent binding referenda on major constitutional and policy questions — beginning with Lords reform
- Citizens' Assembly model: randomly selected citizens deliberate on complex policy questions; produce recommendations that go to referendum — informed rather than reactive direct democracy
- Long-term constitutional review commission in year two to explore how policy-based voting could be incorporated into Britain's constitutional framework

This is a direction, not a first-Parliament policy. We include it because we believe it is where democracy should go — and because honesty about long-term ambitions matters more than pretending they don't exist.

## 1.4 CONSTITUTIONAL FRAMEWORK

**The Monarchy — Constitutional Custodian of Last Resort.**
Sovereignty resides in the British people. The Monarch is a constitutional custodian — a non-political safeguard ensuring no actor can capture the entire constitutional apparatus. Acts only in genuine constitutional crisis to restore conditions for popular sovereignty. Ceremonial in normal times. Present and real in extremis.

**The House of Lords — Public Decision.**
Current form: indefensible. Function: valuable. Britain Forward publishes three reform options, consults publicly, holds a binding referendum. The public decides. Options: elected Senate; hybrid chamber (partially elected, partially independently expert-appointed); full abolition with functions to National Oversight Council.

**National Oversight Council.** Mixed composition — elected members plus independently appointed experts. No government appointments, ever. Power to delay and refer legislation — not permanently block. Integrated with judicial review.

**Parliament — London Now, Direction Set.** Remains in London this Parliament. Government departments: phased regional relocation begins immediately. Independent commission assesses Parliament's future location — reports within five years. Public referendum decides.

## 1.5 POLITICAL ACCOUNTABILITY

**Conflict of Interest — The Precise Standard.**

*The test:* Did this company materially benefit from a specific decision this MP made or influenced?

- No involvement with such companies in the previous **5 years** before taking office
- No involvement while **serving**
- No involvement for **10 years after** leaving office
- Where demonstrable benefit flows to an MP *through* a family connection: same standard applies

Sector expertise is encouraged. Personal financial benefit from specific decisions is prohibited. These are not the same thing.

**MP Pay — Public Service Rate.**
- National median wage with London cost-of-living adjustment only
- Zero outside income while serving
- Genuine work accommodation expenses only — not second property purchase
- All expenses: publicly searchable, audited quarterly, personal costs never eligible
- Pay rises: same process as public sector — not self-determined

## 2.3 SMALL BUSINESS — TAX RELIEF WITH LEGAL TEETH

**Tiered Corporation Tax:**

| Annual Qualifying Revenue | Rate |
|---|---|
| Under £500,000 | Substantially reduced |
| £500,000 — £5 million | Intermediate |
| £5 million — £50 million | Standard |
| Over £50 million | Full — no reduction |

**The Anti-Fragmentation Rule — Where This Policy Lives or Dies:**

Without rigorous anti-fragmentation, large businesses restructure into twelve artificial small companies overnight. This is not theoretical — it happens wherever tiered rates exist without legal safeguards. The safeguards are built into the primary legislation.

**Legal definition of "common control" — a company is treated as part of a larger group if ANY of the following apply:**

1. **Ownership threshold:** One person or entity holds more than 25% beneficial ownership in two or more companies — assessed together
2. **Director control:** A director or officer holds executive authority in two or more companies — flagged for assessment
3. **Operational integration:** Two or more companies share premises, branding, supply chains, customer base, or key personnel above a defined threshold — HMRC assesses whether genuine operational independence exists
4. **Non-arm's-length financial flows:** Transactions between companies at demonstrably artificial rates — assessed as a single entity
5. **Post-legislation division:** A company that was a single entity before this legislation and divided after it — presumed to remain a single entity for 10 years unless genuine operational independence is demonstrated to HMRC's satisfaction

**The burden of proof is on the business** to demonstrate genuine operational independence — not on HMRC to prove fragmentation.

*Penalty for deliberate fragmentation:* Full back-tax at the higher rate plus 100% penalty plus interest. Criminal referral for systematic schemes.

**Additional Small Business Measures:**
- Simplified single reporting framework for businesses under £500,000 revenue
- Business rates: substantial relief for small high-street businesses
- Late payment: large companies paying small suppliers late face escalating penalty interest; expedited dispute resolution for small businesses
- Public procurement: contracts below a defined threshold reserved for British small businesses — cannot be bundled into large contracts only large businesses can bid for

## 2.5 FUEL DUTY RELIEF — AUTOMATIC AND DEFINED

**The problem with ad hoc relief:** Announced politically, creating uncertainty and the impression of panic. "Petrol is 6p more expensive than last week" triggers political pressure. A mechanism with a defined trigger removes politics from the decision.

**Automatic Trigger — ALL conditions must be met simultaneously:**

**Condition 1 — Qualifying Crisis Event.** One of the following confirmed by independent assessment:
- Armed conflict directly affecting a major oil-producing region or supply route (e.g. conflict disrupting Strait of Hormuz passage)
- OPEC+ production cut of more than 10% of quota within a 90-day period
- Sanctions on a major producer affecting more than 5% of global supply
- Critical infrastructure failure affecting more than 5% of European refining or distribution capacity
- A declared energy emergency by the International Energy Agency

*This condition specifically excludes routine market fluctuation. "Petrol is more expensive this week" does not trigger this. "War has disrupted global supply" does.*

**Condition 2 — Price Threshold.** Retail unleaded petrol exceeds the 36-month rolling average by more than 20%, sustained for at least 14 consecutive days.

**The Relief:** Fuel duty reduced by up to 50% of standard rate while both conditions are met. Reduction scales with severity of price spike — proportionate, not uniform. Relief ends automatically when either condition is no longer met — assessed every 14 days.

**Priority during prolonged crises:** Extended relief directed first at rural communities with no viable public transport alternative; low-income households in bottom two income quintiles; small logistics businesses with fuel costs above 15% of operating costs.

**As road pricing replaces fuel duty:** The mechanism adapts — equivalent relief applies to the road pricing rate for commercial vehicles during qualifying crisis events.

## 3.3 ENERGY — BRITAIN POWERS BRITAIN

**The Honest Transition Sequence:**

Britain is committed to green energy. Britain is also honest about timescales. Renewable capacity cannot replace all current demand immediately. During the transition, Britain maintains domestic fossil fuel production for domestic energy security. This is not a contradiction of green ambitions. It is honest sequencing. Germany provides the cautionary tale: premature nuclear phase-out, increased dependence on Russian gas, acute crisis when that supply was cut. Britain will not repeat that mistake.

**Renewables — expanded aggressively:**
- Solar: rooftops, not fields; mandatory on all new builds; retrofit programme
- Wind: onshore and offshore expansion
- Tidal and wave: world-class British resources — currently strategic negligence; exploitation begins
- Hydro: Scotland's river network; community micro-hydro across dozens of sites — aggregate effect substantial
- Geothermal: Cornwall's deep geothermal project as expansion template

**Nuclear:** Stable baseload alongside variable renewables. Next-generation reactor investment.

**Transition Fuels — Maintained During Transition, Phased by Milestone:**
- British oil and gas: maintained for domestic energy security during transition
- Coal: limited production maintained for industrial uses (coking coal for steel — no green alternative currently exists at commercial scale)
- No new long-term fossil fuel infrastructure
- Phase-out triggered by demonstrated green capacity meeting relevant demand — NOT by calendar date

**Phase-Out Milestone System:**

Each fossil fuel use has a defined phase-out trigger — achievement thresholds, not deadlines:
- Coal power generation: phases out when green baseload can demonstrably cover peak demand for 30 consecutive days
- Gas for domestic heating: phases out sector by sector as heat pump penetration and grid capacity demonstrate readiness
- Coking coal for steel: phases out when green steel production is demonstrated to be commercially viable at scale in Britain

Milestones: public, independently monitored, reported to Parliament annually.

**Worker Transition — Explicit Commitment:** Workers in oil, gas, and coal not abandoned when industries phase down. Retraining funded and practical. Green energy jobs prioritised for former fossil fuel workers. Regional investment in fossil-fuel-dependent communities. No community left behind by the green transition. Policy.

**Smart grid:** Excess household power bought back fairly. **District heating:** New cities and major urban areas.

## 3.4 RURAL CONNECTIVITY — NO COMMUNITY LEFT BEHIND

*Currently almost nobody in British politics addresses this. Britain Forward does.*

**The Problem:**

Significant parts of rural Britain are disconnected from infrastructure urban areas take for granted:
- Off-grid homes on heating oil — no gas network connection; no viable heat pump pathway until insulation is upgraded
- Areas without viable public transport — complete car dependency; maximum fuel price vulnerability
- Communities reliant on coal for heating — no alternative provided
- Poor or absent broadband — excluded from the digital economy
- Poor road connectivity — long journey times to employment, healthcare, services

These communities cannot decarbonise at the same pace as well-connected urban areas. Penalising them for this — through carbon levies, fossil fuel phase-outs, or fuel duty increases without viable alternatives — is unjust and counterproductive.

They need connection, not condemnation.

**The Rural Connectivity Fund:**

*Energy connection:*
- Grid extension to off-grid communities — capital costs funded nationally, not charged to local communities
- Community energy schemes — shared heat networks, community solar, community wind — funded where individual installation is not viable
- Insulation programme targeting off-grid rural homes — funded to the standard required for heat pump viability before any heat pump transition is required of them
- Heating oil phase-out: communities given sufficient notice, practical funded alternatives, and extended phase-out timelines where no viable alternative yet exists

*Transport connection:*
- Rural bus and community transport networks restored and funded — minimum service standards for all inhabited communities
- Demand-responsive transport for genuinely sparse areas — bookable community vehicles where fixed routes are not viable
- Branch line restoration assessed — communities isolated since the Beeching cuts reviewed for reconnection
- EV charging in rural areas: installed as public infrastructure where commercial installation is not viable

*Digital connection:*
- Gigabit broadband: universal, including all rural areas — no postcode lottery; publicly funded where commercially unviable
- Satellite broadband as interim for the most remote properties while fixed-line is built

*Healthcare and services:*
- Mobile healthcare services for communities below viable fixed-service threshold
- Telemedicine investment — specialist appointments without prohibitive travel
- Rural pharmacies and post offices: supported and protected

**The Principle:** The green transition and digital transition happen for everyone — or they happen inequitably and incompletely. Rural communities are not a niche concern. They are a substantial portion of Britain's population and a defining test of whether "Forward" means forward for everyone.

## 3.9 INTERNATIONAL GREEN PRESSURE

Britain going green while major economies continue high-carbon production achieves limited global impact and competitively disadvantages British industry. Britain uses its trade relationships and diplomatic weight to ensure others genuinely transition too.

**Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM):**

Imports from countries not meeting agreed minimum carbon standards face a border levy proportionate to the estimated carbon cost of production not priced in the import.

- Applies where British producers face significant carbon costs: energy, steel, cement, chemicals, manufacturing
- Rate: estimated carbon intensity of production in exporting country, minus carbon pricing already paid there
- Revenue: invested in British green industrial transition and international green development
- WTO compliant — explicitly designed within WTO framework; the EU has already implemented this

**Effect:** British green producers not competitively disadvantaged. Foreign producers face financial incentive to decarbonise. Countries with genuine carbon pricing receive credit.

**Trade Agreements:** All new bilateral agreements include binding green standards — not aspirational language; binding, with enforcement mechanisms.

**International Carbon Accountability Report:** Published annually — tracking major economies' commitments against actual performance. Named publicly. Reported to Parliament.

**No Backing Out:** Countries announcing green commitments and quietly reversing them face public naming, CBAM at full unpriced carbon rate, and reduced priority for British international partnership investment. Agreements mean something. Britain acts like they do.

## 5.2 THE EDUCATION-TO-EMPLOYMENT PATHWAY

*Closing the cliff-edge between qualification and relevant work.*

People train for years and face a cliff-edge: no experience, no network, no clear route in. Archaeology graduates become baristas. Engineering graduates can't get their first interview. Teachers qualify and have no school connection.

**Formal Education-Employment Liaisons:**

Every school, college, university, and apprenticeship programme establishes formal liaison relationships with relevant employers and institutions in their primary subject areas. Not tokenistic careers fairs — structured pathways.

| Training | Liaison Partners | Pathway |
|---|---|---|
| Teacher training | Partner schools from year one | Priority consideration for permanent positions on qualification |
| Nursing/medicine | NHS trusts throughout training | Direct employment pipelines |
| Engineering | Industry partners co-designing curriculum | Guaranteed interview pathways |
| Archaeology/history | Museums, English Heritage, local authorities, commercial firms | Internships during study; direct consideration on graduation |
| Law | Firms, chambers, legal aid bodies, councils | Structured training contract and pupillage pipelines |
| Creative industries | Theatres, studios, galleries, publishers, production companies | Placements during study; structured showcasing |
| Social work | Local authority social services, charities | Placement-to-hire pipelines |

**Requirements:**

## 6.2 IMMIGRATION — POINTS-BASED, SKILLS-FIRST

**The Principle:** Britain is open to people willing and able to contribute. Britain manages immigration in the interest of existing residents and newcomers alike. Britain's public services, housing, and infrastructure have finite capacity.

**Points-Based Assessment — All Economic Immigration:**

| Factor | Points Available |
|---|---|
| Skills and qualifications (shortage occupations) | Up to 30 |
| English language proficiency (tested) | Up to 20 |
| Age (working-age weighted) | Up to 15 |
| British employer sponsorship (confirmed job offer) | Up to 25 |
| Regional sponsorship (willing to work outside major cities) | Up to 10 |

Minimum threshold to qualify for economic immigration: 65 points.

**Visa Categories:**

*Skilled Worker:* Points threshold met; employer sponsorship; occupation on shortage list or salary threshold met.

*High-Value Investor/Entrepreneur:* Significant capital investment or genuine business creation; genuinely high threshold.

*Family Reunion:* Limited to spouse/partner and dependent children of settled residents.

*Humanitarian Protection:* Separate from economic migration. Asylum seekers processed fairly, promptly, and humanely — needs-based assessment.

*Temporary Sector Visas — The Honest Complexity:*
Some sectors genuinely require workers Britain cannot currently supply domestically:
- Seasonal agriculture: capped, time-limited; workers must leave at visa expiry; no route to settlement
- Social care: transitional skilled worker visa while Britain invests in domestic training and pay; phases down as domestic workforce grows

These are deliberate, time-limited exceptions — not permanent backdoors. Each reviewed annually.

**Integration Requirements:** English language test; civic knowledge assessment before settlement; employment or genuine family dependency; no recourse to public funds during qualifying period.

**Enforcement:** Visa overstaying: serious enforcement priority; employer sanctions for employing overstayers; civil penalty then removal. Foreign nationals committing serious crimes: deported on completion of sentence.

**UBI and Voting:** After 5 years settled residency (2 years for high-skilled visa tier).

## 6.3 FOREIGN AID — REFORM, NOT SENTIMENT

Britain's foreign aid budget is approximately £15 billion per year. A significant documented portion is: absorbed by administrative costs; captured by corrupt intermediaries; spent on projects with no measurable outcomes; or directed in ways serving donor country interests rather than recipient country needs.

**Britain Forward commits:**

*Independent audit — day one:* Every foreign aid programme reviewed. What is it funding? What are measurable outcomes? Who receives the money? Where does it actually go?

*Three outcome categories from the audit:*

1. **Retain and expand:** Clear, measurable outcomes; genuine benefit to recipients — vaccines, emergency disaster relief, specific infrastructure with demonstrated impact. Continued or increased funding.

2. **Reform:** Genuine potential, poor implementation — reformed under results-based model with measurable outcomes required before continued funding.

3. **Cut:** Cannot demonstrate recipient benefit; money demonstrably captured by corruption or administration; serves donor interests rather than recipient needs. Cut. Money redirected to British public services and the results-based partnership model.

*Total foreign aid budget:* May decrease substantially after audit. Britain is not obligated to fund corruption. We are obligated to help where help is effective. After the audit, we will do the latter and stop doing the former.

**Results-Based International Investment — The Replacement:**

Trade, science, infrastructure partnerships with measurable outcomes. Britain as a hub of excellence: medicine, food technology, clean energy, engineering, AI. Partnerships of equals — not post-imperial paternalism.

**The EU Question:** Explicit neutrality — not re-litigating Brexit in either direction. Constructive partnership on science, research, and trade where agreements serve Britain. Any formal relationship change: public referendum.

# FISCAL FRAMEWORK — THE HONEST VERSION

*(Full detail: sub-document — Money)*

**Revenue spending** (NHS, UBI, welfare, education, public services): funded from tax revenues and nationalised asset revenues.

**Capital investment** (roads, rail, cities, water, energy): financed through long-term bonds, repaid by the assets' own revenues. Investments, not spending.

*These are not the same thing. Treating infrastructure investment as current spending is how governments make bad decisions.*

**How we pay for it:**
1. Nationalised asset revenues — profits flowing abroad stay in Britain
2. Pharmaceutical savings — stop overpaying for drugs
3. Stopped mismanagement — procurement reform, benefits administration replaced by UBI efficiency
4. Road pricing — replaces fuel duty fairly
5. UBI tax recoupture — higher earners return UBI through income tax
6. Foreign aid reform — ineffective programmes redirected
7. Long-term economic growth — from investment in people and infrastructure
8. Long-term bond financing — for capital investment, self-funded by asset revenues

Full independent fiscal assessment before any general election. We show our working. Always.
