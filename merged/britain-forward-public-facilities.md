# BRITAIN FORWARD
## POLICY: PUBLIC FACILITIES — THE DIGNITY INFRASTRUCTURE

> *"A country that works properly provides the basics. This is as basic as it gets."*

---

## THE CORE ARGUMENT

Britain has spent twenty years quietly closing public toilets.

Councils cut them to save money. High streets lost them when shops closed. Motorway services became the only option on major routes — and charge for the privilege. Laybys exist in their thousands with almost no facilities whatsoever.

HGV drivers are *legally required* to take rest breaks. The infrastructure to support those breaks barely exists. Truckers — the people who stock Britain's shelves, deliver Britain's goods, and keep Britain's economy moving — are expected to manage on goodwill, bushes, and the occasional McDonald's.

This is not a minor inconvenience. It is a systemic failure that affects:

- **HGV and van drivers** — legally mandated breaks, nowhere adequate to take them
- **Elderly people** — reduced mobility and bladder control make absent facilities genuinely distressing
- **People with health conditions** — Crohn's disease, IBD, bladder conditions, diabetes; for these people, "just wait" isn't an option
- **Parents with young children** — anyone who has desperately hunted for a toddler toilet knows exactly what this costs
- **Disabled people** — Changing Places facilities (larger accessible toilets with hoists) are rarer still
- **Women** — queue times and safety concerns in absent or poorly maintained facilities disproportionately affect women
- **Anyone in a rural area** — where the nearest facility can be genuinely miles away

The closure of public toilets was framed as saving money. It shifted the cost onto individuals — in discomfort, in health consequences, in dignity. That is not saving money. That is moving the cost to people who can least afford to carry it.

Britain Forward will fix this.

## POLICY FRAMEWORK

### 1. ROADSIDE & LAYBY FACILITIES

**The Problem**

Britain has thousands of laybys. The vast majority have no facilities whatsoever — not a toilet, not running water, nothing. Truckers on mandatory rest breaks have nowhere to go. Long-distance drivers plan routes around service stations rather than geography. Rural roads have nothing for miles.

**The Policy**

- **All laybys on A-roads and above** to be assessed for facility provision
- **Priority laybys** (high usage, long stretches without services) upgraded with:
  - Toilet facilities — maintained, lit, free to use
  - Basic washing facilities
  - Waste disposal
- **New layby facilities** built to a minimum standard — not luxury, but functional and clean
- **Maintained on a statutory basis** — not left to decay once built
- **EV charging integrated** where feasible (connects to road infrastructure policy)

**For HGV Drivers Specifically**

- Designated HGV rest areas upgraded substantially
- Facilities scaled to actual usage — not token provision
- Shower facilities at major HGV rest stops
- Safe, lit, secure environments — driver safety is part of this
- Working in partnership with the logistics industry on location planning

---

### 2. MOTORWAYS & MAJOR ROUTES

**The Problem**

Motorway services are privately operated and can charge what they like — including for toilets. Drivers who need facilities must either pay or divert. The monopoly position of motorway services creates a captive market with no alternative.

**The Policy**

- **Toilets at motorway services: free at point of use** — this is non-negotiable for any operator holding a service area licence
- **Minimum service intervals** — no stretch of motorway or major A-road more than a defined maximum distance without accessible facilities
- **Petrol stations on public roads** receiving any public contract or operating on public land must provide free toilet access
- **Standards enforced** — clean, maintained, accessible, available during opening hours minimum; 24-hour where traffic warrants it

---

### 3. TOWNS, CITIES & HIGH STREETS

**The Problem**

Local councils have closed public toilets across Britain for two decades, cutting them as an easy budget saving. Town centres now depend on the goodwill of shops and cafés — a system that works until you're not a paying customer, it's late, or the café is closed.

**The Policy**

- **Statutory duty on local authorities** to provide a minimum level of public toilet provision relative to population and footfall — this cannot be cut below a defined floor
- **Ring-fenced funding** from central government for public toilet provision — removing the excuse that councils can't afford it
- **Town centre facilities** upgraded and maintained to a consistent standard
- **Community Toilet Scheme** nationally standardised — businesses that provide public toilet access receive a payment from the local authority; expands provision without building new facilities everywhere
- **Free at point of use** — the 30p turnstile is abolished
- **Opening hours** extended where demand exists — evening economy means evening need

---

### 4. PARKS, OPEN SPACES & RURAL AREAS

**The Problem**

Public parks — often the primary outdoor space for families, elderly people, and those without gardens — frequently have no facilities, or facilities that are locked, broken, or unusable.

**The Policy**

- **All maintained public parks** above a minimum size: toilet facilities required, maintained, and open during park hours
- **Rural facilities** mapped and gaps identified — walking routes, beauty spots, and visitor destinations prioritised
- **Seasonal provision** where permanent facilities aren't viable — high-quality temporary facilities during peak periods rather than nothing

---

### 5. CHANGING PLACES — FOR THOSE WHO NEED MORE

**The Problem**

Standard accessible toilets don't work for everyone. People with complex disabilities — including those who need hoists, changing benches, and more space — require Changing Places facilities. These exist but are vanishingly rare.

**The Policy**

- **Changing Places facilities** required in all new large public buildings, transport hubs, shopping centres, and major public spaces
- **Retrofit programme** for existing key sites — prioritising transport, healthcare, and public venues
- **National map** kept current and publicly accessible — so people can plan with confidence rather than uncertainty
- **No exceptions** for new builds above a certain footprint — this is not optional provision, it is a requirement

---

### 6. STANDARDS & MAINTENANCE

A toilet that exists but is broken, filthy, or unsafe is not a toilet — it is an insult.

**The Policy**

- **National minimum standards** for public facilities — cleanliness, lighting, safety, accessibility
- **Inspection regime** with real consequences for persistent failures
- **Reporting mechanism** — simple public reporting of facility failures, with required response times
- **Design standards** — facilities designed to be maintainable, vandal-resistant, and safe, particularly for lone users at night

---

### 7. THE COST QUESTION

Opponents will say: "This costs money."

It does. Here is what it also costs when facilities don't exist:

- NHS treatment for UTIs caused by people holding on too long — a genuine, documented health consequence
- Lost economic activity when people avoid town centres, long journeys, or public spaces because they can't guarantee access to facilities
- Reduced workforce participation for people with health conditions who cannot work in environments without adequate provision
- The hidden cost of dignified care — when public facilities fail, the burden falls on families, carers, and individuals

The investment is modest. The return — in health, dignity, economic participation, and basic quality of life — is substantial.

## THE POLITICAL CASE

This is one of the easiest policies to defend and one of the hardest to attack.

Who opposes public toilets? Nobody. Who benefits? Everyone.

It is also a perfect expression of the Britain Forward principle: **the basics should work**. Not luxury. Not aspiration. The things a functioning country simply provides.

When a party demonstrates it notices the small things — the everyday indignities that politicians in Westminster never experience because they have staff, drivers, and private facilities — it signals something important:

**We live in the same country you do. And we're going to fix it properly.**
