# BRITAIN FORWARD
## PILLAR: NATIONAL INFRASTRUCTURE & URBAN TRANSFORMATION

> *"We're not patching what's broken. We're building what should have been built."*

---

## THE CORE ARGUMENT

Britain's infrastructure was built for a different era — Roman roads, Victorian sewers, post-war housing estates, 20th century cities designed around cars.

The choice isn't *repair or replace*. It's **spend small now and pay forever, or spend smart once and stop paying repeatedly**.

Britain Forward chooses to build properly. Once. Right.

---

## 1. ROADS — REBUILT FROM THE GROUND UP

### The Problem
UK roads are resurfaced constantly because cheap asphalt degrades rapidly under British weather and traffic. We pay again and again for the same roads. This is not economy — it's false economy.

### The Policy

**Materials Overhaul**
- Replace standard asphalt with long-life composite surfaces — reinforced, weather-resistant, permeable where appropriate to handle water runoff
- Whole-life costing replaces upfront-cost thinking in all road contracts
- Roads built once, properly, rather than patched indefinitely

**Road-Embedded EV Charging**
- Inductive charging coils embedded in key road surfaces during rebuild
- Vehicles charge passively while moving — removing range anxiety entirely
- Solves the commercial EV problem: a *laden* electric van currently barely manages 50 miles on a full charge. Road-embedded charging makes payload range irrelevant.
- Replaces fuel duty revenue as EVs kill pump receipts — usage-based road pricing integrated into the system

**Design for the Future**
- Road hierarchies redesigned for modern freight, cycling, and autonomous vehicles
- Laybys and rest stops upgraded with:
  - Public toilet facilities (mandatory)
  - EV charging points
  - Basic amenities
- No more charging for petrol station toilets on public roads — if you receive public road contracts, you provide public facilities

**Why It Pays for Itself**
- Stop resurfacing the same roads every 5 years
- Road charging replaces lost fuel duty
- Embedded charging infrastructure stimulates EV adoption, which reduces NHS costs from air pollution

## 2. ELECTRIC VEHICLES — SOLVE THE REAL PROBLEM

### The Problem
An empty electric van: ~200 miles. A full electric van: ~50 miles. This makes commercial EVs nearly useless for actual logistics work. The industry knows it. Nobody's fixing it at policy level.

### The Policy

**Road-embedded charging** (see above) is the primary fix — continuous charge while moving removes the payload penalty entirely.

**Beyond that:**
- National EV infrastructure fund for fleet operators transitioning from diesel
- Accelerated charge point rollout at industrial estates, depots, and logistics hubs
- R&D investment in solid-state batteries and rapid charging technology — Britain leads the science, not just the adoption
- Mandate EV-readiness in all new commercial and industrial builds

**For private drivers:**
- Rooftop solar (see below) + home charging = near-zero running costs for most users
- End the postcode lottery on charge point availability — rural areas included, not as afterthought

## 3. ENERGY — SOLAR ON ROOFS, NOT IN FIELDS

### The Problem
Large solar farms occupy agricultural land that could grow food. Meanwhile, millions of rooftops sit unused. Fields should feed people. Roofs should generate power.

### The Policy

**Mandatory Rooftop Solar**
- All new residential builds: solar panels standard, not optional
- All new commercial and industrial builds: solar mandatory
- Major retrofit programme for existing housing stock — prioritising social housing and low-income households first

**Protect Agricultural Land**
- No new planning permission for solar farms on Grade 1 or 2 agricultural land
- Existing farm solar reviewed and phased where alternatives exist
- Fields are for crops, livestock, rewilding, or development — not electricity generation when roofs are available

**Smart Grid Integration**
- National smart grid upgraded to handle distributed rooftop generation
- Excess household power bought back at fair rates — energy bills become energy *income* for many households
- Community energy cooperatives supported and funded

**Additional Energy Sources That Fit Britain**
- Tidal and wave energy — Britain has some of the best tidal resources in the world and is barely using them. This is a competitive national advantage being ignored.
- District heating networks in new cities and major urban areas — share heat efficiently rather than each building heating itself independently
- Nuclear (as per existing platform) provides baseload stability alongside renewables

## 4. CITIES — MULTI-LAYERED, HUMAN-SCALE

### The Problem
UK cities were largely designed around cars — which created congestion, poor air quality, fragmented communities, and urban spaces that are unpleasant to be in.

### The Vision: The Three-Layer City

**Layer 1 — Underground: Heavy Transit**
- Trains, metros, trams: underground or elevated, separated from surface
- Private vehicles: underground road network, separated from rail level
- Underground freight delivery networks for city centres — lorries never need to surface in residential or commercial areas

**Layer 2 — Surface: Human Scale**
- Walking and cycling only
- Green space, trees, clean air as standard — not luxury
- Outdoor dining, markets, community space
- Emergency vehicles only exception

**Layer 3 — Rooftops: Energy + Green**
- Solar panels standard
- Green roofs where solar isn't installed — insulation, biodiversity, rainfall management
- Rooftop growing and community gardens where feasible

**Result:**
- Congestion disappears from city surfaces
- Air quality transforms
- Cities become genuinely pleasant places to live and work
- Property values rise, which helps fund the investment

**Precedents:** Elements of this exist in Singapore, Copenhagen, Tokyo. Britain would integrate them more comprehensively than any existing city has.

## 5. NEW PLANNED CITIES — BUILD WHERE WE NEED THEM

### The Problem
The housing crisis isn't *only* about number of homes. It's that demand is concentrated in places already at capacity. Building more in London just adds pressure to broken infrastructure. Building somewhere new lets you design it right from the start.

### The Policy

**New Town Programme — 21st Century Version**
- Commission multiple new planned cities and large towns across Britain
- Located deliberately in areas of low economic activity — redistributing opportunity, not just population
- Designed from scratch with:
  - Multi-layer urban structure (see above)
  - Integrated public transport from day one
  - Mixed housing tenure (owned, social, cooperative)
  - Employment districts planned alongside residential
  - Green corridors and open space built in, not squeezed in later

**This solves three problems at once:**
1. Housing supply — at genuine scale
2. Congestion — new cities designed to avoid it
3. Regional inequality — economic gravity moves north and outward

**Precedents:** Milton Keynes. Stevenage. South Korea's Sejong City. Australia's Canberra. The idea works when executed with proper investment and planning.

## 6. REGIONAL EQUALITY — NO MORE LONDON PRIVILEGE

### The Problem
London's infrastructure spending per head has historically far exceeded every other region. Cultural institutions, government, media, financial infrastructure — all gravitates to London by default. This is not natural. It is the result of accumulated decisions that can be reversed.

### The Policy

**Equalised Infrastructure Spending**
- Per capita infrastructure investment legally equalised across all regions
- Written into statute — not a promise, a requirement
- Independent body monitors and enforces compliance

**Government Decentralisation**
- Government departments physically relocated out of London
- Civil service jobs distributed to regional cities
- Policy decisions affecting regions made closer to those regions

**Regional Economic Hubs**
- Deliberate investment in Manchester, Birmingham, Leeds, Bristol, Cardiff, Edinburgh as genuine economic centres — not satellites of London
- Regional financial and legal infrastructure supported
- Broadcasting and cultural funding decentralised

**Transport Connectivity**
- The north gets the rail investment it was promised and denied
- Regional airports supported where they serve genuine connectivity needs
- Road quality equalised — not just motorways, but regional A-roads and rural routes

## 7. THE CAPITAL — REDISTRIBUTE POWER GEOGRAPHICALLY

### The Proposal
Split the capital function, as several successful nations already do.

**Model:**
- **Parliamentary & Government Capital — Birmingham or Manchester**
  - Birmingham is the geographic centre of England
  - Manchester has the economic gravity of the North
  - Either sends an unambiguous signal that Britain has actually changed
  - Parliament's upcoming renovation is the perfect moment — rebuild it elsewhere

- **Financial Capital — London**
  - The City isn't moving and shouldn't. It stays.

- **Judicial Capital — to be determined independently**

**Precedents:**
Germany (Berlin/Frankfurt), Australia (Canberra), South Africa (three capitals), Netherlands (Amsterdam/The Hague).

**Political reality:** Everywhere outside London would support this. London-based media will oppose it loudly — which is itself evidence the policy is working.

## 8. WATER INFRASTRUCTURE

*(Often forgotten — shouldn't be)*

Britain loses an estimated 3 billion litres of water per day through leaking pipes. That is not a maintenance problem. That is a systemic failure.

**Policy:**
- National water infrastructure audit and upgrade programme
- Water companies brought under public oversight with binding leakage targets
- New cities built with modern water infrastructure standard — not Victorian-era pipes with modern buildings on top
- Drought resilience built into new infrastructure planning

---

## 9. DIGITAL INFRASTRUCTURE — THE INVISIBLE ROAD

Broadband is infrastructure. It is as essential to modern economic participation as roads and electricity. Treating it as a commercial product produces the same result as treating roads as commercial products — coverage gaps, rural exclusion, and poor quality where profit margins are thin.

**Policy:**
- National gigabit broadband as public infrastructure — not a market product
- Universal coverage including rural areas, treated as a right not a luxury
- Public ownership of the core digital backbone (already in existing platform)
- Cybersecurity infrastructure treated as national defence (already in existing platform)

## 10. REWILDING ALONGSIDE INFRASTRUCTURE

Infrastructure and nature are not opposites.

**Policy:**
- Green corridors built alongside new roads and rail — wildlife routes integrated into engineering design
- New cities designed with biodiversity built in — not concrete with a few trees, but genuine ecological planning
- Agricultural land freed from solar farms partially rewilded
- Urban waterways restored and made accessible

## IMPLEMENTATION TIMELINE

**Short Term (Year 1–2)**
- Roads materials and design standards overhauled — all new contracts under new spec
- Rooftop solar mandated on all new builds
- Public toilet requirements introduced for rest stops and contracted petrol stations
- Agricultural land solar protection enacted
- Regional spending equalisation legislation introduced

**Medium Term (Year 2–5)**
- Road-embedded charging piloted on key freight corridors
- New city sites selected, planned, and begun
- Government department relocations phased
- Water infrastructure audit completed; upgrade programme begins
- National broadband programme launched

**Long Term (Year 5+)**
- Multi-layer city design standard for new developments
- Capital functions relocated
- Road network substantially rebuilt to new standard
- Energy independence achieved through combined rooftop solar, tidal, nuclear, and wind
- New cities receiving first residents
