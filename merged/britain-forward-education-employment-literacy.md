# BRITAIN FORWARD
## POLICY: EDUCATION, EMPLOYMENT & DEMOCRATIC LITERACY

> *"A country where people are equipped to think, to work, and to participate — not just to comply."*

---

## THE CORE ARGUMENT

Britain has spent decades teaching people what to think, not how to think.

Schools prepare students for exams, not for life. Employers select for interview performance, not job performance. Government spends public money without meaningfully explaining where it goes. And the systems that are supposed to support people into work frequently fail the people who need them most.

The result is a population that is often underprepared for adult life, underserved by employers, and disconnected from the democratic decisions made in their name.

Britain Forward will fix all three — education, employment, and democratic participation — as a connected system, because they are one.

---

# PART ONE: EDUCATION

## 1. THE CURRICULUM — WHAT SCHOOLS SHOULD ACTUALLY TEACH

### Academic Foundation (Existing — Maintained and Strengthened)

Core academic subjects remain. The reform is not to remove academic rigour but to add what is currently missing.

### Life Skills — Mandatory Across All Schools

**Critical Thinking & Research**
- How to find reliable information
- How to evaluate sources — distinguishing journalism, opinion, advertising, and evidence
- How to identify bias — including in sources you agree with
- How to construct and challenge an argument
- Media literacy — understanding how news, social media, and advertising work

This is not political education. It is cognitive infrastructure. A democracy functions better when its citizens can think clearly. This subject alone would do more for political health than almost any other reform.

**Financial & Civic Literacy**
- How wages, taxes, National Insurance, and pensions work
- Where tax money goes — local and national
- What UBI is and how it might work
- How to read a payslip, a tenancy agreement, a contract
- How Parliament works, how to vote, how local councils function
- How to find and use public services
- Rights at work — what employers can and cannot do

**Employment Preparation**
- How to write an effective CV
- How to write a cover letter
- How to research a prospective employer
- How to prepare for an interview — including alternative formats
- How to negotiate (professionally and appropriately)
- What probationary periods mean and how they work
- How to handle workplace conflict

**Personal & Practical Life Skills**
- Basic budgeting and personal finance
- Renting and tenancy rights
- Understanding utility bills and household costs
- Basic cooking and nutrition
- Basic first aid
- Mental health literacy — recognising and talking about mental health

## 2. VOCATIONAL PATHWAYS — EQUAL, NOT LESSER

The current system treats vocational education as the consolation prize for those who couldn't manage academic routes. This is wrong in principle and damaging in practice.

**Policy:**
- Vocational and technical qualifications given equal standing with academic qualifications in university and employer entry requirements where relevant
- Apprenticeship expansion — properly funded, properly supervised, genuinely educational
- Vocational subjects introduced earlier — not as alternatives to academic subjects but alongside them
- Industry partnerships with schools — real working relationships, not tokenistic careers fairs
- No stigma in school culture around vocational routes — this requires active cultural change, not just policy

---

## 3. SPECIAL EDUCATIONAL NEEDS & NEURODIVERGENCE

The system currently fails neurodivergent students at every level — late identification, inadequate support, and assessment methods that measure compliance more than capability.

**Policy:**
- Earlier and more thorough neurodivergence identification — treated as support trigger, not label
- Teaching methods diversified — not every student learns the same way
- Assessment methods diversified — written exams are one tool, not the only tool
- Teacher training in neurodivergent learning — mandatory, not optional CPD
- EHCP (Education, Health and Care Plan) process reformed — currently too slow, too adversarial, too dependent on parental capacity to fight the system

---

## 4. ARTS, CREATIVITY & EXPRESSION

The arts are not extras. They are how humans process experience, build community, and develop empathy. Cutting arts in schools to focus on STEM has produced a generation with fewer outlets and no evidence of better STEM outcomes.

**Policy:**
- Arts, music, drama, and creative subjects protected in school curricula — ringfenced, not optional
- After-school arts programmes funded — theatre, choir, dance, visual arts, music
- **Graffiti and Street Art Centres** — dedicated legal spaces where young people can learn spray art, mural design, and urban art forms; evidence from Bristol and elsewhere shows legal walls reduce tagging substantially
- Youth arts as an explicit crime prevention and mental health strategy — not a nice-to-have
- Community arts funding restored and expanded

---

## 5. FREE SCHOOL MEALS — UNIVERSAL

The evidence is unambiguous. Universal free school meals improve attendance, concentration, academic outcomes, and equality. They also remove the stigma that targeted provision creates.

**Policy:**
- Free school meals for all primary school children — universal, no means-testing
- Expansion to secondary — phased, starting with Years 7-9
- Nutritional standards enforced — not just food provided but food that actually nourishes
- Holiday hunger programmes funded — the problem doesn't stop in July

---

## 6. LIBRARIES — PROTECTED AND EXPANDED

Libraries are not relics. They are:
- Warm, safe spaces for people without adequate home environments
- Internet access for those who have none
- Early literacy for children whose homes have few books
- Community hubs, particularly for elderly and isolated people
- Free access to knowledge for everyone, regardless of income

**Policy:**
- Statutory protection — councils cannot close libraries below a minimum provision floor
- Ring-fenced funding — not subject to general budget cuts
- Extended opening hours where demand exists
- Digital services expanded alongside physical — not instead of
- Libraries as distribution points for transparent government spending information (see Part Three)

# PART TWO: EMPLOYMENT

## 1. INTERVIEW REFORM — SELECT FOR THE JOB, NOT THE INTERVIEW

### The Problem

The current interview system selects for interview performance. These are not the same thing as job performance. The result is:

- Talented people who struggle in formal interview settings — neurodivergent, shy, early-career, or simply not trained in interview performance — lose out
- People who are skilled at performing in interviews but less capable in the actual role succeed
- Employers wonder why their new hires aren't as good as they seemed
- Enormous potential goes to waste

### Advertised Salaries — Mandatory

**The salary question is banned because salary must already be stated.**

- All job advertisements must include the actual salary or salary range — not "competitive" or "DOE"
- This is already required in several countries; the arguments against it do not survive scrutiny
- Eliminates a negotiation that disproportionately disadvantages women, neurodivergent people, those from lower-income backgrounds, and anyone less comfortable with financial confrontation
- Saves candidates time and employers time — only people who find the salary acceptable will apply

### Banned Questions

Questions with no evidential relationship to job performance are prohibited in formal hiring processes:

- Hypothetical personality questions ("if you were an animal...")
- Questions about personal life, family plans, or relationship status
- Salary history questions (you must not penalise people for what previous employers paid them)
- Any question whose answer could be used to discriminate unlawfully — currently prohibited but widely used

### Alternative Assessment Formats

**Employers encouraged and supported to use:**
- Work trials and paid task assessments
- Portfolio and project reviews
- Written application stages replacing early interviews
- Group exercises with clear, fair evaluation criteria
- Job-specific skills tests

**Public sector leads by example** — government hiring reformed first, providing a model for private sector.

### Neurodivergent and Accessibility Adaptations

- Reasonable adjustments in hiring processes strengthened and enforced — currently required in law but rarely implemented
- Interview questions provided in advance as standard option — this helps neurodivergent candidates and produces better, more considered answers from everyone
- Alternative communication formats available — written responses, remote options
- Employers provided with clear guidance and support for implementation — this is not just compliance, it is access to a wider talent pool

### Early Career Pathways

- Entry-level roles must genuinely be entry-level — not requiring 2 years experience for a starter position
- Apprenticeships and traineeships treated as genuine career entry, not cheap labour
- Graduate scheme reform — diversity of background prioritised, not just university prestige

## 2. EMPLOYMENT SUPPORT — ACTUALLY WORKING

The current system focuses on compliance — are you looking for work? — rather than outcomes — are you finding work that suits you?

**Policy:**
- Skills-matching services — proper assessment of what people are good at, what they want, and where they fit
- Retraining funded properly — not just signposted to courses, but genuinely supported through them
- Employer incentives for long-term hiring from unemployment — not just short-term subsidies
- Supported employment for people with disabilities and health conditions — tailored, not generic
- No cliff edges — transitions into work should not cause sudden loss of support before the job is secure

---

## 3. WORKPLACE RIGHTS & STANDARDS

- 4-day working week: phased transition where viable — piloted in public sector first, with evidence base built for wider rollout
- Equal pay for equal work regardless of age — age-based minimum wages abolished; if the job is worth the wage, it is worth it regardless of who does it
- Zero-hours contract reform — guaranteed minimum hours for anyone employed regularly
- Right to disconnect — employers cannot routinely require availability outside contracted hours
- Corporate pay ratio limits — CEO pay cannot exceed a defined multiple of the lowest-paid employee in the same organisation

# PART THREE: DEMOCRATIC LITERACY & TRANSPARENCY

## 1. TRANSPARENT TAX SPENDING — PUBLIC AND ACCESSIBLE

**The Policy:**

Every taxpayer in Britain contributes to public spending. Almost none of them know in any detail where that money goes.

- **Annual Transparent Spending Breakdown** published by both national government and local councils
- Written in plain English — not bureaucratic language
- Broken down by category with actual proportions — not just totals
- Available in every library, GP surgery, post office, and job centre
- Available online in accessible, searchable format
- Independently verified — not just government self-reporting

**What this achieves:**
- Public trust increases when spending is visible and explained
- Public pressure increases when spending is wasteful — legitimately and informedly
- The democratic conversation becomes grounded in fact rather than assumption
- People understand what their taxes fund — NHS, roads, schools, defence — and in what proportions

This costs almost nothing to implement and changes the entire quality of democratic debate.

---

## 2. CIVIC EDUCATION — HOW BRITAIN ACTUALLY WORKS

Currently taught barely at all. This is not an accident — an uninformed electorate is easier to mislead.

**Mandatory curriculum content:**
- How Parliament works — bills, debates, votes, committees
- How to register to vote and why it matters
- How local councils work and what they control
- How to contact your MP and what they can do
- How public consultations work and how to participate
- What the courts do and how the justice system functions
- What the NHS is, how it's funded, and how to use it effectively
- How to make a complaint — to any public body — effectively

---

## 3. CRITICAL THINKING AS DEMOCRATIC INFRASTRUCTURE

A population that can identify bias, evaluate evidence, and resist manipulation is a healthier democracy.

This connects directly to:
- Media literacy — how to read a newspaper, understand editorial bias, identify sponsored content
- Online literacy — how algorithms work, what filter bubbles are, how misinformation spreads
- Political literacy — how to evaluate a policy claim, what evidence looks like, what a logical fallacy is

This is taught in schools (see Part One) but reinforced through:
- Public library programmes
- Community education initiatives
- Adult learning courses

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| People don't know how to find work | Life skills curriculum + employment support |
| Talented people fail interviews | Interview reform + alternative formats |

| People don't know where taxes go | Transparent spending breakdown |

## THE UNDERLYING PRINCIPLE

A functioning democracy requires citizens who are:
- **Capable** — equipped with real skills for real life
- **Informed** — able to access and evaluate information
- **Participating** — connected to the decisions made in their name

None of this happens automatically. It requires deliberate investment in people — not as economic units, but as the actual substance of the country.
